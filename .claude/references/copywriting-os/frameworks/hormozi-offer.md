# Hormozi Grand Slam Offer (applied to copywriting)

## What this is

Hormozi's $100M Offers framework adapted for copywriting service offers (and for any client-side service offer the writer is positioning in copy). The market doesn't pay for better writing — it pays for better offers. Mark uses this framework to push project values from $2,500 to $15,000+ on identical deliverables. The value equation governs every buying decision; the four levers (Dream Outcome, Likelihood, Time Delay, Effort) determine whether a prospect feels stupid saying no or stupid saying yes.

## Inputs / prerequisites

Before this framework can be applied:
- Current offer description (deliverable + price + timeline)
- Buyer-profile.md or equivalent — who exactly is the avatar, what do they actually dream about
- Real client results with specific numbers (not "many clients saw a lift")
- Honest inventory of what client effort/time the current process demands
- A list of bonuses you can actually deliver without bleeding margin

## The framework / process

### The Value Equation (governs every buying decision)

```
Value = (Dream Outcome × Perceived Likelihood of Achievement)
        ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────
                  (Time Delay × Effort & Sacrifice)
```

Top half drives value up. Bottom half destroys it. Most copywriters obsess over making bigger promises (top half) while ignoring the value killers below the line.

**Hormozi's anchor example:** Ozempic costs $15,000/year. Gym membership costs $30/month. Identical dream outcome (weight loss). 500x price difference comes from minimizing time delay (weeks vs months) and effort (one weekly injection vs daily workouts) while maximizing likelihood (90% vs 10%).

For copywriters: "better copy" is vague. "Increase cart conversion from 2.3% to 3.5%" is a dream outcome worth paying for.

### Lever 1 — Dream Outcome (specific to their situation)

Stop pitching the wrong dream. Clients don't dream about copy. They dream about:
- Adding $100K in monthly recurring revenue
- Watching their product sell out on launch day
- Getting 20% of their email list to buy their new offer
- Hitting $5M this year instead of stuck at $2M again

Mark: when he stopped promising "sales page copy" and started promising "add $50K in monthly revenue within 90 days," average project value jumped from $2,500 to $15,000.

### Lever 2 — Perceived Likelihood (proof mechanisms)

A big promise without proof creates skepticism, not excitement. Stack:

- **Testimonials with specificity** — not "increased conversions" but "Sarah's email sequence generated $340,000 in 6 days. Here's the exact sequence I'll adapt for you."
- **Demonstrable competence** — Mark's cold outreach: *"Your headline isn't clear within the first 3 seconds. Here's a rewrite: [headline]. This fix alone typically lifts conversions by 10-30% based on my client results."*
- **Relevant track record** — match proof to their situation. E-commerce clients don't care about your SaaS wins.
- **Proprietary methodology** — "Revenue Acceleration Framework" sounds more certain than "I'll write some copy and see what works."

### Lever 3 — Time Delay (cutting time multiplies value)

Cutting time in half doesn't double value — it can 10x it. Mark's compressions:

- **Systematic process** — what used to take 6 weeks of back-and-forth now takes 10 days:
  - Day 1-2: Customer research calls (pre-scheduled)
  - Day 3-4: Competitor analysis (templates)
  - Day 5-7: First draft
  - Day 8-9: Client review + live revisions on Zoom
  - Day 10: Final delivery
- **Templatized research** — what took 2 weeks now takes hours via AI agentic workflows
- **Pre-written frameworks** — codified after 500+ sales pages
- **Milestone delivery** — even when total time can't compress, perceived time can: headlines by day 3, opening section by day 7, complete first draft by day 14, polished final by day 21

### Lever 4 — Effort & Sacrifice (real leverage)

Every unit of client effort removed multiplies value exponentially.

**Traditional approach (6+ weeks of overhead):**
- 2-hour brief meeting
- 1-2 hour research questionnaire
- Ongoing clarifying emails
- 2-3 hours reviewing each draft
- 2-4 revision cycles, 3-5 day waits between
- Stakeholder approval chasing
- Final review meetings

**Mark's engineered approach (2 hours total client time):**
- One 30-minute kickoff call
- He handles all research via interviews and data mining
- One 90-minute live revision session

3x reduction in client effort justifies a 5x price increase. Copy quality is identical.

### The Stacked Bonus Structure

Single offer is less valuable than the same offer broken into component parts and stacked. Mark's $15,000 sales page stack:

| Component | Stated value |
|-----------|---|
| Core: Complete sales page copy | $15,000 |
| Bonus 1: Sales Page Testing Roadmap | $2,500 |
| Bonus 2: Implementation Guide (dev checklist) | $1,500 |
| Bonus 3: 5 Alternative Headline Split Test | $1,000 |
| Bonus 4: Objection-Handling Script Library | $1,500 |
| Bonus 5: 30-Day Performance Review | $2,000 |
| Fast-Action Bonus: First-Week Launch Support (48hr decision) | $1,000 |
| **Total stated value** | **$24,500** |
| **Investment** | **$15,000** |
| **Stated savings** | **$9,500** |

Same deliverables he always provided. Itemizing and stacking pushed close rate from 35% to 48%.

### The Triple Guarantee Structure

Weak guarantees scream insecurity. Strong guarantees demonstrate confidence. Stack three:

1. **Quality Assurance** — *"If you implement the copy exactly as delivered and aren't satisfied with the quality, I'll revise until you are."* Addresses quality without opening to bad-faith refunds.
2. **Performance** — *"Beat your control by 25% within 90 days or I'll rewrite the underperforming sections."* Confidence + requires proper implementation.
3. **Strategic Partnership** — *"I'll provide optimization recommendations until you hit your target conversion rate."* Positions invested partner.

Important conditional: *"Assuming you maintain your typical traffic levels and implement the copy as delivered."* Protects you while encouraging client success. In five years, Mark had two refund requests — both at 20% lift instead of 25%, both became long-term clients after revision.

### Authentic Scarcity & Urgency (real, not fake)

Fake scarcity destroys trust. Real constraints create action. Hormozi distinguishes:
- **Scarcity** = limited quantity
- **Urgency** = limited time

Mark's authentic constraints:
- **Capacity scarcity** — exactly 4 clients per quarter (verifiable)
- **Group start urgency** — all Q1 clients start January 5th. Miss it, wait for Q2.
- **Bonus urgency** — fast-action bonuses literally removed after 48 hours
- **Price urgency** — $2,000 early-start discount for projects starting before [date]

### The MAGIC Naming Formula

Generic names guarantee generic prices. Specific names allow premium pricing by pre-qualifying buyers.

- **M**agnet — The attractive outcome
- **A**vatar — Specific target audience
- **G**oal — Promised transformation
- **I**nterval — Time component
- **C**ontainer — System / method / blueprint

Examples:
- Generic: *"Sales Page Copywriting"* → *"The 90-Day Revenue Acceleration System for B2B SaaS Founders"*
- Generic: *"Email Marketing Services"* → *"The 6-Week Cart Abandonment Recovery Blueprint for E-commerce Brands"*

## Outputs

A complete Grand Slam offer document containing:
- MAGIC-formula offer name
- Specific dream outcome statement (with target metric)
- Likelihood-stack (specific testimonials, demonstrable competence asset, methodology name)
- Compressed timeline with milestone delivery markers
- Client-effort budget (target: under 2 hours)
- Stacked bonus list with stated values
- Triple guarantee with implementation conditions
- Authentic scarcity + urgency mechanism
- 2-minute pitch script

## Application rules / scoring

Pass criteria:
- Dream outcome is **specific to their business situation** (target metric + timeline) — not "better copy"
- Likelihood layer has at least 3 of 4 proof mechanisms
- Total client time across the engagement is itemized and under 3 hours
- Stated bonus value > offer price
- All three guarantees present
- Scarcity is verifiable (you can prove "2 spots left" if asked)
- Offer name passes MAGIC test (all 5 elements present)

Test threshold: send to 5 warm prospects. Once you hit 40% close rate, raise prices 20%.

## Common failures

1. **Big promise, weak likelihood.** Promising "$50K in 90 days" without specific testimonials or demonstrable competence reads as a scam, not a Grand Slam.
2. **Optimizing dream outcome only.** Most copywriters keep promising bigger numbers while ignoring the bottom half of the equation. Effort and time delay are where the leverage actually lives.
3. **Fake scarcity.** Stamping "only 2 spots left!" you can't verify destroys trust permanently. Constraints must be real and enforceable.
4. **Inflated bonus values nobody believes.** A $1,500 "Implementation Guide" that's a Notion link with three bullet points kills the whole stack. Each bonus must look and feel worth the stated value.

## When to use vs skip

**Use when:**
- A copywriter (or service-business client) has a generic offer competing on price
- Close rate is under 30% on warm leads
- Average project value is below market for the deliverable
- Writing a sales page or proposal where the offer itself needs reframing

**Skip when:**
- You don't yet have client results with specific numbers — go gather proof first
- The deliverable itself is broken (Hormozi amplifies effective offers; it can't fix bad copy quality)
- Audience is fully transactional commodity (e.g. $5 fiverr-tier work where the buyer specifically doesn't want a system)
