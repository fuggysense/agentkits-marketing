# 6 Emotional States (Skeptic → Buyer Sequence)

## Canonical definition

Every prospect moves through six emotional states before they buy. Skip one, the sequence breaks. Reverse two, you lose them. The newsletter actually documents 7 internal stages (Indifference is the starting condition, with Pain → Problem → Hope → Belief → Desire → Urgency as the 6 states the writer must engineer). Curiosity is not a state — it is the fuel that pulls the reader between states.

> "You can write the most compelling copy in the world and still lose the sale if the emotional sequence is wrong... Every stage builds on the one before it." — Mark Masters

> "The difference between 0.9% and 2.4% isn't talent. It's architecture." — Mark Masters

## The 6 elements (plus Indifference as the starting state)

### State 0: Indifference (starting condition)
Internal monologue: *"I don't care about this."*
Default state of all prospects. Distracted, scrolling, not engaged. Must be broken before anything else works.
- Triggers: pattern interrupts, unexpected statements, direct callouts ("You're a freelancer who..."), contrarian takes, specific numbers, self-reflection questions.
- Copy elements: headlines, hook statements, opening lines, ad copy, email subject lines.
- Transition out: hit an emotional nerve — not information, emotion.

### State 1: Pain Recognition
Internal monologue: *"Wait — that's ME."*
- Definition: emotional activation. They feel symptoms, don't yet understand the cause. The wound is open.
- Example trigger: "You know that feeling when..." constructions; vivid sensory descriptions of daily frustration.
- Example: "You stare at your laptop at 11 PM, stomach knotted, watching another day end with nothing to show for it."
- When to use: opening hooks, problem agitation, story openings that mirror the reader's experience.
- Transition: promise diagnosis. Create curiosity about the cause.

### State 2: Problem Awareness
Internal monologue: *"So THAT'S what's causing it."*
- Definition: the diagnosis / aha moment. They move from victim to informed.
- Example trigger: "The real reason is...", mechanism explanation, naming what they couldn't articulate.
- Example: "You're not making enough sales because you're competing on price in a race you can't win."
- When to use: problem-diagnosis sections, "here's what's actually happening," enemy-naming.
- Transition: show the problem has been solved before — open the door to possibility.

### State 3: Hope
Internal monologue: *"Maybe there's a solution."*
- Definition: belief that change is possible — generally, not yet about your specific solution.
- Example trigger: stories of others who solved this, "What if..." future pacing, permission statements ("It's not your fault — you were never taught this").
- Example: "Sarah was exactly where you are — same frustrations, same doubts, same 3 AM anxiety. Eighteen months later, she's [specific outcome]."
- When to use: bridge sections (problem → solution), "but here's the good news" transitions, social proof previews.
- Transition: introduce YOUR specific solution with evidence.

### State 4: Belief
Internal monologue: *"This solution could work for me."*
- Definition: logical acceptance + identification with proof subjects. YOUR solution, not just any solution.
- Example trigger: testimonials from similar people, case studies with specifics, demonstrations, credentials, mechanism walkthroughs, risk reversal.
- Example: "[Name] was a [same job/situation as reader] who thought [same doubt reader has]. Now [specific result]."
- When to use: proof sections, testimonial blocks, "how it works" explanations, guarantee blocks.
- Critical nuance: belief isn't just proof — it's identification. They need to see "people like me" succeeding.
- Transition: move from "it works" to "I want it." Paint the after.

### State 5: Desire
Internal monologue: *"I want this."*
- Definition: emotional wanting (not just logical acceptance). Visualization of outcome.
- Example trigger: future pacing, contrast painting (before/after in sharp relief), identity shift framing, sensory outcome descriptions.
- Example: "Imagine checking your phone at 10 AM Tuesday — not from the office, from a coffee shop — and seeing [specific positive outcome]. That's not 'someday.' That's 12 weeks from now."
- When to use: future-pacing sections, transformation descriptions, benefit amplification, outcome visualization.
- Transition: create a reason to act NOW. Without urgency, desire becomes "someday."

### State 6: Urgency
Internal monologue: *"I need this NOW."*
- Definition: compulsion to act immediately. Fear of missing out or delay.
- Example trigger: legitimate deadlines, limited availability, price increases, bonus expiration, cost of waiting, momentum framing.
- Example: "We opened 50 spots. 31 are gone. When they're gone, the next cohort isn't until [date]."
- When to use: close sections, deadline statements, "act now" CTAs, cost-of-inaction framing, final push.
- Result: purchase decision.

## The Curiosity Engine (between every state)

Curiosity is the fuel between states. Every transition needs a hook:

| Transition | Verbatim hook examples |
|---|---|
| Indifference → Pain | "But here's the part nobody talks about..." |
| Pain → Problem | "Want to know what's actually causing this?" / "There's a reason this keeps happening..." |
| Problem → Hope | "But here's what I discovered..." / "Most people never realize this, but..." |
| Hope → Belief | "Let me prove it to you..." / "Here's exactly how this works..." |
| Belief → Desire | "Now imagine what happens when..." / "Picture yourself six months from now..." |
| Desire → Urgency | "But here's the thing..." / "There's one catch..." |

Principles: open a loop before closing the previous one; promise value in the next section; create information gaps; use specificity to drive curiosity; never fully satisfy until the close.

## Application rules

> "A reader cannot feel a later state until they've fully experienced the earlier ones. Pain before Problem. Problem before Hope. Hope before Belief. Belief before Desire. Desire before Urgency." — Mark Masters

**Hard rules:**
- States execute in order. No skipping. No reversing.
- Every transition needs a curiosity hook. Without it, momentum dies.
- Each state must hit intensity ≥ 3/5 to count as "achieved." Below 3 = needs amplification.
- Belief must include identification — "people like me" signals, not just any proof.
- Urgency without Desire = 0. Urgency multiplies desire. If desire is zero, urgency × 0 = 0.

**Audience-specific adjustments (verbatim):**
- High-ticket ($2,000+): longer Belief, extended Hope, multiple urgency touches.
- Low-ticket (under $100): compressed sequence, less proof, faster to desire.
- B2B: longer Problem Awareness (logic matters), ROI-focused Desire, authority-objection handling.
- B2C: longer Pain (emotion-driven), story-heavy Belief, lifestyle Desire, FOMO urgency.
- Aware audience: shorter Pain/Problem; can move faster to Belief.
- Cold audience: full sequence required, extended Pain and Problem, heavier proof stack.

## Diagnostic / scoring

Reviewer audit produces a 5-step report.

**Step 1: Section Mapping** — for each section: target emotional state? achieved? required prior state met?

**Step 2: Sequence Check** — fill the table:

| Section | Target State | Previous Required State | Sequence Valid? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Headline | Break Indifference | None | ✓ |
| Opening | Pain Recognition | Indifference broken | ✓ / ✗ |
| Problem | Problem Awareness | Pain felt | ✓ / ✗ |
| Bridge | Hope | Problem understood | ✓ / ✗ |
| Solution | Belief | Hope established | ✓ / ✗ |
| Offer | Desire | Belief established | ✓ / ✗ |
| Close | Urgency | Desire felt | ✓ / ✗ |

**Step 3: Transition Audit** — for each transition: curiosity hook present? earned? gap or reversal?

**Step 4: Identification Check** (Belief specifically) — does proof match reader identity? "People like me" signals? Relatable specifics?

**Step 5: Intensity Scoring** — rate each state 1–5 (5 = powerfully felt, 3 = present but weak, 1 = barely there). Anything < 3 needs amplification.

## Common failures (the 7 sequence breaks)

1. **The Premature Close** — asking for the sale before Belief. "Buy now!" before showing proof. They want it to work but don't believe it will.
2. **The Missing Diagnosis** — Pain without Problem Awareness. Three paragraphs of agitation but never naming the cause. They feel bad but can't evaluate your solution.
3. **The Hope Gap** — jumping Problem → Belief, skipping Hope. "Here's why you're failing. Here's my product with 47 testimonials." Feels manipulative; the testimonials read as salesmanship.
4. **The Logic Leap** — Indifference → Problem without Pain. "Most freelancers struggle with client acquisition because..." They don't FEEL anything yet — the information bounces off.
5. **The Urgency Without Desire** — pushing scarcity before they want the thing. "Only 7 spots left!" before you've made them want a spot. Sounds desperate.
6. **The Belief Without Identification** — proof from people unlike the reader. Fortune 500 CMO testimonials when selling to freelancers. They believe it works for THOSE people, not them.
7. **The Curiosity Collapse** — hitting an emotional state but failing to pull them forward. Strong pain section that ends without teasing the diagnosis. They felt something — and then left.
