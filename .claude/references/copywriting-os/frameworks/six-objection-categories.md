# 6 Objection Categories

## Canonical definition

Every objection a prospect will ever have falls into one of six categories. Master these and you'll never be blindsided. Objections must be handled **pre-emptively** — woven into copy before the prospect's brain crystallizes them — not reactively patched into a FAQ. A missed objection is a leaked conversion.

> "The Amateur Approach: Write the copy. Send to client. Client says 'people keep asking about X.' Scramble to add a FAQ. Repeat until launch. This is objection whack-a-mole. You're always one step behind." — Mark Masters

> "The difference between 0.3% and 3.2% conversion is systematic objection anticipation." — Mark Masters

## The 6 elements

### Category 1: Price Objections — *"This costs too much"*
Common variations: "It's too expensive" / "I can't afford this right now" / "I've seen similar products for less" / "Price doesn't match value" / "Wait until I have more money" / "Why does this cost more than [competitor]?" / "Can I get a discount?" / "What if it doesn't work — I'll have wasted the money" / "Can't justify this expense to my spouse" / "Payment plan still too much monthly."

Handling approaches:
- Price anchoring against higher alternatives
- ROI calculations (cost vs. value gained)
- Cost of inaction framing
- Daily/weekly cost equivalents
- Investment vs. expense reframe
- Payment plan positioning

### Category 2: Timing Objections — *"Not right now"*
Common variations: "I'll do this later" / "Now isn't a good time" / "Too busy" / "I'll wait until after [event]" / "Need to finish X first" / "Let me think about it" / "More research first" / "Not ready yet" / "Maybe next month" / "Too much on my plate."

Handling approaches:
- Future pacing the cost of delay
- "Perfect timing" myth destruction
- Quick-start positioning
- Implementation timeline clarity
- Urgency through scarcity / deadline
- Immediate small-win demonstration

### Category 3: Trust Objections — *"How do I know this works?"*
Common variations: "Who are you?" / "Never heard of you" / "Is this a scam?" / "What if it doesn't work for me?" / "Been burned before" / "Testimonials could be fake" / "Sounds too good to be true" / "What's the catch?" / "Why trust you over [competitor]?" / "No reviews from people like me."

Handling approaches:
- Social proof stacking (testimonials, case studies, logos)
- Credentials & authority markers
- Transparency about limitations
- Risk reversal (guarantees)
- Behind-the-scenes access
- Third-party validation

### Category 4: Need Objections — *"I don't really need this"*
Common variations: "I'm doing fine without it" / "My situation is different" / "Already have something similar" / "Not a priority" / "Doesn't apply to me" / "Gotten by without it" / "Nice-to-have, not must-have" / "Not sure I'd use it" / "Problem isn't that serious" / "I can figure this out myself."

Handling approaches:
- Problem agitation
- Hidden cost revelation
- "What you don't know" positioning
- Future consequence projection
- Opportunity cost framing
- "Doing fine" vs. "thriving" contrast

### Category 5: Authority Objections — *"I can't decide alone"*
Common variations: "Need to ask my spouse" / "Run this by my boss" / "Business partner handles this" / "Check with my team" / "Discuss with my accountant" / "Affects more than just me" / "I don't make purchasing decisions" / "Need approval for this amount" / "Stakeholder wouldn't approve" / "Present to the board."

Handling approaches:
- Shareable summary creation
- Stakeholder objection anticipation
- Decision-maker specific benefits
- "How to pitch this to your [stakeholder]" content
- Money-back guarantee for "permission" safety
- ROI documentation for approval processes

### Category 6: Effort Objections — *"This looks too hard"*
Common variations: "Don't have time" / "Too complicated" / "Not tech-savvy" / "Don't have the skills" / "Too much to learn" / "Never finish" / "Don't know where to start" / "What if I get stuck?" / "Tried before, couldn't keep up" / "Requires too much work."

Handling approaches:
- Time requirement transparency
- Step-by-step simplification
- Quick-win positioning (Module 1 result)
- "Done for you" highlighting
- Support / community proof
- Progress milestones and checkpoints

## Application rules

**Probability scoring (1–5):**
- 5 = Almost everyone will have this objection
- 4 = Majority
- 3 = About half
- 2 = Some
- 1 = Few

> "Any category scoring 4+ requires aggressive pre-emptive handling. Categories scoring 3 require standard handling. Categories scoring 1-2 can be addressed in FAQ or ignored." — Mark Masters

> "Never let copy leave without addressing all objections scoring 3 or higher. A missed objection is a leaked conversion." — Mark Masters

**Placement map by funnel stage (verbatim):**

| Stage | Objections to handle here |
|---|---|
| Headline / Hook | Need ("Is this for me?"), Trust ("Who is this?") |
| Problem / Agitation | Need ("Is my problem really that bad?") |
| Solution Introduction | Trust, Effort |
| Offer Presentation | Price, Authority |
| Close / CTA | Timing, Price (final), all residual |
| FAQ | Low-priority residual (score 1–2) |

**Three weaving rules:**
1. **Weave, don't insert.** Objection handling should feel like natural copy progression. Never say "You might be thinking..." Address objections through stories, proof, and framing. The reader shouldn't notice they're being handled.
2. **Pre-empt, don't react.** Handle price before stating price. Handle trust before asking for action. Handle effort before describing implementation.
3. **Stack multiple approaches.** One handler rarely neutralizes an objection completely. Use 2–3 handling approaches for high-probability objections, spaced throughout the copy. Return to major objections at key decision points.

| Authority | X/5         | Present/Missing    | Strong/Weak/Missing    | High/Med/Low     |
| Effort    | X/5         | Present/Missing    | Strong/Weak/Missing    | High/Med/Low     |

### Critical Gaps
### Recommended Handlers
### Implementation Priority
```

Audit process: (1) extract offer details (price, competitor pricing, time-to-results, implementation, audience sophistication, available proof, guarantee). (2) Score each category 1–5. (3) For each scoring 3+, evaluate current handling strength. (4) Map placement against funnel-stage table. (5) Recommend handlers from the library.

## Common failures

1. **Reactive FAQ patching** — adding handlers only after clients say "people keep asking about X." Always one step behind.
2. **Unhandled high-probability objection** — leaving a 4–5 score category unaddressed (the $23K launch failure: $997 product priced 2x competitors, zero price-objection handling anywhere).
3. **Single-handler stacking on the dominant objection** — one weak attempt at the load-bearing objection instead of 2–3 handlers spaced throughout.
4. **"You might be thinking..." inserts** — obvious, defensive objection blocks that signal salesmanship instead of weaving handling invisibly through stories, proof, and framing.

**Objection Arsenal — Custom Instructions:**

```
You are an expert at identifying and neutralizing buyer objections in copy. You
have access to a comprehensive objection library with 6 categories and pre-written
handlers.

When analyzing copy or offers:
1. Always identify objections by category (Price, Timing, Trust, Need, Authority, Effort)
2. Score each objection's probability (1-5) for the specific audience

When writing or improving copy:
1. Weave objection handling naturally—never as obvious "FAQ" insertions

**Pre-written handler library (excerpts — verbatim):**

Price:
- *P1 Investment Reframe*: "This isn't an expense—it's an investment that pays for itself within [timeframe]. Here's the math: [specific ROI calculation]. You're not spending $X. You're trading $X for $Y."
- *P2 Daily Cost Breakdown*: "$997 sounds like a lot until you realize it's $2.73 per day for a year. That's less than your morning coffee. Except your coffee doesn't [primary benefit]."
- *P3 Cost of Waiting*: "Every month you wait costs you approximately $[amount] in [lost revenue/wasted time/missed opportunities]. In 6 months, you'll have spent more avoiding this solution than investing in it."
- *P4 Competitor Anchor*: "Yes, you can find cheaper alternatives. You can also find cheaper surgeons, cheaper lawyers, and cheaper accountants. Some things you don't optimize for price."

Timing:
- *T1 Perfect Time Myth*: "There's never a 'perfect time' to [achieve goal]. There's only the time you start and the time you wish you had."
- *T2 Implementation Reality*: "You don't need to clear your schedule. This takes [X] per [day/week]. If you have time to [common activity], you have time for this."
- *T4 Quick Win*: "Within [short timeframe], you'll have [specific early result]."

Trust:
- *TR2 Guarantee Reversal*: "Try it for [X days]. If you don't [specific outcome], you get every penny back. I take the risk. You take the results."
- *TR3 Skeptic Acknowledgment*: "I'd be skeptical too. In fact, I was when I first discovered [method]. Then I saw [specific proof point]."
- *TR5 People Like You Proof*: "[Avatar description] thought the same thing. Here's what happened when they tried it anyway: [specific result]."

Need:
- *N1 Hidden Cost Revelation*: "You think you're doing fine. But here's what 'fine' is actually costing you: [hidden cost 1], [hidden cost 2], [hidden cost 3]. 'Fine' is expensive."
- *N2 Future Projection*: "In 12 months, one of two things will be true: Either you'll have [achieved outcome], or you'll be exactly where you are now, wishing you'd started. There's no third option."

Authority:
- *A1 Shareable Summary*: "Show them this: [brief summary of offer, results, ROI]."
- *A3 Risk Removal*: "The money-back guarantee means there's no risk to get approval for. You're asking permission to try something at no cost."

Effort:
- *E1 Time Truth*: "This requires [exact time commitment]. That's it. Not hours per day. [X] per [timeframe]. If you can't find that, the problem isn't time."
- *E3 Quick Win First*: "Start with [Module 1]. It takes [time] and gives you [immediate result]. If that doesn't convince you to continue, nothing will."

**Objection Weaver techniques (5 invisible-surgery moves):**
1. **Embedded Testimonial** — *"I almost didn't buy because [objection]. Then I realized [handler]."*
2. **Acknowledged Aside** — *"...and even though [objection acknowledgment], [handler reframe]..."*
3. **Proof Point** — *"[Existing claim]—verified by [proof that handles objection]."*
4. **Story Expansion** — *"She hesitated because [objection]. But then [what happened]."*
5. **Preemptive Strike** — *"Before I share the investment..."* → add value/ROI context first.
