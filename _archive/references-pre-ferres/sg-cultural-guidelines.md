# Singapore Cultural Sensitivity Guidelines for Ad Content

## General Principles

Singapore is multiracial (Chinese ~75%, Malay ~15%, Indian ~8%, Others ~2%). Ad content must be culturally aware regardless of which demographic is being targeted.

## Malay-Singaporean Audience

### Do
- Reference family values — multigenerational wealth, legacy, providing for parents and children
- Acknowledge the aspiration to progress financially while staying grounded in community
- Use "we" framing — the couple decides together, not one person leading
- Acknowledge Islamic financial principles exist (without claiming to follow them unless the client does)
- Reference community touchpoints (void decks, neighbourhood, estate identity)
- Show families, not just individuals — property decisions are household decisions

### Don't
- Mock or trivialise HDB living — many families are proud of their HDB homes
- Imply that not upgrading = failure or low status
- Use "keep up with the Joneses" framing — it reads as shallow and disrespectful
- Reference alcohol, gambling, or non-halal imagery in visuals
- Show overly westernised lifestyle imagery that doesn't reflect the audience
- Use aggressive debt-shaming language ("stuck in debt," "throwing money away")

### Sensitive Periods
- **Ramadan / Hari Raya Aidilfitri** — if running ads during this period, acknowledge respectfully or stay neutral. Don't use religious occasions as urgency triggers ("Hari Raya special — upgrade now!")
- **National Day period** — property ads with national pride angle can work but avoid exploiting patriotism

## Property-Specific Sensitivity

### Do
- Acknowledge the emotional weight of property decisions in SG culture
- Recognise that HDB is a point of national pride, not something to "escape from"
- Frame upgrading as progression, not escape
- Use "private property" not "condo" when the client's methodology covers ECs and condos
- Acknowledge that agents have a trust deficit — don't pretend it doesn't exist

### Don't
- Name specific HDB estates negatively ("Don't get stuck in Woodlands")
- Compare HDB to condo in a way that denigrates HDB owners
- Imply government housing policy is failing
- Use language that could trigger PDPA concerns ("We know your MOP is ending")
- Make financial guarantees or implied promises of returns (MAS compliance)
- Show only luxury/high-end imagery when the audience is mass-market

## Islamic Finance Sensitivity

### Do
- Acknowledge that riba (interest) is a genuine concern for devout Muslim families
- Reference Shariah-compliant financing options (Musharakah Mutanaqisah) when relevant
- Use "monthly commitment" or "financing" instead of "mortgage" when targeting Muslim audiences
- Treat the question "Is this halal?" with deep respect — it's a values question, not a trivial one
- Reference that SG banks (Maybank, OCBC, etc.) offer Islamic financing options

### Don't
- Trivialise Islamic finance principles
- Frame conventional mortgages as positive/desirable when targeting devout Muslim audience
- Claim the client's service is "halal" unless they specifically offer Shariah-compliant options
- Use Islamic imagery as decoration or marketing gimmick
- Oversimplify — Islamic finance is complex and families take it seriously

## Visual Representation

### Ethnicity in Image Generation
When creating ad images for Malay-Singaporean audience:
- Subject descriptors: "Malay-Singaporean," "Southeast Asian Muslim"
- Clothing: Smart casual or professional — hijab if representing Muslim women (not mandatory, represent diversity)
- Setting: Modern Singapore interiors, HDB upgrader context, family-oriented
- Avoid: Stereotypical ethnic markers used as props

### Diversity
- If showing multiple people, reflect Singapore's multiracial context naturally
- Don't tokenise — if the ad is for a Malay-focused brand, Malay representation is primary

## Language Register

- Singlish is acceptable in casual/social content (TikTok, Instagram Stories) but not in formal ad copy
- "Lah," "lor," "can" — use sparingly and authentically, never forced
- For Meta ad copy: Standard English with natural Singaporean phrasing
- Never use fake Singlish to seem relatable if the brand doesn't naturally speak that way
