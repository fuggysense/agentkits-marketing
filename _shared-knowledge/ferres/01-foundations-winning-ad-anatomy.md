## 0. Scope of his system
- Ferres' pipeline scope is AD CREATION only — explicitly excludes offer creation, sales/landing-page writing, and media buying (campaign setup/scaling) (02_part-1-the-million-dollar-opportunity_DZj9WD1kLg [00:06:13]–[00:06:48]).
- Goal of advertising is to MAXIMIZE (scale spend), not over-optimize ROAS decimals — a 2x at scale beats a 4x at trickle (02_part-1-the-million-dollar-opportunity_DZj9WD1kLg [00:01:44]–[00:02:01]).
- Run direct-response ads ONLY: immediate, measurable action — no brand-awareness or "consideration" plays (03_part-2-what-makes-a-winning-ad_GZW1EY4dqA [00:04:57]–[00:05:09]).

## 1. Market positioning before any copy
- **Schwartz awareness gate:** write only to Problem-Aware and Solution-Aware buyers (through Most-Aware). Never target Unaware — that's billion-dollar-brand territory (03_part-2-what-makes-a-winning-ad_GZW1EY4dqA [00:00:15]–[00:01:51]).
- **Chet Holmes stadium rule:** ~3% of any market is "buying now" (in-market), ~7% "needs convincing." Market ONLY to the 3% until you've literally saturated it (he pegs that at ~$1M/mo spend) before descending to the 7%. Let competitors do the convincing; you scoop ready buyers (03_part-2-what-makes-a-winning-ad_GZW1EY4dqA [00:02:10]–[00:03:30]; text/03_63572440_The_AI_Ads_Lab_Pt_2_-_What_Makes_a_Winning_Ad.txt).
- Practical test: an in-market ad just names the want + offer ("need a PA yesterday? here's where to get the best for a fair price"); a needs-convinced ad must argue WHY they need it. If your draft is arguing why, you targeted the wrong layer (03_part-2-what-makes-a-winning-ad_GZW1EY4dqA [00:03:38]–[00:04:47]).
- **B2B vs B2C register split:** B2B/wealthy = direct, credible, expert dress/talk, simple talking-head fine, lead with ROI + proof, get to the point fast. B2C/mass-market = shorter attention, flashy fast cuts and wild AI scenes pay off. Edutainment dial: B2B leans education, B2C leans entertainment. Flashy editing actively turns rich buyers off (03_part-2-what-makes-a-winning-ad_GZW1EY4dqA [00:09:55]–[00:11:35]).

## 2. The only 3 things that matter ⚠️PLATFORM (Meta)
1. **Right person** (precise ICP), 2. **Right offer** (irresistible value prop for that person), 3. **Pixel conditioning** (only let qualified conversions report to the pixel — e.g., separate thank-you pages, no pixel on the unqualified page, or Meta optimizes toward junk). Miss one and you struggle; get all three and targeting is Meta's job, not yours (03_part-2-what-makes-a-winning-ad_GZW1EY4dqA [00:06:10]–[00:08:59]).
- Corollary: don't fiddle with manual targeting — craft messaging so specific the right person can't help responding; the algorithm matches it (transcribes your video, scans your landing page) (03_part-2-what-makes-a-winning-ad_GZW1EY4dqA [00:09:01]–[00:09:22], [00:26:33]–[00:26:48]). His "52,000 data points per user" framing is a Meta-internal claim — treat as directional, not audit-grade (03_part-2-what-makes-a-winning-ad_GZW1EY4dqA [00:17:41]).

## 3. Messaging law + ICP
- **#1 Law of Sales:** describe the prospect's current reality better than they can themselves and they automatically assume you have the solution (his Iman Ghanzhi "$27k bought on the spot" story is the proof case) (03_part-2-what-makes-a-winning-ad_GZW1EY4dqA [00:11:55]–[00:13:18]).
- ICP is upstream of everything — hooks, angles, copy, visuals, ad type all flow from who you target; nail it and "the messaging writes itself" (03_part-2-what-makes-a-winning-ad_GZW1EY4dqA [00:13:47]–[00:14:23]).
- **Mirror-spokesperson rule:** the on-camera person should LOOK like the avatar (plumbers listen to a plumber, not a suit — a suit violates Law #1 before a word lands). AI lets you cast any avatar-mirror spokesperson without production cost (03_part-2-what-makes-a-winning-ad_GZW1EY4dqA [00:14:46]–[00:16:34]).

## 4. Andromeda context ⚠️DATED (July-2025 Meta algo rebuild) ⚠️PLATFORM
- Old playbook (3–5 core bodies × swapped hooks ≈ 10–15 ads, incl. Hormozi-style "5 bangers + new hooks") is dead. You now need **25–35 completely unique ads end-to-end** to feed the algorithm's creative-diversity demand. "Creative diversity" = diversity of MESSAGE, not backgrounds (03_part-2-what-makes-a-winning-ad_GZW1EY4dqA [00:26:17]–[00:27:42]).
- **Minority hooks are now majors:** fringe motives (sleep better, save the marriage, live longer) that used to be B-roll variants can each carry their own ad, because the algo can route niche messages to niche people (03_part-2-what-makes-a-winning-ad_GZW1EY4dqA [00:24:40]–[00:26:08]).
- Still true as of May 2026: "the methodology hasn't changed"; his AI-hook ads from Oct 2025 were still his best performers 6+ months later (text/01_65586044_BODY.txt) ⚠️DATED.

## 5. Ad anatomy — 3-part skeleton, 7 elements
Every ad = **Hook → Body → CTA** (03_part-2-what-makes-a-winning-ad_GZW1EY4dqA [00:27:53]–[00:28:20]). The 7-element checklist (recap at [00:48:09]–[00:48:21]):
1. **Hook (pattern interrupt).** Avg retention ≈ 1.8s ⚠️DATED — if the scroll doesn't stop, nothing else exists (03 [00:28:30]–[00:29:07]). Forms: shocking statement, unexpected visual, provocative question, bold claim (03 [00:36:11]–[00:36:19]). Job = open curiosity loops the viewer must resolve ("so good they'd recharge a dead phone to finish it") (03 [00:31:02]–[00:31:28]). Mechanism: each unit's only job is to earn the next unit (subject line → first line → … → click) (03 [00:29:10]–[00:29:39]). Get them past ~3s and the retention curve says they'll likely stay to the end (03 [00:30:30]–[00:31:07]).
2. **Agitate the problem.** Don't mention pain — make it visceral: frustration, cost of inaction, mounting consequences, in the avatar's own words; stay Meta-compliant (no direct "you're a failure" callouts); best delivered by a been-in-your-shoes spokesperson (03 [00:37:23]–[00:38:08]; text/03 deck "Element 2").
3. **Tease the solution.** Sell the transformation (WHAT it does), never the mechanism (HOW) — details live after the click. Never resolve the tension mid-ad; viral videos end the instant the payoff lands because viewers scroll the moment curiosity is satisfied (03 [00:38:08]–[00:38:25], [00:53:19]–[00:54:16]).
4. **Proof — "proof is your god."** Numbers, testimonials, specific results; his A/B: identical sales page minus proof = $800 spend, zero sales; with proof = first sale in ~2h (03 [00:38:43]–[00:39:28]). Proof must MATCH the avatar (busy-mom result for busy-mom targets, not "Bob lost weight") (03 [00:39:46]–[00:40:04]). Demonstration is the most powerful form of proof — show it, don't claim it (03 [00:53:05]).
5. **Handle objections in the order they arise ("copyboarding").** Agora-style: read the script to a cold human, capture the question/objection each line triggers, answer at that exact moment. Every claim creates a new objection; handle by explanation or proof (03 [00:40:09]–[00:42:52]). Decision sequence: care → "is it for me" → want it → believe it (proof) → only then logistics/price (03 [00:43:50]–[00:44:06]). #1 objection everywhere: "does this really work?" — crush with proof; guarantees alone are weak ("pretty mid") (03 [00:44:21]–[00:44:40]).
6. **Urgency + scarcity — but TRUE.** Without a reason to act now, people don't act; >half of launch sales land on the last day (03 [00:45:03]–[00:45:16]). Build real urgency into the offer's structure (caps, closing dates, application-only prestige) rather than faking countdowns (03 [00:45:25]–[00:45:41], [00:47:30]–[00:51:05]). Evidence: adding one true "registrations closing soon" bar doubled page conversion ~17%→31% (03 [00:46:06]–[00:47:04]).
7. **CTA: command + pre-frame.** Tell them exactly what to do AND what happens next ("I shot a video for you on the next page — click below to watch") so they comply on landing; the ad's job is the next step, not the sale — the page does the heavy lifting (03 [00:33:00]–[00:33:52], [00:54:48]–[00:55:12]).

## 6. Length + format rules
- Length = "as long as it needs to be": 15s minimum effective, **30–60s sweet spot**, ~90s short-form ceiling; full 5–10-min VSLs run as in-feed ads are converting ⚠️DATED (03 [00:31:33]–[00:32:45]; text/03 deck). Trade-off rule: the less selling the ad does, the more the page must do (03 [00:32:19]–[00:32:28]). Every second earns its place — "all killer no filler" (03 [00:32:45]–[00:32:54]).
- Hook caption tip ⚠️PLATFORM: native IG-story-style on-screen text for the first 3s (a curiosity headline) dramatically lifts thumbstop (03 [00:29:43]–[00:30:26]).
- **Five ad formats:** talking head; AI hook → talking head; fully-AI video; testimonial/UGC (human or AI); B-roll + captions/voiceover (03 [00:55:13]–[00:55:48]; text/03 deck).
- Hook archetypes he generates in batches: shock/controversy, curiosity-gap, social-proof, fear/urgency, benefit-driven (06_part-5-full-end-to-end-ai-ad-creation-demo_Yq2N89Pvqa [00:27:25]).

## 7. Winner-vs-loser diagnostics (from live ad roasts)
- **The hook decides WHO sees the ad, not just whether they watch** — Meta routes distribution off it, so a sloppy hook trains the pixel on the wrong crowd (22_roast-my-ads-call-for-daniel-throssell-promo-buy_aZ8PA306Zp [00:24:22]–[00:24:38]).
- Spend disproportionate effort on the hook/headline — his Ogilvy-paraphrase "~80% of your effort" even though it's one sentence (22 [00:34:10]–[00:34:25]).
- **Broad-hook/narrow-offer mismatch kills funnels:** if the offer isn't for the masses, the hook must call out the avatar explicitly or you fill the funnel with junk (22 [00:40:22]–[00:40:41], [00:43:09]–[00:43:36]).
- Avatar callouts without saying the name: write hooks around their problems, circumstances, and outcomes, using insider language only that avatar decodes (22 [00:50:39]–[00:51:15]).
- Common-enemy angles reliably rile emotion for problem-/un-aware audiences (gov't, Wall Street, "agencies don't want you to know") (22 [00:34:43]–[00:34:54]).
- Don't open on an unknown authority's name — it shrinks attention to people who already know them (22 [00:24:03]–[00:24:12]).
- Don't draft from a blank page: model the client's own proven winners + competitors' long-running ads first (04_part-3-how-to-create-winning-ads_5Z3aBJwWqa [00:04:43]).
