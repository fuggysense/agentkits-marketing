# Ferres Quality Bar — Critique Rubric. Operational checklist distilled from Sean Ferres' live ad critiques; run it section-by-section against any drafted Meta ad before launch.
**Primary lectures:** `transcripts/22_roast-my-ads-call-for-daniel-throssell-promo-buy_aZ8PA306Zp.md` (cited as **ROAST**) and `transcripts/09_bonus-q-a-call-for-first-20-buyers_WLa4AAQEqk.md` (cited as **QA**), plus course docs in `text/`.
**Last distilled:** 2026-06-10.

## 0. Diagnostic protocol — before critiquing anything
1. Never review blind. Establish first: what's the offer, who's the avatar, which funnel does this ad feed (ROAST [00:23:00]).
2. Ask if the ad is live and performing. "If it's working, it's working" — ROAS is the only North Star; things you'd swear can't work sometimes do, so critique winners only for scale, never for replacement (ROAST [00:51:48]).
3. A previously-working ad that died is not automatically a copy problem: check frequency creep (3–4x ⚠️PLATFORM Meta) for fatigue, and whether the pixel swung to a different audience, before touching the words (ROAST [00:54:19]).

## 1. Hook — where review effort concentrates (his #1 recurring criticism)
- [ ] **Effort allocation.** Disproportionate time goes on the hook vs the body — he cites Ogilvy's ~80%-of-effort-on-headline line as the operating ratio (ROAST [00:34:10]).
- [ ] **Hook does TWO jobs:** stops the scroll AND tells the algorithm who to show the ad to — the words in the hook steer delivery, not just persuasion (ROAST [00:24:28]) ⚠️PLATFORM Meta/Andromeda.
- [ ] **First 3 seconds are the hook**; most viewers never reach the body, so each new hook on the same body is effectively a new ad to the market (QA [00:12:45], QA [00:41:31]).
- [ ] **Kill opener name-drops.** Authority/founder names in line 1 limit attention to people who already know them — recurring prescribed fix: delete the first line unless the name is a true industry-wide authority (ROAST [00:24:03]).
- [ ] **Pattern interrupt required:** shocking statement, unexpected visual, provocative question, or bold claim (text/03_63572440_The_AI_Ads_Lab_Pt_2_-_What_Makes_a_Winning_Ad.txt).
- [ ] **No template call-outs.** "If you're a coach or agency owner doing $30k/mo…" is ad-blind wallpaper; do the call-out creatively — insider language, pain articulation, identity humor only the avatar gets (ROAST [00:46:30], [00:51:01]).
- [ ] **Frame hooks on problems / circumstances / outcomes** specific to the avatar. Specific goal ("2–3 high-paying retainers") beats generic goal ("make six figures online") every time (ROAST [00:50:44], [00:51:24]).

## 2. Audience call-out & self-disqualification (recurring criticism #2)
- [ ] If the offer is NOT for the general masses, the ad must say who it's for **and who it's NOT for** — ideally in the hook; a single disqualifier sentence is enough (ROAST [00:40:32], [01:10:29]).
- [ ] **Brokie-bait scan.** Any "made $X fast/easily" framing opens the floodgates to low-quality leads who ignore all later qualification — it poisons pixel targeting even when the rest of the funnel is clearly niched (ROAST [00:27:16], [00:41:26]) ⚠️PLATFORM Meta pixel.
- [ ] **Hidden-assumption scan.** Any phrase assuming the reader has something most lack ("your book" → "wait, I don't have a book") makes them self-disqualify and scroll; address or reword the instant the assumption appears (ROAST [01:15:27]).
- [ ] Add **"even if…" qualifiers** for the disqualifying beliefs surfaced in research — the overwhelming majority of prospects disqualify themselves by default (ROAST [01:16:21]).

## 3. Claims, proof, objections — "copyboarding" (recurring criticism #3)
- [ ] **Every claim raises an objection.** Crush each with proof and/or handling the moment it forms in the reader's head — the Agora "copyboarding" discipline: objection-handling in real time, since copy has no live feedback loop like sales calls (ROAST [00:36:17], [00:38:28]).
- [ ] An unhandled objection means the reader hears every later line through "yeah but…" and either dismisses the offer or scrolls (ROAST [00:38:04]).
- [ ] **Objections come from research docs and ad comments, never the writer's guesses.** "This is a scam" comments = a believability gap the ad failed to close; each recurring comment becomes a new ad that answers it (ROAST [00:37:37], [00:40:04]).
- [ ] **Emotion → logic bridge.** An emotionally charged open (common enemy is his most reliable charge: "nothing gets people more emotionally riled up") must hand off to a tight logical chain of arguments that lands on the offer in a few sentences (ROAST [00:34:49], [00:35:37]).
- [ ] Fallback headline formula when in doubt: **"How to [thing they want] without [thing they don't want] even if [objection]"** — the negative modifiers must come from customer research (ROAST [01:11:17], [01:11:54]).
- [ ] If the market has been burned by lookalike offers (e.g. done-for-you Amazon stores), the ad must explicitly differentiate from the nuked model or readers mentally file it as "one of those" and scroll (ROAST [01:12:09]).
- [ ] **Proof is "the #1 most important element… your god in copywriting"** — numbers, testimonials, specific outcomes for every major claim (text/03_63572440_The_AI_Ads_Lab_Pt_2_-_What_Makes_a_Winning_Ad.txt).

## 4. Native feel & word economy
- [ ] **The best ads don't look like ads** — they read as organic content until late in the piece; very direct "obviously an ad" formats only convert the small now-buyer slice and won't scale (ROAST [00:47:33], [00:47:03]).
- [ ] A top performer can be structurally trivial: extended avatar call-out → articulate their pains in their words ("I am one of you") → basic problem-solution → CTA (ROAST [00:44:51], [00:45:21]).
- [ ] **Word economy is the mastery test**: fit everything required into the fewest words; every word counts (ROAST [01:10:36]). Body length is flexible (30–60s sweet spot) but "every second must earn its place. Cut all the fluff" (text/03_63572440_The_AI_Ads_Lab_Pt_2_-_What_Makes_a_Winning_Ad.txt).
- [ ] On-screen captions for the first seconds + a curiosity headline overlay — people instinctively read text on screen (text/03_63572440_The_AI_Ads_Lab_Pt_2_-_What_Makes_a_Winning_Ad.txt).

## 5. Angle diversity & scale diagnosis (recurring criticism #4)
- [ ] **One hyper-specific angle = TAM exhaustion.** Rising lead costs on a niche angle usually mean the pool is scooped, not that the copy broke. Piano metaphor: "you're hitting the same note again and again… there's 87 other notes" — mind-map 20–30 reasons someone would buy; each becomes an angle (ROAST [00:56:54], [01:00:01], [01:00:06]).
- [ ] When an angle is exhausted: try a different funnel type (webinar → lead magnet unlocks a new pool) or a different topic, before rewriting the ad (ROAST [01:02:03], [01:02:32]).
- [ ] **1% remixes don't scale.** Meta's similarity score classes near-duplicates as one idea; hook-swaps on one body are fine at test budgets but hit a ceiling at scale — new ideas required (ROAST [01:05:44], [01:06:10]) ⚠️PLATFORM Andromeda, 2026.
- [ ] **Newsworthy angle crushes:** frame like a journalist — "X quietly rolled out [feature] last month and almost no one knows" — something just changed in the market (ROAST [01:13:08], [01:14:45]).
- [ ] **Urgency must be baked into a true market narrative** (a real, closing window). "Limited time/spots" slapped on is level-1 urgency, rated "pretty meh" (ROAST [01:14:12]).
- [ ] **Don't blindly swipe.** Another advertiser's choices solved *their* specific problem (his über-aggressive call-outs existed to retrain a polluted pixel); copying form without the diagnosis copies the wrong medicine (ROAST [00:43:24]).
- [ ] Statics: the hook must sit at the **intrigue × benefit intersection** — "pure intrigue with no benefit pulls everyone and qualifies no one. Pure benefit with no intrigue gets ignored"; 80% of tests iterate proven winners, 20% fresh concepts; native statics route to an advertorial, almost never straight to a product page (text/24_65967688_The_Winning_Statics_Playbook_v4_by_Sean_Ferres.txt).
- [ ] Volume bar: "You don't need perfect. You need a volume of good-enough images that sell the click. The winners show up in the dashboard" (text/24_65967688_The_Winning_Statics_Playbook_v4_by_Sean_Ferres.txt).

## 6. Compliance & measurement gates ⚠️PLATFORM (all Meta-specific)
- [ ] No income/money-amount claims — disapproval risk (ROAST [01:11:42]).
- [ ] No personal-attribute call-outs ("you're morbidly obese and want to lose 30kg") — wildly non-compliant (QA [00:34:25]).
- [ ] **Pixel conditioning:** whoever converts trains future targeting; ~50 events/week to exit learning phase ⚠️DATED rule-of-thumb; optimize on the deepest event volume affords — buyer > qualified booked call > qualified lead (ROAST [00:28:24], [00:33:20]).
- [ ] **Test-budget realism:** pick a low cost-per-result event so a ~7-day test reaches significance; winning by one conversion on a $100+/call funnel proves nothing (QA [00:38:02], [00:38:36]).

## 7. Post-launch verification loop — the quality bar extends past the draft
- [ ] Launching is the start of iteration, not the end: see who comes through, then act (ROAST [00:39:42]).
- [ ] **Survey-gate the deliverable** (Typeform before download) to learn who's actually arriving — "radical clarity because otherwise you're just guessing"; cross-reference good leads back to specific ads and keep quality-lead ads even when their cost-per-lead is higher (ROAST [00:30:53], [00:55:41], [00:33:32]).
- [ ] **Marketing–sales feedback engine:** setters/closers tell you who the ads attract; want more → scale the ad, want different → fix front-end messaging (ROAST [00:42:00], [00:42:29]).
- [ ] Customer-support questions + ad comments = standing objection-research feed for the next batch (ROAST [00:39:51]).

## 8. Structural baseline (the course skeleton every draft is judged against)
7 elements, in order: **Hook (pattern interrupt) → Agitate problem** (visceral, relatable "been in their shoes" spokesperson) → **Tease solution** (the what/transformation, never the how) → **PROOF → Handle objections → Urgency/scarcity → CTA** that says exactly what to do AND what happens next (text/03_63572440_The_AI_Ads_Lab_Pt_2_-_What_Makes_a_Winning_Ad.txt).
Every generated angle must: pattern-interrupt in 3s, address researched objections, trigger strong emotion (fear/greed/curiosity/social proof/urgency/scarcity), feel native rather than salesy, and drive one action; majority of angles capture existing demand rather than create it (text/04_63572448_The_AI_Ads_Lab_Pt_3_-_How_to_Create_a_Winning_Ad.txt).
Anchor principle: AI video is "the cherry on the cake" ⚠️DATED (tactic pendulums decay as adoption spreads) — the messaging and the words, grounded in research, are the permanent core (QA [00:31:54], [00:32:25]).

## Most-recurring criticisms (frequency across the roast call)
1. Hook neglected or doing the wrong job (≥6 separate ads/threads). 2. Missing who-it's-for / who-it's-NOT-for (4+). 3. Claims with unhandled objections / no proof (4+). 4. Single exhausted angle misdiagnosed as bad copy (3). 5. "Looks like an ad" instead of native content (3).
Portfolio corollary: ship only your absolute best — reviewers sample one or two random pieces and judge everything from those (ROAST [01:03:11]).
