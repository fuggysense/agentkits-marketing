# Ferres — The Winning Statics Playbook (operational rulebook)

Last distilled: 2026-06-10.

## 1. Why statics (and the one tax you pay)
- **Speed/independence:** one operator can produce a batch of 25 launch-quality statics in a few hours — no editor, designer, founder filming, or UGC creator dependency (24_bonus-1-the-winning-statics-playbook [00:00:22]; text/24_65967688_The_Winning_Statics_Playbook_v4_by_Sean_Ferres.txt).
- **Cheap angle-testing:** before committing an angle to video, spin up ~5 statics on ~$100 spend and let the data answer — "cheap to be wrong, fast to be right" (24_bonus-1-the-winning-statics-playbook [00:01:01]).
- **They often beat video:** in his pricing-cheat-sheet campaign, a plain stock photo of someone holding the cheat sheet beat all 25 custom video ads on lead quality AND cost-per-lead. Never assume video wins by default (24_bonus-1-the-winning-statics-playbook [00:01:38]).
- **The tax — faster fatigue:** a static gives its whole message in one glance, so nothing pulls viewers back for a second pass. Rule: keep feeding fresh statics into the account and hold ad frequency **under 3** (24_bonus-1-the-winning-statics-playbook [00:02:05]–[00:02:16]) ⚠️PLATFORM (Meta frequency metric).

## 2. Direct vs indirect (awareness decides)
Map directness to Schwartz awareness: the hotter the audience, the more direct the static; the colder, the more indirect/curiosity-driven it must be (24_bonus-1-the-winning-statics-playbook [00:02:39]).
- **Direct** = the ~3% ready to buy. Product shot + headline (+ price), looks/smells like an ad, links straight to product page. Highest-ROAS but small ceiling — you tap out the audience fast once spend scales (24_bonus-1-the-winning-statics-playbook [00:02:52]–[00:03:25]; text/24_65967688_...txt).
- **Indirect** = the rest of the TAM. Doesn't look like an ad — looks like content (news post, friend's status). Earns the click on curiosity; the long-form copy or advertorial does the persuading. These are what scale on cold traffic; **the less it looks like an ad, the better it performs** (24_bonus-1-the-winning-statics-playbook [00:03:36]–[00:03:58]).
- Two indirect sub-strategies: **blend in** (camouflage as native content — personal post, confession, meme, ugly selfie; rawer = better) vs **stand out** (tabloid headline, weird circled inset, deliberately ugly graphic the brain must stop and process) (24_bonus-1-the-winning-statics-playbook [00:04:03]–[00:04:44]).

## 3. The 5 go-to formats (most → least direct)
(Source for all anatomy: 24_bonus-1-the-winning-statics-playbook [00:05:02]–[00:09:15] + text/24_65967688_...txt; visual examples per format in text/deck_visuals/24_..._pp01-12.md pp.4–7.)
1. **Product shot (DIRECT).** Product is the hero + one big benefit + one proof cue (rating/badge/guarantee) + clear CTA. Polished, links to product page. Use on warm/buyer-ready audiences; the retargeting workhorse ([00:05:18]–[00:05:39]).
2. **Social proof / testimonial (DIRECT).** Real-feeling customer face or review card: named person, star rating, quote in their words. Sells via borrowed trust; works in every niche. Variants: testimonial collage, single case study, or **anti-proof** (Liquid Death ran a screenshotted hate comment as a testimonial for ~2 years — lean contrarian only if the brand tolerates controversy) ([00:05:43]–[00:06:43]).
3. **Educational / infographic (mostly INDIRECT).** Teaches or proves something in the image itself — diagram, how-it-works, stat, clean comparison. Earns the click by giving value up front and pre-sells the mechanism. Litmus test: would the target screenshot it to read later? ([00:06:46]–[00:07:33]).
4. **Native static (INDIRECT, blend-in).** Anything that reads as regular content: candid phone photo, selfie, Notes-app screenshot, handwritten text, meme, wall-of-text confession. The image carries zero selling — **copy does 100% of the work**, so pair with long emotional first-person body copy and send to an advertorial ([00:07:47]–[00:08:18]). Compliance: never show literal before/after photos — Meta disapproves and can kill the account ([00:08:20]–[00:08:30]) ⚠️PLATFORM.
5. **Breaking news / tabloid (INDIRECT, stand-out) — his favorite.** Slightly odd primary image + smaller circled/arrowed inset + bold news-style headline bar (red accents, "BREAKING"). Two competing images stop the scroll because the brain tries to reconcile them ([00:08:34]–[00:09:15]). He attributes ~$3M of his own revenue to this style via Instagram-shoutout-format ads ([00:09:36]–[00:09:45]).

**Tabloid "stop the scroll" test — all 3 required** (24_bonus-1-the-winning-statics-playbook [00:10:04]–[00:11:13]; text/24_65967688_...txt):
1. Pattern interrupt in the image (often the circled/arrowed inset).
2. Burning intrigue in the headline — an open loop the brain must close.
3. A targeted big benefit (explicit or implied) aimed squarely at YOUR market so the algorithm knows who to find.
Failure modes: pure intrigue with no benefit = lots of clicks, zero buyers; pure benefit with no intrigue = ignored. Write at the intersection ([00:11:41]–[00:11:49]). Clickbait is fine **iff the landing page is congruent with the ad** ([00:11:53]–[00:12:03]).
Objection kill: "my market is above tabloid ads" — no; news/drama dopamine is universal from fry cook to CEO; don't fight human psychology, lean in ([00:12:20]–[00:12:45]).
Idea source: Meta's quarterly **Widely Viewed Content Report** — top-engaged pages are all news/tabloid (NY Post, TMZ, Unilad…). Model those pages + big IG shoutout pages for tabloid creative ([00:12:55]–[00:14:04]) ⚠️PLATFORM.

## 4. Funnel routing (where the click goes)
- Direct formats (product shot, testimonial) → straight to product page/checkout. Don't add steps for buyers ([00:15:56]–[00:16:05]).
- Indirect formats → **advertorial first** (the "57-year-old from Norway reveals…" article pattern; listicles like "5 Reasons Women Over 35 Call This Anti-Wrinkle Pillow Their Anti-Aging Secret" are one sub-type). The advertorial educates/warms, then links to product ([00:16:20]–[00:17:33]; advertorial anatomy in text/deck_visuals/24_..._pp01-12.md p.10).

## 5. The "25 statics before lunch" production workflow
**Strategy split:** swipes usually win (you ride someone else's paid-for test data) but cap your ceiling; originals can scale further. Run **80% iterations of proven winners / 20% fresh concepts** (24_bonus-1-the-winning-statics-playbook [00:17:48]–[00:18:19]).
**Stack:** Claude for ideation + prompt-writing; Higgsfield → Nano Banana Pro for the actual images (Claude doesn't generate well) ([00:18:24]–[00:18:33]) ⚠️DATED (model landscape; see §7).

**Step 0 — Brief (non-negotiable).** Messaging beats format; skipping research = ads miss no matter how good the generation ([00:18:58]–[00:19:16]). With a full research/Intel report done, drop avatar + voice-of-customer + offer + angles docs into Claude and skip ahead ([00:18:50]). Cold-start/spec client: run his **Quick Brief prompt** — Claude extracts, facts-only, flagging gaps as [GUESS]: who it's for, awareness level, the pull, what we sell, the promise, what makes it different, why they'd believe it, numbers + next step, look & feel (text/24_65967688_...txt; [00:20:07]–[00:20:32]).

**Path A — Swipe a proven winner (80%):**
1. Find winners in Foreplay (ad-spy tool, ~$59/mo ⚠️DATED). Filter: image format · still live · running 30+ days · English · FB+IG; **sort by longest-running** — long runtime is the closest thing to a "this works" stamp (no impression sort available) ([00:20:56]–[00:21:22]) ⚠️PLATFORM. Budget alternates: GetHookd, free Meta/TikTok Ad Libraries ([00:21:22]).
2. Reverse-engineer in Claude with the **Swipe Teardown + Rebuild prompt** — front-load brand docs/brief, paste the winner, run 3 passes: PASS 1 why it wins (eye-path, hook/promise/action, awareness level, emotional button); PASS 2 rebuild for your brand (keep the converting structure/psychology, swap product/offer/voice/colors); PASS 3 one copy-paste image-gen prompt specifying format lane, what it should "pass as," scene, top-to-bottom layout + product placement, brand colors, framing/lighting, exact on-image words in quotes — rewritten for YOUR offer — plus 2 alternate test headlines. Build 4:5 with face/product/headline/CTA center-safe for a square crop (text/24_65967688_...txt; [00:21:30]–[00:21:55]).
- Pro move: also run the teardown on **your own past winners** to learn why they won ([00:38:46]–[00:38:55]).

**Path B — From scratch (20%):** run the **Concept Engine prompt** — "give me 25 fresh static-ad concepts," grounded in customer language, each genuinely different (vary angle, emotion, awareness level — not just visual format), output exactly 3 parts per concept: FORMAT (one of the 5 lanes) / HOOK (the exact on-image words — concrete, a number or named thing, feed-glance short) / PROMPT (copy-paste, 4:5, text center-safe). "Twenty more" continues without repeats (text/24_65967688_...txt; [00:22:00]–[00:22:21]).

**Generate (Higgsfield → Nano Banana Pro)** ([00:22:37]–[00:24:57]) ⚠️DATED tooling:
- Settings: 4:5 portrait (1:1 is outdated — feeds have gone vertical ⚠️PLATFORM), 2K quality, 4 generations per prompt ([00:22:44]–[00:23:16]).
- Product shots: upload a clean background-stripped PNG of the real product (Adobe Express bg remover) so the model composites the actual product ([00:24:03]–[00:24:15]).
- Why Higgsfield over plain Gemini: batch gens, no watermark, all models in one place — speed/flow beats the small markup ([00:24:29]–[00:24:45]). Images only: Higgsfield is "a total ripoff" for video — use fal.ai raw API pricing for video gen ([00:25:12]–[00:25:30]) ⚠️DATED.
- Nano Banana 2 vs Pro: NB2 is 2–3× faster at same credit cost — use it to rapid-test concepts; switch to Pro for the final ad (noticeably higher quality; don't nickel-and-dime an ad you'll put $1000s of spend behind) ([00:23:19]–[00:23:50], [00:36:38]–[00:37:08]) ⚠️DATED.
- Edit discipline: glitchy gen → regenerate or feed back "fix the text to read exactly: …". If a regen doesn't fix it on the **first** retry it never will — regenerate from scratch instead of looping ([00:24:53]–[00:25:11]). Use inpainting to surgically swap a headline while keeping the image ([00:34:39]–[00:34:55]). Quality degrades with repeated full regenerations — stop before ~10 iterations ([00:35:51]–[00:36:02]). Upscale 2× for final/print quality ([00:36:27]).
- Face-locked composites (his demo hero ad): one real selfie + a prompt that opens with a non-negotiable face-fidelity block (pixel-level likeness, no smoothing/beautifying, treat as background-replacement around an untouched subject), exact on-image headline in quotes, text in safe area (text/24_65967688_...txt §Demo 1; [00:27:54]–[00:28:59]).

**QA gate before launch** ([00:25:37]–[00:26:24]):
- On-image text legible and correctly spelled (AI still fumbles text).
- On-brand; product rendering accurate (founders care).
- Compliance pass — esp. no literal before/after transformations (imply or animate instead); do Meta's free compliance course once ⚠️PLATFORM.
- Never push raw AI output straight to Ads Manager.

## 6. The 5-Ad Sprint (operator drill, 60 min hard cap)
Pick 5 hooks from your hook bank / best video scripts / swipes → Foreplay swipe board of 10–20 long-running winners in niche → brand docs into Claude → teardown 3–5 winners → generate **one of each of the 5 formats** (4 variations each, pick best) → label by format + hook (e.g. "NEWS 3 – AI Clients") → allocate: cold traffic gets the 3 indirect formats (infographic, breaking news, native); warm/retargeting gets product shots + testimonials → for every native static, draft a one-sentence matching advertorial angle (+ its headline) (24_bonus-1-the-winning-statics-playbook [00:38:11]–[00:39:43]; text/24_65967688_...txt). Operating creed: you don't need perfect — you need **volume of good-enough images that sell the click**; the dashboard picks winners ([00:41:10]–[00:41:17]).
