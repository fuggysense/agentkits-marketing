# Copywriter — Learnings & Patterns

> This file updates continuously. Add confirmed patterns, insights, and fixes discovered during use.

## Confirmed Patterns
- Ad creative quality is the main lever for lowering effective cost on Meta (CPM model)
- Hook must stop scroll in 3 seconds (video ads) or instantly (image ads)
- CTR < 1% = creative/headline problem — test new variations
- Frequency > 2-3 = creative fatigue — need fresh assets
- High CPM signals low engagement with current creative

## Diagnostic Rules
- Low 3-second views → Weak hook/opening — rewrite first 3 seconds
- Low ThruPlay (15s+) → Content not engaging enough after hook
- CTR below 1% → Test new headline and primary text
- Ad fatigue (frequency > 2-3) → Create new creative variations, same message

## Mistakes Not to Repeat
- Using AI-tell words: delve, comprehensive, robust, leverage, crucial, streamline, navigate, unlock, unleash
- Opening with generic phrases: "In today's fast-paced world..." / "Let's dive in!" / "Are you ready to..."
- Writing from feature level instead of 3-levels-deep emotional/financial payoff (So What? Chain)
- Same-length paragraphs, no rhythm variation — reads like a textbook
- Curiosity-only headlines without benefit = clickbait that disappoints

## Headline Archetype Selection — Universal Pattern (added 2026-04-23)

**Applies across verticals.** The emotional/psychological framework is industry-agnostic. Whenever a buyer is stuck in a decision loop — alone or with another party — the same three archetypes recur and the same selection rule applies. Works for real estate, SaaS, coaching, health, finance, legal, wedding, fertility, education, B2B services.

**When presenting 3 headline options, tag each by archetype. Never present 3 rewordings of the same archetype — always offer different tools.**

### The three archetypes

1. **Sharp / Specific (A)** — verbatim scene language + exact time or place + blunt promise.
   - Strength: stopping power, specificity, instant recognition.
   - Weakness: clunky rhythm, can feel manufactured, sometimes "salesy" at the close.
   - Route to: cold scroll-stop ad, retargeting, short-form, single-decision-maker buyer.

2. **Scene + Relational Validation + Mechanism Image (B)** — builds a two-party micro-scene the reader recognizes instantly, validates BOTH parties ("neither of you is wrong, both of you are stuck" / equivalent), ends with a mechanism image that implies closure ("closes the tab" / "ends the loop" / "stops the Googling" / "closes the bookmarks folder" / "ends the demo marathon" / "ends the Pinterest spiral").
   - Strength: emotional realism, removes blame, peer-to-peer feel, persuasion > cleverness.
   - Weakness: longer and slower — not for scroll-stop.
   - Route to: long-form sales letter, warm traffic, any buyer stuck in repeated real-life conversations with another party — spouse, co-founder, business partner, committee, board, advisor, parent, team.

3. **Diagnostic / Premium (C)** — tells the reader what they actually need, analytically. Implies expert authority.
   - Strength: cleaner, more premium, rewards the reader's intelligence.
   - Weakness: emotionally thin, does not dramatize the lived moment, logic-first.
   - Route to: cold high-sophistication analytical buyers, B2B consulting, solo-decision buyer whose identity is "the careful one" or "the smart one."

### Universal decision rule

When buying emotion is **"I want this mental burden off my shoulders"** (decision fatigue, analysis paralysis, repeated-conversation pattern), **B > A > C every time — across every vertical.**

Three reasons the rule is industry-agnostic:
1. Buyer is not purchasing logic first. They are purchasing **relief from uncertainty** + (if applicable) **permission for the relationship**.
2. Taking sides between two parties creates resistance. Validating both + naming indecision as the enemy removes resistance. Applies equally to spouses buying property, co-founders choosing a vendor, boards approving budget, or a solopreneur arguing with their own past self.
3. The reader is trapped in a loop. Selling more information extends the loop. Selling the end of the loop (mechanism image → terminal action) breaks it.

### Mechanism image library (verticals → closing phrases)

- Home / big-ticket purchase: "closes the tab" / "ends the 11pm Excel loop"
- SaaS vendor selection: "ends the demo marathon" / "closes the Notion doc"
- Coaching / consulting: "ends the free-webinar loop" / "closes the bookmarks folder"
- Health / fertility: "stops the Googling" / "replaces the 14 browser tabs with one answer"
- Legal / financial: "ends the 'let me think about it'" / "finally lets you sleep"
- Wedding / one-time purchase: "ends the Pinterest spiral"
- B2B committee buying: "ends the 'let me loop in one more stakeholder'"

All do the same job: translate abstract uncertainty into concrete closure.

### First observed

2026-04-23, real-estate vertical. Founder selected B over A and C because B created a lived-in scene, validated both parties, and ended with a mechanism image ("closes the tab") that translated abstract uncertainty into terminal action. A was sharp but clunky. C was elegant but emotionally abstract. Pattern is vertical-agnostic — next 2-3 live selections across other industries should confirm.
