# Onboarding Intake — Meridian Property Advisory

> **FICTIONAL SMOKE-TEST DATA - not a real client.** Invented for the regression baseline.
> 21-question intake style. Captured 2026-06-11. Founder: Daniel Tay (fictional).

## Section 1 — Business basics

**1. What's the business and what does it sell?**
Meridian Property Advisory. We help Singapore residential buyers pick the right unit using data, then we hold their hand through to signing the Option to Purchase. We charge one flat fee. We don't take commission from the seller or the developer, so we have no reason to push any particular unit.

**2. What's the core paid offer and the price?**
The Property Purchase Advisory. S$4,500, flat. Covers the full buy: shortlist review, unit-by-unit data teardown, viewings prioritised by the numbers, negotiation strategy, and OTP sign-off. Most clients close in 6 to 10 weeks with us.

**3. What's the one-line promise?**
Data-backed unit selection. You buy the right unit at the right price, and you can see exactly why on a spreadsheet, not on a feeling.

**4. Who's the founder and what's the short story?**
Daniel Tay. He spent nine years as a buy-side analyst before this, the kind of work where you get fired for a hunch and paid for a model. He bought his own first place in 2019, a resale HDB in the east, and watched a commissioned agent steer him toward a unit that paid the agent more and helped Daniel less. He walked. He bought a different unit off his own analysis. Three years later a near-identical unit two floors up sold for S$71,000 less than he'd been quietly told it was "worth." That gap is the whole company. Friends started asking him to "just run the numbers" on their shortlists. He did it free for two years. When the fifth person tried to pay him, he built Meridian.

## Section 2 — Offer and ladder

**5. Front-end / lead magnet?**
The "Overpaying Checklist" — 9 unit red flags that quietly cost buyers S$30k or more. Planned, not built yet.

**6. Mid-funnel offer?**
The S$290 "Shortlist Teardown" — a 90-minute call where Daniel runs the buyer's existing shortlist through the model and gives a verdict on each unit. Low-risk way to feel the method before the full advisory.

**7. Back-end / main offer?**
The S$4,500 flat-fee advisory (see Q2).

**8. What's the guarantee or risk reversal?**
If after the Shortlist Teardown the buyer doesn't think the method is worth it, the S$290 comes off the full advisory fee if they upgrade, or they walk and owe nothing more. The full advisory itself isn't outcome-guaranteed — Daniel won't promise a price he doesn't control.

## Section 3 — Market and buyer

**9. Who's the ideal buyer?**
HDB upgraders moving to their second property, and cautious first-timers buying resale. Household income roughly S$8k to S$18k a month. Numbers-comfortable but not property-fluent. They've already been burned or scared by an agent.

**10. What's the buyer's main pain?**
They can't tell whether the agent is on their side. Every "advice" comes from someone paid more when they spend more. So they either over-research alone until they're paralysed, or they trust a stranger and lie awake wondering if they overpaid.

**11. What does the buyer secretly fear?**
Signing for the wrong unit and being stuck with it for a decade. In Singapore the cost to undo a property mistake is brutal — stamp duties, the resale levy, the time. One bad OTP can set a family back years.

**12. What's the buyer's desired outcome?**
To sign and actually sleep. To know they paid a fair number for a unit that holds value, and to be able to explain to their spouse exactly why, with evidence.

**13. What have they tried before?**
PropertyGuru and 99.co for hours. Asking in HDB and condo Telegram groups. One or two commissioned agents. A cousin "in the industry." None of it removed the core doubt: is this person on my side or theirs?

## Section 4 — Competition and positioning

**14. Who are the main competitors?**
Commissioned estate agents (the default), the big portals' "advisory" arms, and a couple of so-called buyer's agents who still quietly take referral splits. See `competitor-notes-260611.md`.

**15. What makes Meridian different?**
The flat fee. No commission, no referral split, no developer kickback. The incentive is clean: Daniel gets paid the same whether you buy a S$600k flat or a S$1.4m condo, so his only job is being right.

**16. What do you NOT want to be associated with?**
The hard-sell agent vibe. The "I have a hot unit, decide tonight" pressure. We are the opposite of urgency theatre.

## Section 5 — Funnel and assets

**17. Current funnel?**
Pure referral. Stranger hears about Daniel from a past client, WhatsApps him, books the advisory. No paid ads, no email list, no first-touch capture. (This is the fragility — one quiet referral month and the pipeline is dry.)

**18. Email list / provider?**
None.

**19. What assets exist?**
Daniel can film himself talking to camera. There's a rough deck of anonymised past teardowns. No website copy worth keeping, no ads, no lead magnet built.

## Section 6 — Goals

**20. 90-day goal?**
Stand up the first paid-acquisition funnel: lead magnet → Shortlist Teardown → full advisory. Get to a point where new clients arrive without a referral.

**21. What does "done" look like for this engagement?**
A working funnel and the messaging that makes the flat-fee model obvious to a fee-shy, agent-burned buyer in under thirty seconds.
