**Persona (working):** The agent-burned Singapore home buyer.
**Who they are:** HDB upgraders and cautious resale first-timers. Household income ~S$8k-S$18k/month. Numbers-comfortable but not property-fluent. Already scared or burned by a commissioned agent.

## THE CORE PROBLEM
- **Emotional:** They can't tell whose side the advice is on. Every "expert" earns more when they spend more, so no advice feels safe.
- **Situational:** About to make a 7-figure, decade-long decision with no neutral party in the room.

## TOP EMOTIONS (from VoC dump)
1. Distrust — "is this person on my side or theirs?"
2. Fear of overpaying — can't judge fair value.
3. Paralysis — over-researched, can't decide.
4. Resentment toward fake urgency / hard-sell tactics.
5. Quiet shame — "I feel unqualified to make this choice."

## BIGGEST FEARS
1. Signing for the wrong unit and being stuck for 10 years (costly to undo in SG).
2. Overpaying by tens of thousands and only realising later.
3. Being manipulated by someone with a hidden incentive.

## PAST SOLUTIONS TRIED
1. PropertyGuru / 99.co for months.
2. HDB and condo Telegram groups (conflicting anonymous opinions).
3. One or two commissioned agents (the source of the burn).

## WHAT THEY WANT
- Someone contractually on THEIR side, paid the same regardless of what they spend.
- A verdict backed by numbers they can see, with no pressure to decide tonight.
- To sign and actually sleep.

## BUYER-TRUTH LINE (from VoC)
> "Fix the incentive and I'm in." The objection isn't the fee — it's whether the advisor is genuinely on their side.

## SCHWARTZ AWARENESS (primary, one-buyer)
- **Primary level:** Problem-aware, edging into Solution-aware. They KNOW the conflict-of-interest problem. Most don't yet know a true flat-fee buyer-advisory exists as a category.
- Implication: lead with the problem (the rigged incentive), then introduce the flat-fee model as the named solution. Don't open product-aware.

### Micro-Persona 1: The Repeat-Mistake Upgrader

> The HDB upgrader who already overpaid once, is buying their second home, and has sworn it won't happen again — this time on data, not an agent's word.

| Field | Value |
|-------|-------|
| Motivation | Avoid repeating a costly mistake. Prove to themselves (and their spouse) they learned. |
| Pain | The same agent who sold their flat fast goes cold on the buy side, and they can feel the incentive flip. |
| Desired Outcome | Buy the second unit with numbers they can defend, and finally feel competent instead of lucky. |
| Lifestyle / Context | Dual-income family, selling a 4-room and trading up. Models the rest of their life on spreadsheets and resents that the biggest purchase runs on gut. Often mid-30s to mid-40s with kids and a renovation budget on the line. |
| Buying Trigger | Their flat sells, the clock starts on the next purchase, and the agent's attention visibly drops. |
| Awareness Level | Solution-aware. Knows the conflict, actively wants a different model, may not know flat-fee advisory exists by name. |
| Sophistication Level | 4 |
| Language | "This time I want data not vibes." "Less money for them on the buy, makes sense." "We overpaid maybe 40-50k looking back. Not making that mistake twice." |
| Proof | The visible spreadsheet. Anonymised before/after teardowns. A named fair-value gap they can check themselves. Not credentials, not testimonials alone. |
| What Makes This Segment Distinct | They carry a specific scar (a past overpay) and a specific trigger (the buy-side incentive flip). That history makes them the fastest to grasp the flat-fee pitch and the hardest to fool with polish. |
| Source Confidence | medium — strong VoC signal (quotes 1, 3, 7, 21), but all fictional and inferred. |

#### Psychology

- **Primary emotion:** Resentment, tipping into resolve. They've been burned and they're done being passive.
- **Primary fear:** Doing it again. A second decade-long mistake would confirm the first wasn't bad luck.
- **Beliefs to overcome:** "All advice is tainted by commission." "I should be able to figure this out myself if I'm a numbers person." Counter: a clean incentive plus a second set of eyes is not weakness, it's the move a careful person makes.
- **Past solutions tried:** Self-analysing URA caveats (didn't trust their own answer), the same dual-role agent (felt the buy-side drop-off).
- **Raw inner dialogue:** "I can model my whole life on a spreadsheet. Why am I buying a house on a feeling and an agent's word?"
- **Relationship impact:** Tension with a spouse who still trusts the agent. Wants a neutral third party to break the tie without a fight. (Folded-in couple signal.)

#### Market Behavior

- **Similar products considered:** PropData Pro (data tool), the dual-role commissioned agent, DIY caveat analysis.
- **Why those fell short:** The tool gave data but no verdict and no accountability. The agent's incentive flipped on the buy. DIY left them with a number they couldn't trust.
- **Messages seen before:** "Your dream home is closer than you think." "Make data-driven property decisions." Aspiration and dashboards.
- **Messages rejected:** Drone-shot aspiration, "decide this weekend" urgency, "trust me I'm experienced" with no visible work.
- **What still breaks through at the buyer-truth level:** A concrete, checkable number that names what a mistake costs, paired with the flat-fee incentive stated plainly. Evidence over reassurance.
- **Evidence sources:** VoC quotes 1, 3, 7, 21; market stats 2, 4; onboarding Q9-Q11; competitor notes (SkylineEdge, PropData Pro).

### Micro-Persona 2: The Paralysed First-Timer

> The cautious first-time resale buyer who has researched for months, saved dozens of listings, and still can't pull the trigger because they don't trust their own judgment.

| Field | Value |
|-------|-------|
| Motivation | Stop drowning in options and make one decision they won't regret. |
| Pain | They've done the homework and it made things worse — more data, more doubt, zero conviction. |
| Desired Outcome | A clear verdict from someone who isn't selling, so they can sign and stop second-guessing. |
| Lifestyle / Context | Late 20s to mid-30s, often a couple buying their first home. PropertyGuru open for months, dozens of saved listings, three Telegram groups giving three different answers. Time-poor, decision-fatigued. |
| Buying Trigger | A lease or rental ending, a wedding, a baby on the way — a life event that turns "someday" into "this quarter." |
| Awareness Level | Problem-aware. Feels the doubt acutely, hasn't yet framed it as an incentive problem or found the flat-fee category. |
| Sophistication Level | 2 |
| Language | "28 saved listings, zero decisions, send help." "Three Telegram groups, three opinions, all anonymous." "I feel completely unqualified to choose." |
| Proof | A simple, human verdict over raw data. Plain-language reasoning. A low-risk first step (the S$290 teardown) so they can feel the method before committing. |
| What Makes This Segment Distinct | Their block isn't trust in agents (yet) — it's trust in themselves. They want a conclusion, not more inputs. That makes the "verdict, not a dashboard" angle land hardest here. |
| Source Confidence | medium — clear VoC signal (quotes 9, 11, 13), all fictional. |

#### Psychology

- **Primary emotion:** Anxiety and quiet shame. They feel they should be able to figure this out and can't.
- **Primary fear:** Signing the OTP and discovering six months later it was the wrong unit, now stuck for a decade.
- **Beliefs to overcome:** "More research will eventually give me certainty." "Paying for advice means I failed to do it myself." Counter: the research was never going to produce a verdict — a person who isn't selling will.
- **Past solutions tried:** Months on PropertyGuru and 99.co, anonymous Telegram crowdsourcing, asking a cousin "in the industry."
- **Raw inner dialogue:** "I'm about to make the biggest purchase of my life on basically a feeling."
- **Relationship impact:** Often deciding as a couple where neither partner wants to be the one who pushed the wrong unit. (Folded-in couple signal.)

#### Market Behavior

- **Similar products considered:** Property portals, Telegram groups, PropData Pro.
- **Why those fell short:** All gave more inputs and no conclusion, which deepened the paralysis instead of ending it.
- **Messages seen before:** "Make data-driven property decisions." Endless listing feeds. "Trust your agent."
- **Messages rejected:** Anything that adds homework. Intimidating analytical tools. Anonymous crowd opinions.
- **What still breaks through at the buyer-truth level:** The promise of a verdict and a calm, no-pressure first step. "Show me which one and let me sleep," not "here's more data."
- **Evidence sources:** VoC quotes 5, 9, 11, 13, 24, 27; market stat 3; onboarding Q10, Q12; competitor notes (PropData Pro).

### Micro-Persona 3: The Fee-Allergic Convertible

> The ROI-driven skeptic who scoffs at paying for "free" advice — until the hidden commission cost is made visible, at which point the math flips them fast.

| Field | Value |
|-------|-------|
| Motivation | Not overpay — on the unit, and on the advice itself. Refuses to be the sucker. |
| Pain | They suspect the "free" agent isn't free but can't see the cost, so they can't price the trade-off. |
| Desired Outcome | A trade they can prove on a calculator: S$4,500 flat beats a hidden S$12,000 commission. |
| Lifestyle / Context | Financially literate, transactional, probably reads r/singaporefi. Optimises fees on everything — index funds, insurance, credit cards. Treats this as one more cost-benefit problem to solve. |
| Buying Trigger | Seeing the hidden-commission math laid out, or a peer telling them what they actually paid an agent. |
| Awareness Level | Solution-aware, edging to Product-aware once shown the flat-fee model. Sharpest of the three. |
| Sophistication Level | 4 |
| Language | "Pay 4.5k for advice when agents are free? No thanks. (But also I have no idea if my free agent is screwing me.)" "4.5k flat is nothing if it stops me overpaying 50k. The math is obvious." "Show me the spreadsheet, not a sales pitch." |
| Proof | The hidden-commission breakdown. The visible analysis up front. A clear before-you-pay sample of the method. Anti-hype tone. |
| What Makes This Segment Distinct | They're not blocked by emotion or paralysis — they're blocked by a pricing illusion. Make the real cost visible and they convert almost on contact. The objection is logical, not psychological. |
| Source Confidence | medium — direct VoC signal (quotes 15, 16, 17, 18, 26), all fictional. |

#### Psychology

- **Primary emotion:** Suspicion, then satisfaction when the math checks out.
- **Primary fear:** Being quietly fleeced and not knowing it. The unseen cost is worse than a visible one.
- **Beliefs to overcome:** "Free is cheaper than S$4,500." "Anyone charging for advice is just another salesperson." Counter: free isn't free, the commission is baked into the price, and a flat fee is the only structure where the advisor doesn't profit from you spending more.
- **Past solutions tried:** Used "free" agents, later worked out the commission was in the price. Looked at data tools to self-serve.
- **Raw inner dialogue:** "The whole game is rigged on the incentive. Fix the incentive and I'm in."
- **Relationship impact:** Lower than the other two — this buyer decides on logic and is less swayed by family pressure.

#### Market Behavior

- **Similar products considered:** "Free" commissioned agents, PropData Pro, The Honest Agent SG.
- **Why those fell short:** The free agent had the hidden cost. The data tool had no verdict. The "honest agent" still took a referral split, which to this buyer is a worse lie than charging openly.
- **Messages seen before:** "Free consultation." "No obligation." Founder rants attacking the industry.
- **Messages rejected:** "Free" (they know it isn't). Outrage marketing. Credentials with no visible work.
- **What still breaks through at the buyer-truth level:** Making the hidden cost visible and contrasting the fee structure — not the people. Numbers they can re-run themselves.
- **Evidence sources:** VoC quotes 4, 12, 15, 16, 17, 18, 25, 26; market stats 6, 7; onboarding Q15; competitor notes (all three).
