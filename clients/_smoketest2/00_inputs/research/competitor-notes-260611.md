## Competitor 1: Athletic Greens / AG1 (category incumbent)

**Model:** premium all-in-one greens powder, $79/mo subscription. Strong ambassador/influencer program.

**Positioning:** "One scoop replaces your entire supplement routine." Premium, aspirational, lifestyle-led.

**What they get right:** brand trust through relentless consistency, doctor/athlete endorsements, strong podcast advertising. Buyers know the name.

**Where they fail (buyer language):**
- Proprietary blends — buyers can't audit what they're getting at what dose. Common r/supplements complaint: "It could be anything."
- $79/mo is hard to justify without measurable results. Cancellation friction.
- Does everything = not optimized for anything. A sleep-specific buyer gets no confidence this addresses their specific issue.
- Taste objections run consistent across Amazon reviews ("tolerable if you hold your nose").

**Key VOC pattern:** "I don't want one product that does everything. I want the right product for my actual problem."

**Implication for VitalKit:** category-specific positioning is a real gap. "The starter kit for sleep" beats "the kit that replaces everything."

## Competitor 2: Ritual (multi-vitamin subscription)

**Model:** subscription multi, $35-45/mo. Women-first branding, delayed-release capsule innovation.

**Positioning:** "Clean, traceable ingredients. No unnecessary additives." Transparency play.

**What they get right:** ingredient transparency (they show sources), gender-specific formulas, strong packaging aesthetic. Appeals to educated buyers.

**Where they fail:**
- Multi-vitamin framing means they never speak to the specific problem (sleep, stress, energy). Every ad is generic wellness.
- No starter/trial option. Subscribe or leave.
- Buyers who come for "sleep support" leave confused — a multi-vitamin doesn't address sleep specifically.

**Key VOC pattern:** "I like the idea of Ritual but I don't need a multi — I need something for my specific issue."

## Competitor 3: Amazon private-label (magnesium/ashwagandha)

**Model:** $8-15 one-time purchase, no brand story, reviews-dependent.

**Positioning:** essentially none. Race to the bottom on price and review count.

**What they get right:** low-friction trial, no subscription commitment. Buyers buy these first.

**Where they fail:** no guidance on dose, form, timing, or expected outcome. Buyers don't know if it's working. High return-to-graveyard rate.

**Key VOC pattern:** "I tried magnesium from Amazon and I don't know if it worked. There were three types and I picked the cheapest one."

**Implication for VitalKit:** VitalKit wins by being the step above Amazon — guided, explained, curated — without the AG1 price and all-in-one overreach.
