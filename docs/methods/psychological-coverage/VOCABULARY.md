## Why bother (30 sec)

**Meta is quietly raising your costs when your ads feel the same.** Its algorithm (Andromeda) now flags "Creative Similarity" — ten ads with different hooks but the same emotional message get treated as one ad, shown to the same people, at a higher price.

So the win isn't "make more ads." It's "make ads that genuinely feel different to different people." To do that on purpose instead of by luck, we need a shared map of the *kinds of feeling* an ad can run on. Two new ideas below. One thing we're explicitly NOT adding.

---

## New idea #1 — Emotional Zone

Two sliders on every ad:

- **Good feeling or bad?** (hope, pride, relief … vs anxiety, anger, embarrassment)
- **Loud or quiet?** (urgent, alarmed … vs calm, reassuring, slow-burn)

Cross them → four zones. On a phone, just read them as four lines:

- **Alarm** (loud + bad) — "you're doing it wrong, act now." → where **most brands over-live.**
- **Big Aspiration** (loud + good) — "imagine the life six months from now."
- **Calm Hope** (quiet + good) — "you've got this, here's the quiet path." → **usually missing.**
- **Quiet Doubt** (quiet + bad) — "something here isn't adding up…" slow-burn. → **almost always missing.**

The job: don't pile a whole wave into Alarm. Spread across zones → reach people the loud-negative ads never touch → Meta drops the redundancy tax.

**What you already have:** your copy already swings from Pain (bad) to Hope (good) inside one ad, and big-angle-spotter already tags every angle with an emotion. So **the only genuinely new thing is one new dial: loud vs quiet.** We are not re-labelling feelings you already track.

---

## New idea #2 — Who the ad makes them feel like

Every ad quietly casts the reader as a version of themselves:

- **Who they are now** (the mirror) — "stressed parent who can't find the time." → you do this constantly (your L4 "their exact situation" hooks).
- **Who they want to be** (aspiration) — "picture the calmer, sorted you." → you do this (your desire-state copy).
- **Who they feel they should be — for someone else** (duty) — "do it for your kids, not for you." → **this is the gap.**

Here's the honest version: the *raw material* for that third one already sits in your avatars (Eugene's avatar-1 literally says "a legacy I'd like to leave for our kids"). What you almost never do is **build a whole ad angle around it.** None of avatar-1's go-to angle types use it. So the gap isn't finding it — it's firing it. Cheap to test, and it opens an emotional door you mostly leave shut.

## Where this could go wrong (so we don't kid ourselves)

- **The map is a prompt to think, not a quota.** Chasing all four zones just to fill the grid — when two genuinely don't fit your buyer — makes creative worse. Cover white space only where the territory is worth occupying.

- **It only pays off if you use the signal.** Glance at the gap and approve anyway every time, and we've built a chart that does nothing.
