## Clustering methodology & data quirks

1. **Classifier agreement:** Nemotron (56 tags, short phrases) vs Gemini (37 tags, long sentences) mapped to identical underlying clusters 85% of the time. Semantic coherence across models validates taxonomy robustness.

2. **Overlap resolution:** Data-driven (31), URA (14), upgrade (8), and risk-mitigation (7) overlap semantically. Hierarchical assignment prioritized most specific promise:
   - "Data-backed property decisions" → data-driven
   - "Leverage URA plans + data" → URA (infrastructure timing primary)
   - "Upgrade research framework" → upgrade (life progression primary)

3. **Saturation concentration:**
   - **HIGH (>10):** data-driven (31), URA (14) — commoditized industry playbooks, avoid without strong differentiation
   - **MEDIUM (4-10):** upgrade (8), risk-mitigation (7), rental yield (4) — secondary levers with room for positioning
   - **LOW (1-3):** all others — white space for differentiation

4. **Buyer sophistication skew:** 53 of 93 ads target Stage 3 (aware, now optimizing). Only 1 Stage 1 (lease decay education). Market is upper-funnel saturated; entry-level angles underserved.

5. **Wealth dominance:** 78 of 93 ads (84%) lead with wealth desire. Safety (4), ease (8), status (2), mixed (1). Wealth-focused positioning locks out 16% of addressable buyers. **Recommendation: reserve 2-3 ease/safety angles for underserved buyer segments.**

6. **Missing clusters:** No strong "below-market deals" cluster despite expectation. Only 1 ad (~1% of sample). Possible explanations:
   - Agents avoid "discount" framing (brand risk: suggests desperation)
   - Platform filtering (Meta may suppress low-price messaging)
   - Genuinely low frequency in high-end property market

7. **Stage 1 insights:** Lease decay is only Stage 1 angle (1 ad, 1% coverage). Suggests education/awareness content severely underrepresented. Entry-level buyer education is white space.
