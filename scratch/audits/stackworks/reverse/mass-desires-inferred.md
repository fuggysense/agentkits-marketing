# Mass Desires (Inferred from letter — sandbox, requires approval)

**Source:** `scratch/audits/stackworks/copy/stackworks-letter.md`

The letter activates 4 mass desires:

---

### 1. Capital that performs without commanding more time, energy, or attention
**Confidence:** high
The letter's entire problem section is built on this. "The market runs 24 hours. You don't." "Your capital stays active. Your life stays yours." This is the dominant desire and it is named with precision.

### 2. Access to the institutional edge that was always gated from them
**Confidence:** high
"The edge that institutional desks have built and private investors were simply never given access to." "Now you can access the same system." The letter explicitly frames Stack as an insider asset newly opened up — status/access desire.

### 3. Freedom from emotional or fatigued execution decisions
**Confidence:** high
"Decisions made under fatigue are decisions made poorly." "Rules run. Not feelings." The decision-fatigue frame is heavy. This desire is dignity-flavored — the reader doesn't want to feel like an emotional amateur with their capital.

### 4. Confirmation that running capital like a system is the right answer (operator-identity validation)
**Confidence:** partial
"You run your business on systems. Your capital deserves the same." The letter assumes the reader is a systems-builder and the desire is to have that identity validated *and* extended to capital deployment. Less explicit than the other three, more inferred.

---

**Operator note:** Desires 1–3 are well-evidenced in the letter. Desire 4 is the inference that does most interpretive work — validate against sales calls before relying on it.
