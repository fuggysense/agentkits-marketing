# Angle Scoring Rubric

Score each angle on 5 dimensions (1-10 each). Total = sum / 5.

## Dimensions

### 1. Novelty (vs. Existing Ads)
How different is this angle from what the client is already running?

| Score | Criteria |
|-------|----------|
| 9-10 | Completely new territory — no existing ad touches this emotion, frame, or entry point |
| 7-8 | Different angle on a related theme — adjacent but clearly distinct |
| 5-6 | Same general territory but different framing or hook |
| 3-4 | Similar emotional territory — risks overlap with existing ads |
| 1-2 | Essentially a rewrite of an existing angle — reject |

### 2. Emotional Resonance
How deeply does this angle connect to the buyer's documented fears, emotions, and desires?

| Score | Criteria |
|-------|----------|
| 9-10 | Hits a documented fear/emotion at the visceral level — "that's literally me" response |
| 7-8 | Clearly connects to buyer psychology but needs the copy to land it |
| 5-6 | Relevant to the persona but doesn't hit a specific documented emotion |
| 3-4 | Tangentially related — could apply to any buyer, not this specific persona |
| 1-2 | Generic — no connection to the documented buyer psychology |

### 3. Clarity
Can someone understand the angle in under 3 seconds while scrolling?

| Score | Criteria |
|-------|----------|
| 9-10 | Instantly clear — one glance and you know what this is about |
| 7-8 | Clear with minimal context — takes 1-2 seconds to land |
| 5-6 | Needs a second read or supporting copy to make sense |
| 3-4 | Ambiguous — could be interpreted multiple ways |
| 1-2 | Confusing — requires explanation |

### 4. Scroll-Stop Potential
Would this angle make someone physically pause their thumb?

| Score | Criteria |
|-------|----------|
| 9-10 | Pattern interrupt — breaks expectations, creates an open loop, demands attention |
| 7-8 | Strong hook potential — curiosity gap or emotional trigger that pulls you in |
| 5-6 | Interesting but not arresting — they might read it if they're already engaged |
| 3-4 | Forgettable — blends into the feed |
| 1-2 | Invisible — the eye slides right past it |

### 5. Brand Fit
Does this angle align with the client's brand voice and positioning?

| Score | Criteria |
|-------|----------|
| 9-10 | Perfect fit — sounds like something the brand would naturally say |
| 7-8 | Fits with minor tone adjustment |
| 5-6 | Borderline — works but pushes the brand voice edges |
| 3-4 | Off-brand — would require significant repositioning |
| 1-2 | Contradicts brand values or positioning — reject |

## Score Bands

| Total Score | Band | Action |
|-------------|------|--------|
| 8.0-10.0 | Elite | Advance to headline generation |
| 6.5-7.9 | Strong | Include in top 10, potential for top 3 |
| 5.0-6.4 | Acceptable | Include only if higher-scoring angles are thin |
| Below 5.0 | Weak | Regenerate — do not present to user |

## Schwartz Coverage Check

After scoring all angles, verify:
- [ ] At least 3 different Schwartz awareness levels are represented
- [ ] No single awareness level has more than 4 angles
- [ ] The client's primary awareness levels have at least 2 angles each

If coverage fails, regenerate angles targeting underrepresented levels.

## Sophistication Alignment Check

After scoring, verify each angle's creative approach matches the avatar's sophistication level (see `references/sophistication-creative-map.md`):

- [ ] L4 avatars: Do angles lead with identification/mirror, NOT claims or mechanisms?
- [ ] L2 avatars: Do angles lead with claims or proof, NOT insider language?
- [ ] Split-sophistication avatars: Does the headline match the LOWER level while body copy matches the HIGHER level?
- [ ] No angle uses a creative approach meant for a LOWER sophistication level than the avatar's (talking down = instant rejection)

If alignment fails, restructure the angle's creative approach — the ANGLE may be right but the EXECUTION is pitched at the wrong level.
