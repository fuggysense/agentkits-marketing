# Awareness × Sophistication — quick lookup

Fast-lookup table for Schwartz's two compasses. Load this before any copy dispatch and state the call in one line: *"Treating as L2 / S3 — problem-led long-form, mechanism in section three."* Making the call visible makes it reviewable instead of buried in reasoning.

## PART 1 — The 5 Levels of Awareness

Where the reader actually is when they hit the page. The wrong lead for the wrong level loses them in the first paragraph.

| Level | Reader state | Lead with |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Unaware | Doesn't know they have a problem | Identity, story, or emotion — never the product |
| 2. Problem-Aware | Knows the pain, no solution category in mind | Name the problem vividly, introduce the category |
| 3. Solution-Aware | Knows solutions exist, comparing options | Your unique mechanism — why yours is different |
| 4. Product-Aware | Knows your product, weighing it | Differentiators, proof, offer, objections |
| 5. Most Aware | Already wants it, needs a push | Price, deal, urgency, availability |

**The length rule.** The less aware they are, the longer the copy needs to be. L1 cold traffic needs the full arc — hook, problem, agitate, credibility, mechanism, proof, offer. L5 needs three sentences and a button. Writing a long page for an L5 reader bores them past the buy. Writing a short page for an L1 reader skips the work that earns the ask.

## PART 2 — The 5 Levels of Market Sophistication

Where the *market* is, regardless of where the reader is. Determines what kind of claim still has any pull left in it.

| Stage | Market state | Approach |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | First-to-market | Plain claim works |
| 2 | Competitors emerging | Bigger claim |
| 3 | Claims exhausted, all sound alike | Unique mechanism |
| 4 | Mechanisms exhausted | New mechanism + bigger promise |
| 5 | Fully sophisticated, cynical | Identity, story, experience — not claims |

**The progression.** Stage 1 — *"lose weight."* Stage 5 — *"the way your grandmother ate."* Each stage burns out the move that worked one stage earlier. Trying to win a Stage 4 market with a Stage 1 plain claim looks naïve. Trying to win a Stage 1 market with a Stage 5 identity story looks pretentious. Match the stage.

## How to use both together

Awareness sets **length + lead type.** Sophistication sets **what kind of lead still works** at that length given how saturated the market is.

Examples:

- **L4 / S2** — *Product-aware reader, low-competition market.* Short page. Differentiators + offer. Plain claims still pull.
- **L2 / S4** — *Problem-aware reader, mechanism-exhausted market.* Long-form. Problem-led opening, mechanism-led middle, identity-led close. The reader needs the full arc and the market needs a new mechanism to believe.
- **L1 / S5** — *Unaware reader, fully cynical market.* Longest form. Story or identity lead, no product mention for paragraphs. Claims are dead here; only the world you build pulls them in.
- **L5 / S5** — *Most-aware reader, cynical market.* Three sentences. The work is done — give them the price, the deal, the button.
