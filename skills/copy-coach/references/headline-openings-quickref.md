# Headlines + openings — quick lookup

Positive pattern menu for the two highest-leverage lines on any page. Pair with `references/big-idea.md` (the headline carries the Big Idea — if the idea isn't there, no pattern saves the line) and `references/stylizing.md` (apply tug + cadence + register checks line-by-line before declaring any candidate done).

## PART 1 — Headline patterns

One headline can outpull another by **19.5x** on the same offer, same traffic, same page. The headline is the 80% lever. Write at least five candidates before picking one. The first one is almost never the winner — it's the version your brain reached for, not the version that earns the click.

| Pattern | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Action + outcome + contrast | *"[Action] [outcome], not [worse outcome]"* | *"Ship your startup in days, not weeks"* |
| Story (Caples) | *"They laughed when I [action]... but when I [result]"* | *"They laughed when I sat down at the piano — but when I started to play..."* |
| Specificity (Ogilvy) | *"[Specific number] + [unexpected detail]"* | *"At 60 miles an hour the loudest noise in this new Rolls-Royce comes from the electric clock"* |
| Question (Sherwin Cody) | *"Do you make these [number] mistakes in [domain]?"* | *"Do you make these mistakes in English?"* |
| Transformation | *"From [bad state] to [good state]"* | *"From overlooked to in-demand in 90 days"* |

**The write-five rule.** Force at least five candidates per page. Read all five aloud back to back. The winner is usually the one you'd be slightly embarrassed to say to a stranger — too specific, too direct, not safe enough.

**Fail modes.** Most weak headlines die from one of four things:

- **Clever over clear** — wordplay the reader has to decode. If they have to think for two seconds, they've already scrolled.
- **Vague claims** — *"transform your business,"* *"unlock your potential."* No specific, no proof, no pull.
- **No curiosity gap** — the headline answers itself. No reason to read line two.
- **No self-interest** — the headline is about the seller, not the reader. *"We do X"* instead of *"You get Y."*

## PART 2 — Opening patterns

The headline's job is to earn the open. The opening's job is to get them to read sentence two. Most pages die in the gap between the two.

| Pattern | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Direct challenge | Tell them they're wrong about something | *"You've been using LinkedIn wrong."* |
| Story scene | Drop them into a moment in motion | *"Last Tuesday, I opened my laptop and saw the message I'd been dreading for six months."* |
| Confession | Admit something the reader expects you to hide | *"I'll be honest — I almost gave up three times before this worked."* |
| Specific result | Open with the outcome, then explain | *"$127,394 in sales from one landing page. Here's what was on it."* |
| Short sentence (Sugarman) | One declarative line, then expand | *"It's simple."* / *"Here's the truth."* |

**Never open with.** These are the four openers that signal *"this is generic content"* in the first six words. Cut them on sight:

- *"In today's fast-paced world..."*
- *"Are you ready to [unlock / transform / take to the next level]..."*
- *"Let's dive in!"*
- *"In this article, you'll learn..."*

Any of these = rewrite. They're load-bearing in zero good pages and present in nearly every bad one.

## Headline → opening handoff

The headline makes a claim or opens a curiosity gap. The opening has 2–3 sentences to pay off — or the reader bounces with the loop still open. The handoff is where most pages break: a strong headline followed by a generic warm-up paragraph trains the reader to discount the next claim. Whatever the headline promised, the opening must immediately deliver, deepen, or twist. No throat-clearing. No *"so let me explain..."* — just go.

If the headline names a result, the opening shows the scene that produced it. If the headline asks a question, the opening answers it before paragraph two. If the headline makes a contrarian claim, the opening lands the first piece of proof. The two lines work as one move.
