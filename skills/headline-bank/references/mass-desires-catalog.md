# Mass Desires Catalog — primal drivers for headline anchoring

> Based on Cashvertising's LF8 + Halbert / Schwartz / Ogilvy on what actually moves buyers. Use this taxonomy when the user asks "which mass desire first?" and needs a menu.

Each mass desire has:
- **Name + synonyms** — what to call it
- **Core drive** — the primal underlying pull
- **Typical verbatim patterns** — how buyers talk when this desire is active
- **Headline anchor moves** — the language moves that activate it
- **Works best at awareness level** — where it lands hardest

---

## 1. Survival / Safety

**Synonyms:** security, protection, stability, peace of mind, avoiding loss.

**Core drive:** stay alive, keep family safe, avoid catastrophic downside.

**Verbatim patterns:**
- "I can't afford to get this wrong."
- "What if it all goes sideways?"
- "I need to protect my family."
- "I just want to know I'm not making a mistake."

**Headline anchor moves:**
- Loss-avoidance ("Don't lose X")
- Risk-removal ("No X. No Y. No trap.")
- Stability framing ("The one decision you won't regret.")

**Works best at:** Problem Aware, Solution Aware.

---

## 2. Health / Wellbeing

**Synonyms:** longevity, energy, pain relief, physical comfort, mental clarity.

**Core drive:** feel good in the body and mind, extend life, avoid suffering.

**Verbatim patterns:**
- "I just want to feel like myself again."
- "I'm tired of being tired."
- "My X has been off for months."

**Headline anchor moves:**
- Symptom-naming ("Still waking up at 3am?")
- Restoration framing ("Get your X back.")
- Specific relief claim ("The Xmg dose that actually works.")

**Works best at:** Problem Aware, Solution Aware.

---

## 3. Wealth / Money

**Synonyms:** income, savings, financial freedom, property, investments.

**Core drive:** accumulate resources, escape scarcity, build a moat for the future.

**Verbatim patterns:**
- "We don't earn enough."
- "Can we actually afford this?"
- "The numbers don't add up."
- "I keep running the math and I don't know."

**Headline anchor moves:**
- Reframe ("The number that actually decides.")
- Concrete math ("$X for Y.")
- Contrarian ("Your salary isn't what decides this.")

**Works best at:** Problem Aware, Solution Aware.

---

## 4. Ease / Time

**Synonyms:** convenience, shortcut, speed, hands-off, set-and-forget.

**Core drive:** conserve effort and time, avoid friction, automate the tedious.

**Verbatim patterns:**
- "I don't have time for this."
- "There has to be a faster way."
- "I just want it done."

**Headline anchor moves:**
- Time compression ("X in 2 minutes.")
- Shortcut framing ("Skip the X. Go straight to Y.")
- Hands-off claim ("We handle X so you don't have to.")

**Works best at:** Problem Aware, Solution Aware, Product Aware.

---

## 5. Status / Significance

**Synonyms:** prestige, recognition, being seen, moving up, being admired.

**Core drive:** be respected, be known as someone who made it, signal rank.

**Verbatim patterns:**
- "I want to finally feel like I've made it."
- "I don't want to be the one still renting while my cousins…"
- "I want people to stop asking me when."

**Headline anchor moves:**
- Identity upgrade ("The X people who skip the Y.")
- Comparison framing ("While others do X, you…")
- Arrival language ("You've outgrown Y. Time to X.")

**Works best at:** Solution Aware, Product Aware, Most Aware.

---

## 6. Freedom / Autonomy

**Synonyms:** independence, control, being your own boss, no rules.

**Core drive:** escape external control, make your own decisions, own your time.

**Verbatim patterns:**
- "I'm done being told what I can and can't do."
- "I want to call the shots."
- "Let me out of the box."

**Headline anchor moves:**
- Permission-granting ("You don't need X to do Y.")
- Escape framing ("Leave the X behind.")
- Control language ("Your X. Your rules.")

**Works best at:** Solution Aware, Product Aware.

---

## 7. Belonging / Connection

**Synonyms:** community, love, acceptance, being understood, being part of.

**Core drive:** be loved, be seen, belong to a tribe, feel understood.

**Verbatim patterns:**
- "I just want to feel heard."
- "Nobody gets it."
- "My partner and I keep fighting about this."

**Headline anchor moves:**
- Identification ("If you're the one who…")
- Peer-signal ("For the X who Y.")
- Relationship repair ("The conversation that ends the fight.")

**Works best at:** Problem Aware, Completely Unaware.

---

## 8. Curiosity / Knowing

**Synonyms:** answers, truth, understanding, finding out.

**Core drive:** close an information gap, know what's really going on, stop wondering.

**Verbatim patterns:**
- "I keep wondering if…"
- "Nobody actually explains…"
- "What's the real answer to…"

**Headline anchor moves:**
- Information gap ("The X nobody tells you about Y.")
- Specificity tease ("The 3 numbers that decide X.")
- Reveal framing ("Why Y actually fails (and what works).")

**Works best at:** Completely Unaware, Problem Aware.

---

## 9. Comfort / Pleasure

**Synonyms:** enjoyment, indulgence, reward, treating yourself.

**Core drive:** feel good now, enjoy the moment, escape discomfort.

**Verbatim patterns:**
- "I deserve this."
- "I just want to enjoy it without guilt."
- "Finally, something that feels good."

**Headline anchor moves:**
- Permission ("You earned this.")
- Sensory claim ("The X that actually feels Y.")
- Reward framing ("Treat your X like you mean it.")

**Works best at:** Product Aware, Most Aware.

---

## 10. Mastery / Growth

**Synonyms:** getting better, skill, progress, levelling up.

**Core drive:** become more capable, close the gap between who you are and who you want to be.

**Verbatim patterns:**
- "I want to actually be good at this."
- "I'm tired of feeling like a beginner."
- "Every time I try, I hit the same wall."

**Headline anchor moves:**
- Skill framing ("The X move that separates Y from Z.")
- Progress promise ("From X to Y in Z days.")
- Gap-naming ("Why your X keeps stalling.")

**Works best at:** Problem Aware, Solution Aware.

## How to pick when the user is unsure

1. Read `clients/<project>/source-of-truth.md` §5 (Buyer Profile) + §5.5 (Golden Nuggets) + §5.7 (ICP Language Analysis).
2. Read `clients/<project>/_brand/buyer-profile.md` — especially the Micro-Persona Map, Top Deep Fears, Raw Inner Dialogue, Desired Transformation, and Relationship Impact sections. Use legacy `clients/<project>/_brand/avatars/avatar-*.md` only when buyer-profile.md is missing or a legacy export is explicitly required.
3. Count which mass desire surfaces most often across the verbatim quotes. The one with the most evidence wins.
4. If there's a tie, the one with the most **specific, quotable, emotionally-loaded** verbatim wins (generic desire language is weak evidence).
5. Surface your top 3 candidates with one-sentence evidence per candidate. Let the user pick.
