# Selling Low-Ticket at a Profit (post-validation)

Source: georgeten.com/materials/bonus-low-ticket-profit.html
Funnel mechanics for $27–$97 products, AFTER validation.

## The expensive lie

"You must lose money on the front-end." False at low volume — a $27 product at $30/day spend can return ~2 profitable sales/day with no funnel. The funnel exists to protect profit when SCALING.

## The real problem — scaling pushes CPA up

| Spend | CPA | AOV $27 | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| $30/day | ~$15 | $27 | +$12/sale |
| $100/day | ~$22 | $27 | +$5/sale |
| $300/day | ~$30 | $27 | −$3/sale |

Meta finds the cheapest buyers first; wider spend = colder pockets = higher CPA. Build the funnel AS you scale, not before. Each piece raises AOV → absorbs higher CPA.

## Funnel pieces — in order

1. **Order bump ($9–$27)** — checkbox on checkout, card already out. Target 25–35% take rate. Must be the obvious companion (drawing course → reference photo pack $12; fitness ebook → printable tracker $9).
2. **One-click upsell ($27–$67)** — separate page right after purchase, card on file, single yes/no. Target 10–20% (drawing → "Vol 2 Advanced" $47; coding course → 5-project companion $67).
3. **Second bump or downsell ($9–$27)** — only later, and only if bump #1 holds ≥25%.

## Rules

1. Build pieces in order, never all at once — each needs its own profitability check.
2. Bump/upsell must be tightly related to the front-end. A relevant bump at 30% beats a generic one at 8%.

## Pricing the front-end (workshop guidance)

- Front-end as low as possible — $27 sweet spot. A $27 buyer and a $97 buyer are equally qualified for the back-end; $27 just buys more volume.
- SaaS/subscription pattern: sell a low-ticket front-end at the same price as month 1, then on the thank-you page: "you already paid for the first month — first month free, subscribe." (~30% subscription conversion reported.)
- >$100 offers: use a bait product (<$99) and upsell, or run content-led campaigns + retargeting instead of impulse direct response.
