# sales-letter-audit corrections

Skill-specific output corrections captured in real-time from operator feedback. Loaded at skill activation per `.claude/rules/skill-activation.md`. Applied as hard constraints on the next run — they represent explicit user preferences from prior sessions.

Format: `YYMMDD | what was wrong → what was right | context`

---

- 260507 | Named copywriters (Schwartz, Halbert, Ogilvy, Caples, etc.) appeared in client-facing brief → strip ALL names from any client-facing output. Audience is business owners, not copywriters. They don't know who Halbert is, don't care, and the references read as showing off | Pilot brief on StackWorks (Jason) — user flagged after first run
- 260507 | "How strong copywriters handle this" / "what good copywriters do" framing in client-facing brief → drop entirely. Replace with mechanical principle language: "What converts instead is...", "The reason this matters is...", "Letters that earn the click do this differently." Describe WHY a thing works, never WHO said it works | Same pilot
- 260507 | Held back / softened criticism ("this is not a fatal problem", mid-paragraph hedges, diplomatic ramp-up before each finding) → don't hold back. Friend-tone is honest-tone, not diplomatic-tone. State the cost of each problem directly. The reader pays you for honesty, not politeness | Same pilot
- 260507 | Purpose-of-letter / purpose-of-CTA / final-goal not declared upstream before auditing → make these Step 0 hard gate. Three questions, declared and confirmed before any audit work begins: (1) what is this letter trying to make happen? (2) what does the CTA button need to achieve? (3) what is the final outcome we are creating this letter for? Without them, every finding is judged against an assumed goal that may be wrong | Process gap admitted by Claude
- 260507 | Halbert/Schwartz/coat-of-arms/A-pile/one-person-rule callouts surfaced in client-facing brief → operator-only register. Two output registers required: (A) operator-facing (full jargon, frameworks named, master attributions intact — for Jerel's eyes) and (B) client-facing (zero attributions, plain business-owner language, principles described mechanically). Skill must select register based on deliverable type, not default to one | Architecture decision
- 260507 | "Client outcomes" finding conflated with "you must request testimonials" → distinguish. Outcomes can be surfaced via de-identified case sketches, aggregate metrics, or anonymized moments without asking clients for quotes. Frame the gap as the client's choice with trade-offs visible. Do not prescribe testimonial-collection | Q4 follow-up; Jason has outcomes but won't chase testimonials
- 260507 | Brand-vs-product narrative engine framed as binary forced choice → reframe. Both can coexist. The question is which carries primary trust weight while the other supports. Surface as a client-answerable question with trade-offs, not a prescription | Q4 first read; user pushed back on binary framing
- 260507 | Client brief opened flat without an anchor line → add 1-line purpose statement immediately after the opener declaring the four audit dimensions being checked. Sets read-through expectation. Example: "This is a first-pass audit. I'm looking at four things: conversion, writing tightness, AI tells, what to do next." | Reviewer feedback on V2
- 260507 | Client brief lacked scope distinction → add 1 line stating this is first-pass and that deeper rewrite engagement exists. Do not pitch the engagement; just signal "deeper work exists if you want it." Prevents the reader from over-weighting minor points | Reviewer feedback on V2
- 260507 | Client brief structure asymmetric with operator brief → both registers must use the same 4 named sections: Conversion Potential / Grammar & Clarity / Anti-AI / Notes & Recommendations. Symmetric structure across registers makes the skill predictable. Content within each section adapts to the register | Architectural decision after reviewer feedback
- 260507 | Findings flat-listed without weight → tag every finding with severity [H/M/L] inline at the heading. H = blocks ship-readiness or core conversion. M = costs measurable conversion but not blocking. L = polish or style. Reader scans severity column first | Reviewer feedback on V2
- 260507 | Anti-AI section read as impressionistic → open the Anti-AI section with one sentence declaring which signals were checked (em-dash density, rule-of-three stacking, Big Contrast form, negative parallelism, no-X stacking, aphoristic reduction, revelation hooks, AI-default vocabulary). Methodology transparency = systematic, not vibes-based | Reviewer feedback on V2
- 260507 | Conversion potential evaluation skipped some major copy elements (e.g. benefits) → every major element (headline, lead/subhead, benefits, proof, CTA) gets a one-line read in Conversion Potential. Even if the read is "this is working." Coverage discipline | Reviewer feedback on V2
- 260507 | "Actionable recommendations missing" suggestion offered as enhancement → REJECTED for strategic items. WHAT-not-HOW holds the line for strategic findings in client-facing brief. The HOW is the paid engagement; giving rewrites for free undermines lead-magnet logic. Exception (already locked): AI hygiene gets HOW because it is mechanical not strategic | User explicitly held the line when offered the override option

Brief becomes "framework lens speaking through verbatim buyer language" instead of "framework lens speaking alone" | v0.4 VOC-anchored upgrade — Step 9c output drives Step 14b/16 finding shape

- 260508 | Discredit-old-solutions check fires on Step 3. Cold-path letters often skip naming alternatives. Reason: 'category-only' dismissals trip the FX-course pattern-match for sceptical buyers — the letter sits in a vacuum vs named competitors and reads as suspicious. Empty array OR all category-only → [H] finding | v0.4 — added discredit_old_solutions[] to ump
- 260508 | High-leverage elements (headline / subheadline / CTA pre-button line) MUST include 2–3 replacement candidates in client-facing brief. Reason: these three elements move more conversion than any other surgical change; candidates grounded in dossier verbatim convert faster than client-side rewrites from vague guidance. Scope explicitly limited to these 3 elements — body sections keep WHAT-not-HOW. Client-facing register: angle-differentiation + mechanical "the reason these candidates work" sentence (no copywriter names). Operator-facing register: same candidates with full framework attribution intact (Halbert one-person seed, Schwartz channeling, Big Contrast collapse, etc.) so operator can validate before client sees them | V4 Stackworks brief upgrade — partial break of WHAT-not-HOW rule, scoped exception
- 260508 | Severity legend [H]/[M]/[L] MUST appear in every audit brief (operator AND client) between the header/preamble and the first content section. Verbatim text: [H] = High priority. Fix before sending traffic. Major conversion, trust, or positioning issue. [M] = Medium priority. Important, but not usually a deal-breaker. [L] = Low priority. Polish, clarity, or testing note. Reason: readers (especially client-side) interpret severity tags inconsistently without the legend. Locking the definitions makes the brief self-explanatory and aligns operator + client interpretation.
