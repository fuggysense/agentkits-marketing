# Sales Letter Method — Learnings

Confirmed patterns from live use. Only promoted facts, not one-offs.

---

## Confirmed Patterns

### Initial baseline (2026-04-21) — from 8-page competitor scrape

- **Back-half gap is universal.** 8/8 competitor pages missed FAQ + PS + Guarantee Stack. Enforcing these alone differentiates output from 90% of market letters.
- **Price Anchor is irrelevant for lead-gen/consultation funnels.** Removed from framework. Guarantee Stack replaces it as primary conversion lever.
- **Specificity scales conversion.** Pages with specific numbers (Damien Tan: 812 Singaporeans, 19.56% returns, $526K/4 years, 30 bookings) felt more credible than pages with vague promises.
- **CTA repetition 3-6× per page** is the observed pattern across every converter. Not optional.
- **"I will NEVER" triple-negative** commitment pattern (waste time / hidden fees / transparency) appears in 3/8 pages — the Syncom template. Works for service/consulting.
- **Dear [avatar] letter opener** appears in 4/8 pages. Halbert pattern. Still converts.

### Headline Archetype Selection — Universal Pattern (added 2026-04-23)

**Applies to:** Any audience, any industry, any high-stakes or high-consideration purchase. The underlying emotional/psychological framework is industry-agnostic — whenever a buyer is stuck in a decision loop (alone or with another party), the same three archetypes recur and the same selection rule applies.

**Pattern:** When presenting 3 headline options, the winning one is rarely the sharpest or the cleanest. For high-sophistication, decision-fatigued buyers, it is the one that **recreates the lived scene + (if two parties are involved) removes blame between them + ends with a mechanism image that implies closure, not more research.**

**The three archetypes (always generate one of each, never three rewordings of the same type):**

- **Archetype A — Sharp / Specific.** Names the behaviour with verbatim language, exact time or place, and a blunt promise. High stopping power. Rhythm tends to feel clunky because the leap from scene → promise is direct. Use when: cold scroll-stop ad, retargeting, short-form, or a single-decision-maker buyer.
- **Archetype B — Scene + Relational Validation + Mechanism Image.** Builds a two-party micro-scene that the reader recognizes instantly, validates BOTH parties by name ("neither of you is wrong, both of you are stuck" / equivalent), then ends with a mechanism image that implies terminal action ("closes the tab" / "ends the loop" / "settles it"). Use when: long-form sales letter, warm traffic, any buyer stuck in a repeated real-life conversation with another person (spouse, co-founder, business partner, committee, board, advisor, parent, team).
- **Archetype C — Diagnostic / Premium.** Tells the reader what they actually need, analytically. Implies expert authority and rewards the reader's intelligence. Emotionally thin. Use when: cold high-sophistication analytical buyers, B2B, consulting, solo-decision buyers whose identity is "the careful one" or "the smart one."

**Universal decision rule (applies across verticals — real estate, SaaS, coaching, health, finance, legal, wedding, fertility, education, B2B services):**

Whenever buying emotion is **"I want this mental burden off my shoulders"** (decision fatigue, analysis paralysis, repeated-conversation pattern), **B beats A beats C.**

Three reasons this rule is industry-agnostic:
1. The buyer is not purchasing logic first. They are purchasing **relief from uncertainty** + (if applicable) **permission for the relationship**.
2. Taking sides between two parties creates resistance. Validating both + naming indecision as the enemy removes resistance. This works for spouses buying a house, co-founders picking a vendor, boards approving budget, a solopreneur wrestling with their own past self.
3. The reader is trapped in a loop. Selling more information continues the loop. Selling the end of the loop (mechanism image → terminal action) breaks it.

**Mechanism image examples by vertical** (all do the same job — translate abstract uncertainty into concrete closure):
- Home upgrade: "closes the tab" / "ends the 11pm Excel loop"
- SaaS vendor selection: "ends the demo marathon" / "closes the Notion doc"
- Coaching / consulting: "ends the free-webinar loop" / "closes the bookmarks folder"
- Health / fertility: "stops the Googling" / "replaces the 14 browser tabs with one answer"
- Legal / financial: "ends the 'let me think about it'" / "finally lets you sleep"
- Wedding / one-time-purchase: "ends the Pinterest spiral"

**Framework wiring:** Phase 1 drafters (Hook Half) MUST generate one variant per archetype — Sharp / Scene+Relational / Diagnostic — not three rewordings of the same type. Phase 3 buyer-lens-reviewer + copy-chief-reviewer evaluate against audience profile and route:
- Two-party or repeated-conversation buyer → B
- Cold scroll-stop or single-decision-maker → A
- High-sophistication analytical buyer with expert identity → C

**First observed:** 2026-04-23, real-estate vertical (SG couples upgrading from HDB). Founder selected B over A and C for the reasons above. Pattern is vertical-agnostic — log the next 2-3 selections across different industries and the rule should hold.

## Mistakes Not to Repeat

- Don't force all 12 components when 8 fit the offer — context-first, framework-second.
- Don't use heavy scarcity (countdown timers) for high-ticket consulting — reads as manipulation.
- Don't invent bonuses or testimonials if they don't exist. Pivot to credentials instead.
