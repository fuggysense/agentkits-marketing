## COMPONENT INCLUSION CHECKLIST (do this FIRST)

Before writing a single word, evaluate which components fit this specific offer. Skip any that don't. Do NOT force all 12.

For each component below, answer YES/SKIP with reasoning:

1. **Headline + Sub** — ALWAYS YES
2. **Lead** — ALWAYS YES
3. **Pain Cycle / Why Most Fail** — YES unless audience has no clear "old way" to dismantle
4. **Integrity Tie-Down** — YES for cold traffic (default). SKIP for warm/branded.
5. **Mechanism** — ALWAYS YES
6. **Proof Stack** — YES if real client results exist. SKIP-AND-PIVOT to credentials/methodology/sample work if not.
7. **Offer Breakdown** — ALWAYS YES
8. **Bonus Stack** — YES only if real bonuses exist. Don't invent fake bonuses.
9. **Light Scarcity** — YES only if real capacity limit exists. Otherwise SKIP — fake scarcity destroys trust.
10. **Guarantee Stack** — ALWAYS YES (primary conversion lever)
11. **CTA** — ALWAYS YES
12. **FAQ** — ALWAYS YES (5 objections: Time / Money / Authority / Stall / Preference)
13. **PS Line** — ALWAYS YES

Output the inclusion decision BEFORE drafting. State which components you're skipping and why.

## PRE-DRAFT — DESIRE DISTILLATION (optional but recommended)

Before drafting Component 1 (Headline) and Component 2 (Lead), distill the chosen desire(s) the letter activates. Single-letter projects rarely need 5 desires — but the **distinctness test** is the cleanest way to validate that the chosen Layer-3 / Layer-4 desire isn't a smuggled bundle of two desires, which produces letters that feel emotionally diffuse.

**Procedure:**

1. List the 1-3 desires this letter activates. State each as a verbatim "I want…" sentence in the avatar's voice.
2. **Pairwise distinctness test.** For every pair of desires, ask: *"Could these two desires be merged into one broader desire without losing meaning?"*
   - If **yes** → they're not distinct. One is a variation of the other. Pick the stronger and drop the weaker.
   - If **no** → they're genuinely different. Both belong in the letter.
3. **Within-segment check.** Are all the surfaced desires inside the chosen avatar segment? If a desire belongs to a different persona, kill it.
4. **Layer check.** What desire layer is each at? (Product / Functional / Outcome / Identity — see `references/frameworks.md` → Desire Layer Ladder.) Push to Layer 3 minimum, Layer 4 if reachable.
5. **Spine check.** Could this desire be the spine of the entire letter? If no, it's not load-bearing — it can supplement another desire but shouldn't drive Component 1.

**Examples of distinct desires (weight loss, same avatar):**
- "I want to fit into my old clothes again" (nostalgia for past self)
- "I want to avoid future health problems" (preventative)
- "I want to date again without feeling embarrassed" (social confidence)

These are distinct because each pursues a fundamentally different outcome from the same product. They could share a letter if the spine desire was clear.

**Examples that fail the test:**
- "I want to lose 20 pounds" + "I want to slim down" + "I want to feel lighter" → same desire, different words
- "I want more energy" + "I want to feel less tired" → same desire, opposite framing
- "I want to be healthier" + "I want to live longer" → too close to differentiate

If the distillation surfaces fewer than 1 distinct Layer-3+ desire — halt and surface to operator. The avatar/offer combination needs more research before drafting.

## COMPONENT SPECIFICATIONS

### 1. HEADLINE

Single most important sentence. Must be simultaneously:
- **Exciting** — speaks to urgent desire
- **Important** — addresses a significant pain or opportunity
- **Beneficial** — clear valuable outcome
- **Unique** — suggests a distinct methodology they haven't seen

**Rules:**
- Start with the result. Name the tangible transformation.
- Specificity: numbers, niches, timeframes, dollar amounts
- Position against market fatigue ("without X, Y, Z that everyone else does")
- Visualize the outcome in the reader's mind
- Odd numbers outperform even (7 > 6). Specific beats round (37 > 40).

**Write 10-15 variations. Pick the strongest.** Headline testing is gateway to every other metric.

Templates worth stealing (from competitor scrapes):
- "How to [Dream Outcome] Without [Pain Path 1], [Pain Path 2], or [Pain Path 3]"
- "I Will Help You [Outcome] Using My [Mechanism Name] [Credibility Claim]"
- "How to [Unusual Outcome] in [Timeframe] — Using the Same [Pattern] as [Authority Proof]"

### 2. SUBHEADLINE

Either filters the audience or sharpens desire. 1-2 short lines. NEVER introduces a new idea — deepens belief in the headline.

Three patterns:
- **"If You've Tried and Failed":** "If you've [past attempt] and still [result], you'll want to read every word of this."
- **"You're Not Alone":** "Most [avatar] waste 80% of [resource] before discovering [insight]. This changes that."
- **"Contrarian Frame":** "Everything you've been taught about [topic] is either outdated or incomplete. Here's what actually works in today's environment."

### 3. LEAD (500-800 words)

The Lead is NOT an introduction. It is the trust and momentum builder. Job:
- Reinforce that this offer is exactly what they've been looking for
- Loop the Big Idea so it stays top of mind
- Tease — not explain — the mechanism
- Start dissolving skepticism without hard claims

Core moves (in order):
1. **Restate the offer in new language** — show you're serious, not exaggerating
2. **Show the transformation** — before/after life, emotional but specific
3. **Tease the mechanism** — build anticipation without naming it yet
4. **Preempt early objections** — signal awareness of "will this work for my industry?" "is this another funnel?" etc.

Style:
- Paragraphs 2-4 lines max
- Use language repetition for rhythm ("This isn't X. This is Y.")
- Bold outcome phrases for scanners
- Include light proof early ("This method generated 180+ calls last month")
- Dear [avatar] opener works (Halbert pattern, 5/8 competitor pages use it)

### 4. INTEGRITY TIE-DOWN

Short rhetorical commitment. Gets the reader to micro-agree BEFORE the mechanism reveal. One of Hormozi's most underused moves.

Templates:
- "If you nodded in agreement to ANY of this, you're in the right place."
- "Does any of this sound like your week?"
- "If you've felt even 10% of that frustration, keep reading."

Placement: between Pain Cycle (component 3) and Mechanism (component 5). Creates the emotional pivot point.

### 5. MECHANISM (5 jobs, not 4)

Answer: "How does this actually work AND why does it produce different results than the old way?"

**Five moves:**
1. **Name the system.** Mechanism must have a NAME. Makes it branded, referable, authoritative. "The [X] Method." If the client hasn't named it, invent one that fits.
2. **Describe function, not tools.** What does it achieve at each step, NOT the software/platforms.
3. **Visual clarity, rhythmic flow.** Compress the buyer journey: "Instead of [old way spanning 7 days], the entire journey happens in under 10 minutes."
4. **JUSTIFY — cause-and-effect logic.** Pick 1-2 of 4 patterns from `references/mechanism-justification.md`:
   - **A. Cause-and-Effect Chain** — explain why each step produces its result
   - **B. Contrast Against Conventional** — what most operators do + why it fails + what this does differently
   - **C. First-Principles Reasoning** — derive the method from a foundational truth
   - **D. Constraint Acknowledgment** — what this method gives up and why the tradeoff favors the reader
5. **Numbers + time anchors.** "This structure has booked over 3,500 qualified appointments across 367 clients, with results in as little as 72 hours."

Psychological goal: reader thinks "this is structured, repeatable, I understand WHY it works, and I can picture it working in my world."

**Length cap for Job 4 (justification):**
- 800-word letter → 50-80 words of justification
- 1,200-word letter → 80-150 words
- 1,800+ word letter → 150-250 words

Enough to build conviction. Not so much it reads like a whitepaper.

#### CRITICAL — UMP Derivation Rule (per-angle freshness)

The UMP must be derived **fresh from the specific angle being written.** It cannot be inherited from prior context, prior letters in the project, or other products in the same operator's catalog. Each angle gets its own problem mechanism.

When running multiple letters / angles for one client, cross-contamination of UMPs is a real failure mode — the operator hands off "use the same mechanism" and the second letter ends up addressing the first letter's problem against the second letter's audience. The result: an angle the second audience doesn't recognize.

**Enforcement:** Before drafting Component 5 for any letter, the drafter must write a header block:
```
[UMP for this angle]
- Angle: <one sentence — who is this letter for, what specific painful situation>
- UMP: <2-3 sentences naming the hidden cause for THIS angle's audience>
- Why prior solutions failed for THIS audience: <bullet list, named solutions>
```

If the header block looks identical to a prior letter's header block in the same project, the angle isn't actually distinct — kill it or re-derive.

#### UMS Fixity Rule (product-fixed across angles)

Inverse of the UMP rule. The Unique Mechanism of Solution (UMS) is **product-fixed across ALL letters in the same project.** It does not vary by angle.

The product solves the same problem in the same way regardless of which audience is reading. What varies per angle is which audience's painful situation the UMP frames — the UMS that closes the loop is constant, pulled from the operator's actual product/process documentation.

If two letters in the same project have meaningfully different UMS framings, one of them is wrong. The product has one mechanism of action. Letters can vary which problem the UMS resolves, not how the product itself works.

**Together these two rules prevent the most common multi-letter inconsistency:** UMPs leaking across angles (same problem mechanism applied to different audiences) and UMS drifting across letters (the product appearing to do different things in different angles).

### 6. PROOF STACK

Validates every claim. Replaces hesitation with pattern recognition.

Types (use multiple):
- **Performance screenshots** — actual dashboards, calendars, Stripe. Include time window + outcome + before-context.
- **Short case studies** — 2-4 sentences each, volume over long testimonials
- **Client quotes** — result-specific, emotionally honest, role/niche anchored

**IF NO PROOF YET:** Pivot entire stack to:
- Methodology credentials (frameworks studied, books published, mentors)
- Sample work or "behind the scenes" transparency
- Specific numbers from the client's prior employer/experience
- Third-party proof (industry stats supporting their approach)

Format:
- Bolded summaries above screenshots ("$11K closed in 3 days — $69/day spend")
- Case study callouts between main sections to break fatigue
- Testimonial walls 3-5 rows max, not dense

### 7. OFFER BREAKDOWN

Make value concrete. Most letters fail here from vagueness.

Structure:
1. **Lead with outcome:** "This system is built to consistently deliver X without Y."
2. **Enumerate deliverables** — plain language, bulleted, no jargon. Present as ASSETS not benefits.
3. **Visual anchors** — preview, screenshot, dashboard (if applicable)
4. **Timeframe anchor** — "Once onboarded, your system is live within X days."

### 8. BONUS STACK (if real bonuses exist)

A single offer is less valuable than the same offer broken into parts and presented as bonuses. Bonuses should address SPECIFIC obstacles the buyer faces.

Example: "Bonus: The [Specific Script] for [Specific Objection]"

Skip entirely if there are no real bonuses. Don't invent them.

### 9. LIGHT SCARCITY (if real)

Only if real capacity limits exist. Two patterns that work:
- Numerical cap: "We only accept 5 consultations per month."
- Quality-gated: "We only accept 30 bookings per month as we maintain the quality of our work."

NEVER use:
- Countdown timers
- "Only 2 spots left!" (unless verifiably true)
- Fake urgency that resets when the page reloads

If no real cap, SKIP this component. Integrity > manufactured urgency.

### 10. GUARANTEE STACK (PRIMARY CONVERSION LEVER)

For no-price consultation funnels, choose from `guarantee-variants.md`:
- **No-pitch guarantee:** "If we can't help in the first 10 minutes, we'll end the call."
- **Value-pay guarantee:** "If you waste your time on the call, we'll send you $[amount]."
- **Outcome guarantee:** "Leave with at least 3 actionable insights — or we pay."
- **Integrity layer:** triple-negative commitments ("I will NEVER waste your time / hit you with hidden fees / etc.")

Stack 2-3 together for strongest effect.

### 11. CTA

Benefit-oriented button copy. Friction reducer below.

**Weak:** "Sign Up" / "Submit" / "Book Now"
**Strong:** "Get [outcome]" / "See the exact [method]" / "Claim my [specific thing]"

Below-CTA friction reducer formula: `[Risk reversal] + [Social proof] + [Speed/ease]`
Example: "No pressure. Join 2,600+ agents. 2-minute booking."

Repeat CTA 3-6 times down the page (observed pattern across all 8 competitor scrapes).

### 12. FAQ (5 core objections)

Non-negotiable — missing in 8/8 competitor pages scraped. This is your moat.

Address the 5 core objections:
1. **Time:** "I don't have time for this."
2. **Money:** "Is this worth the investment?" (even though no price — they're thinking about opportunity cost)
3. **Authority:** "Can I decide, or do I need someone else's buy-in?"
4. **Stall:** "Let me think about it / come back later."
5. **Preference:** "This won't work for my specific situation."

Format: Question (bold) + 2-sentence answer. Keep tight.

### 12.5 QUALIFICATION BLOCK (required — at least 1)

Full spec: `references/qualification-patterns.md`.

At minimum one of these must appear (placement flexible):
- **"Who this is for"** — testable conditions (reader can tick yes/no) — in Lead or mid-body
- **"Who this isn't for"** — real disqualifiers — after Offer or before Guarantee
- **Readiness Criteria** — immediately before final CTA

Letter length determines how many:
- 800-1,200 words → 1 block
- 1,200-1,800 words → 2 blocks
- 1,800+ words → all 3 blocks

**Rule:** Every condition must be testable. No vague "ambitious people." Concrete criteria only.

Example:
```
This works best for SG property agents who:
- Have closed at least 3 transactions in the last 12 months
- Currently rely on cold calls or referrals for leads
- Can commit 30 minutes daily to lead response in the first 60 days
```

### 13. PS LINE

One of the most-read parts of any long letter. Handles final threshold fear.

Templates:
- "PS — The only reason people don't book is [fear]. Here's why that doesn't apply to you…"
- "PS — If you're still on the fence, just know that [one-sentence proof or reassurance]."
- "PS — Every day you wait, [specific cost of inaction]."

Personal tone. Short. Direct.

## LENGTH-CREEP AUTO-REJECT (quantitative guardrail)

The components most likely to bloat are also the ones where bloat kills conversion fastest:

- **Pain Cycle / Why Most Fail (Component 3)** — when this overruns, it stops being recognition and becomes whining. The reader exits.
- **Mechanism reveal (Component 5)** — when this overruns, it reads like a whitepaper. Conviction collapses into confusion.
- **Mechanism-of-Solution explanation (within Component 5 or 7)** — when this overruns, the reader can't tell what the product *is* anymore.
- **CTA closing block (Component 11)** — when this overruns, it rehashes the body and buries the action.

**Rule:** Set a word budget for each component before drafting. **Any component that drifts >20% over its budget = auto-reject in Conversion Gate.**

The Conversion Gate must verify component-level word counts, not only total letter length. A 1,400-word letter with a 500-word CTA fails even if the total is on-spec.

**Default budgets (adjust to letter length):**

| Component | 800-1,200 word letter | 1,200-1,800 word letter | 1,800+ word letter |
|-----------|------------------------|--------------------------|---------------------|
| Pain Cycle (3) | 100-150 | 200-280 | 280-380 |
| Mechanism (5) | 80-120 | 130-200 | 200-280 |
| Mechanism-of-Solution payoff | 70-100 | 100-150 | 150-220 |
| CTA closing block (11) | 120-160 | 160-210 | 200-260 |

If a component lands outside its budget, the drafter must either tighten or surface a deliberate exception with reasoning. "It just felt like it needed to be longer" is not a valid exception.

## FAILURE CONDITIONS (auto-reject if any present)

- Any vague promise with no specific number
- Generic headline reusable by 10 other businesses in the space
- Missing Guarantee component
- Missing FAQ (5 objections)
- Missing PS line
- CTA that commands action ("Submit") instead of describing benefit
- Heavy urgency (countdown timer, fake "X left") used without real scarcity
- Fake testimonials or invented proof
- AI-pattern language (detected via Sweep 8 + unslop profile)
- Forcing components that don't fit the offer
- **Self-contained experience failures** (any of the following is auto-reject — these map directly to the three questions the self-contained reviewer asks every create + review):
  - Broken argument beat: a cold reader cannot follow the letter top to bottom without confusion
  - Orphaned idea: a metaphor, acronym, branded term, or job title introduced and never explained or paid off
  - Movement count > 9: too many sections / distinct narrative beats
  - Defensive stacking: two or more back-to-back "why we do it this way" blocks interrupting momentum
  - Sentence > 25 words without a rhythmic justification
  - Spelled-out number that should be a digit (e.g. "twelve months" instead of "12 months")
  - Unexplained acronym or branded internal name (e.g. "RAM" / "SPOT" / "Courage to Change" without a one-line plain-language explanation)
  - Paragraph opening sentence > 8 words
  - Any phrase flagged as "not okay" — wording that would make a tired native-English reader feel slightly lesser, confused, or behind

- Calm, professional, decisive — NOT hypey
