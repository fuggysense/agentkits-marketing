# Classic Direct Response Frameworks

Distilled from Halbert, Sugarman, Schwartz, Kennedy, Hormozi — the fundamentals behind every long-form sales letter. Applied references for each of the 12 components.

---

## Schwartz 5 Levels of Awareness (1966)

The headline approach CHANGES based on traffic temperature. Cold FB scroll traffic is usually Level 2-3.

### Level 1 — Most Aware
Knows you, knows your product, ready to buy.
**Headline approach:** Name + offer + price + urgency.
**Not your traffic.**

### Level 2 — Product-Aware
Knows your product exists but not sure about it.
**Headline approach:** Lead with the unique benefit or differentiator.
**Example:** "How [Product] Helps [Specific Avatar] [Specific Outcome] in [Timeframe]"

### Level 3 — Solution-Aware
Knows solutions exist but doesn't know yours.
**Headline approach:** Lead with your unique MECHANISM. Position as different/better.
**Example:** "A new way to [outcome] without [common pain/old way]"

### Level 4 — Problem-Aware
Knows they have a problem, no idea about solutions.
**Headline approach:** Name the problem sharply + promise relief.
**Example:** "Tired of [specific painful situation]? Here's what finally worked for [specific avatar]."

### Level 5 — Unaware
Doesn't know they have the problem yet.
**Headline approach:** Story + curiosity hook. Not for cold traffic — save for content.

**For cold FB/IG traffic:** default to Level 3 (Solution-Aware) unless buyer-profile.md indicates otherwise.

## Desire Layer Ladder (Layers 1–4)

Schwartz covers awareness — what they know about the problem and solution. The Desire Layer Ladder covers depth — how foundational the wanting is. The deeper you anchor, the harder the desire converts.

| Layer | Name | Example (weight loss) | What it sells |
|-------|------|------------------------|---------------|
| 1 | Product | "I want this supplement" | The thing |
| 2 | Functional | "I want to lose weight" | The capability |
| 3 | Outcome | "I want to fit into my old clothes" | The result |
| 4 | Identity | "I want to be the person I was before" | The self |

**Why Layer 4 converts hardest (and best):**
- Not comparable across competitors (you can't compare two products on identity)
- Compounds with use — every day proves identity is restored
- Triggers loyalty rather than price-shopping
- Survives objections that defeat Layer 1-3 promises

**The conversion ceiling rule:** If the letter never reaches Layer 4, the ceiling is set by whatever the strongest competitor's Layer 3 promise is. To exceed that ceiling, anchor at Layer 4 in at least the headline, the closing, and one body section.

**Layer 4 audit test:**
- Could the headline exist on a competitor's page with one word changed? → too shallow
- Does the close return to "the person you'll become" or only "the result you'll get"? → return to identity
- Does the PS handle a Layer 4 fear (becoming someone you don't recognize, staying stuck as the version of you that failed) or a Layer 3 fear (not getting the result)? → resolve at Layer 4

**Within-segment rule:** All desires across the letter must live INSIDE the chosen avatar segment. Different desires the same person could have — not desires from different personas.

## Halbert: The Boron Letters (1984)

### 1. Dear [Avatar] opener
Cold letter addressed to a specific person. Not a generic opening.

**Examples:**
- "Dear Fellow Property Investor,"
- "Dear Struggling Roofer,"
- "Dear Burned-Out Agent,"

Appears in 4/8 competitor pages. Still converts.

### 2. Specificity as credibility
Numbers, dates, amounts, names. NOT vague promises.

- "812 Singaporeans" > "many clients"
- "$526K every 4 years" > "great returns"
- "19.56% annual returns" > "strong performance"
- "17 days" > "quickly"

### 3. Punch copy — lead with the kicker
Don't warm up to the point. Open with the biggest promise or sharpest pain.

### 4. Read great copy by hand
Not applicable to the skill, but a reminder: the team should study winning letters physically, not just read them. Internalize the rhythm.

### 5. Ask for the sale 7 times
CTA repeats 3-6× throughout the page. Confirmed pattern in 7/8 competitor scrapes.

## Sugarman: The Adweek Copywriting Handbook (1998)

### Every element has one job
Each component pulls the reader to the next.

1. **Headline:** Get attention, draw to subheadline
2. **Subheadline:** Give more info, draw into copy
3. **First sentence:** Short, easy to read (the "slippery slide")
4. **Body:** Slide them down to the CTA
5. **CTA:** Tell them exactly what to do

### The Slippery Slide
First sentence must be SHORT. Like 3-5 words. Just enough to make the second sentence irresistible. Each sentence sets up the next.

**Example:**
- "It's simple."
- "You book the call."
- "We do the rest."
- "Here's exactly how..."

### Curiosity gaps at every paragraph break
Each paragraph ends with a hook that makes the next paragraph impossible to skip.

## RMBC: Knowledge-Gap Anchor

Every long-form letter has a single sentence that — if it lands — makes everything downstream feel inevitable. RMBC calls this the **knowledge gap statement**: a one-sentence distillation of the Unique Mechanism of Problem (UMP) and Unique Mechanism of Solution (UMS) that anchors the letter's hook.

**The pattern (3 jobs, one sentence):**
1. **Validate past failure** — "everything you've tried" framing
2. **Identify the missing piece** — name the hidden cause (UMP)
3. **Set up the solution logically** — name the pathway (UMS)

**Worked example (sleep supplement for kids):**

> "The reason your child still isn't sleeping — despite everything you've tried — isn't a behavior problem. It's a mineral problem. And the reason the mineral supplements you've tried haven't worked is that you've been delivering them through the wrong channel."

That single statement validates failure (everything you've tried), names the UMP (mineral, not behavior), and sets up the UMS (delivery channel, not just supplementation) — without naming the product yet.

**Construction rules:**

1. **The UMP must invalidate at least 2 prior failed solutions by name.** Generic mechanisms ("inflammation") don't qualify. The mechanism has to explain why solution-A specifically failed AND why solution-B specifically failed.
2. **The UMP must remove blame from the prospect.** Never frame as "you weren't trying hard enough." Always frame as "the system / your doctor / the industry didn't tell you this."
3. **The UMS must follow logically from the UMP.** If the UMP is "your cells lack the energy to use the hormones HRT provides," the UMS is "this product rebuilds cellular energy production, allowing the hormones to work." UMS without UMP is just a feature claim.
4. **Both halves form the spine of the letter.** Pain Cycle (Component 3) sets up the need for the UMP. Mechanism (Component 5) reveals it. Offer + Proof (7+6) show the UMS in action.

**Honesty requirement:** Every UMP must be honestly defensible. If the science is debated, acknowledge it. If the mechanism is one of several plausible explanations, frame it that way. Overclaim destroys trust at the sophisticated end of the segment AND invites compliance issues. See `references/mechanism-justification.md` for the 4 justification patterns.

**Cross-vertical applicability:**

- **DTC supplement** — UMP: cellular bandwidth collapse. UMS: AMPK activation rebuilds bandwidth.
- **Real estate first-time buyer** — UMP: couples lack a single trusted number both can commit to. UMS: a derivation process that produces that number live, in 30 minutes.
- **B2B SaaS ops platform** — UMP: process drift detection lag, not manual processes. UMS: immutable audit log surfaces drift in real-time.
- **Coaching career transition** — UMP: every method assumes the wrong starting identity. UMS: rebuild identity foundation first, method on top.

The structure is invariant. The content varies entirely with product, market, avatar.

## Hormozi: $100M Offers + Value Equation

### The Value Equation
**Value = (Dream Outcome × Perceived Likelihood) / (Time Delay × Effort & Sacrifice)**

Every component should address one or more of these variables:

- **Dream Outcome** → Components 1 (Headline), 2 (Lead), 7 (Offer)
- **Perceived Likelihood** → Component 6 (Proof Stack)
- **Time Delay** → Component 5 (Mechanism — speed)
- **Effort & Sacrifice** → Components 5 (Mechanism — ease) + 7 (Offer — done-for-you)

### Living Proof
iPhone-style raw testimonials showing people EXACTLY LIKE the prospect succeeding. Not polished corporate case studies.

- Face + name + result = 3× more trust than just a quote
- Video > text testimonials
- Specific $ numbers beat vague "transformation"

### Deadlines Drive Decisions
Light scarcity (component 9) only if real. Hormozi's "5 spots" works. Fake countdowns destroy trust.

### Risk Reversal > Price Reduction
Making the guarantee stronger beats making the price lower. This is why the Guarantee Stack is the primary conversion lever on no-price pages.

### The Grand Slam Offer Structure
1. Dream Outcome (what they get)
2. Value stack (listed components)
3. Bonuses (if applicable)
4. Price anchor (skip for our use case)
5. Scarcity (if real)
6. Urgency (if real)
7. Guarantee stack
8. CTA

Adapted for our skill: same structure, price anchor removed, guarantees promoted.

## Kennedy: The Ultimate Sales Letter

### The Ten-Point Script
1. Get attention
2. Identify the problem
3. Provide the solution
4. Present your credentials
5. Show the benefits
6. Give social proof
7. Make your offer
8. Inject scarcity
9. Call to action
10. Give a warning (what happens if they don't act)

Maps cleanly to our 12-component framework. #10 (Warning) is a useful addition — weave into PS line.

### PS Line Rules
- Always include
- Most-read element after headline
- Use to handle the #1 remaining objection
- Personal tone, not corporate

## Ogilvy: Confessions of an Advertising Man

### The Tested Headline Principles
1. Headline is 80% of the ad
2. Specific > generic ("17 days" > "quickly")
3. News-style (new / introducing / announcing) still outperforms
4. Numbers in headlines outperform without
5. "How to" beats most openings

### The Reader is Not a Moron — She's Your Wife
Respect the reader. No condescension, no hype. Calm authority beats screaming.

## Quick-Reference Rules

- Odd numbers outperform even (7 > 6)
- Specific beats round (37 > 40)
- "You" 3× more than "I" or "We"
- Active voice dominant
- Short sentences punctuate long ones (rhythm)
- Every paragraph ends with a hook
- CTA describes benefit, not action
- Friction reducer below every CTA: `[Risk reversal] + [Social proof] + [Speed/ease]`

---

## What To Avoid

- Clever > clear (never)
- Features > benefits (never)
- Passive voice dominant
- Vague promises without numbers
- Over-claiming guarantees
- Heavy countdown timers on consulting offers
- Fake testimonials or invented proof
- Generic headlines reusable by 10 other businesses
