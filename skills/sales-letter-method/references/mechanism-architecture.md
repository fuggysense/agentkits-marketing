# Mechanism + Offer Architecture (Phase 0.7 playbook)

**Owns:** the unique mechanism, the named alternatives it discredits, the offer stack, the brand association ladder, and the scene assignment that drives Phase 1 drafting.

**Position in pipeline:** runs after Phase 0.5 (Claim Audit), before Phase 1 (Parallel Drafting). Outputs feed both drafters as a required input alongside the component matrix and claim inventory. Drafters running without these outputs is a hard error — they will improvise mechanism naming and produce category-only ("most agents fail to follow up") dismissals instead of structural ("Endowus = passive index, no downside protection in regime breaks") ones.

**Audit pair:** `skills/sales-letter-audit/` Step 3 (UMP / discredit-old-solutions) checks the resulting letter for MAGIC compliance and named-alternative structure. Creation and audit share vocabulary so a letter that passes here will not surface a UMP failure there.

## A. Unique Characteristic + Discredit Old Solutions

**Goal:** a one-sentence mechanism articulation plus a structural failure-mode breakdown of every named alternative the buyer is currently using or considering.

### A.1 The mechanism sentence

Format: `X works because of Y, which Z [named alternatives] cannot do because [structural failure mode].`

- X = the mechanism's plain-language name (or working tag — final naming happens in step B)
- Y = the unique characteristic — the thing only this mechanism has, expressed at a process or math level
- Z = the named alternatives, by their real product / service / brand names, NOT by category
- Failure mode = math level or process level. Not "they're bad," "they don't care," "they're not as good." Specific structural reason it cannot deliver the outcome.

### A.2 Named alternatives — 3 to 5 minimum

Three is the floor. Five is healthier. Each must be named (real brand / product / method) and have its own failure mode. Generic categories ("most coaches," "other agents") fail this gate — they read as cope and audit Step 3 will mark UMP weak.

### A.3 Failure mode tests

A failure mode is acceptable only if it answers ALL three:

1. Could a competitor read this and feel obligated to defend it? (If no, it's too vague.)
2. Does it name a structural cause — math, process, incentive, system architecture — not a vibe?
3. Could the buyer verify it with one Google search?

### A.4 Worked example — Singapore wealth services

> Stackworks works because each portfolio is structured with explicit downside-protection rails set at allocation time, which Endowus, T-bills, and private banker structured notes cannot do because:
> - **Endowus** runs passive index allocation — no protection layer at all in regime breaks like Q4 2018, March 2020, or 2022 bonds.
> - **DBS T-bills / Singapore Savings Bonds** cap returns at the SG risk-free rate (~3%) — return-of-capital instrument, not a return-on-capital one.
> - **Private banker structured notes** carry 2-3% annual fee drag plus opaque OTC pricing — the bank is the counterparty, not a fiduciary.
> - **Robo-advisors (Syfe, StashAway)** rebalance on volatility triggers, not allocation rules — same regime-break exposure as Endowus with a different label.

Each named. Each has a structural reason. Each is verifiable.

### A.5 Worked example — agency / consulting

> Fuggy's Media works because each lead is sourced from intent-validated property search behavior, which the three alternatives SG agents currently use cannot do because:
> - **PropertyGuru lead packages** sell the same lead to 5-7 agents within 24 hours — buyer fatigue on first call.
> - **Cold-calling district lists** has a 0.3-0.8% conversion floor (industry data, REIB 2024) because intent is absent.
> - **Self-run Meta ads with default targeting** burns ~$80 CPL on broad audiences because the platform's default optimizer chases form-fills, not appointment shows.

## B. MAGIC Name composition

**Goal:** 3 candidate mechanism names, each broken down by M / A / G / I / C, ranked by stickiness + audit-pair compliance.

| Letter | Stands for | Question |
|--------|-----------|----------|
| M | Magnetic reason | Why this, why now? |
| A | Avatar | Which specific segment? |
| G | Goal | The dream outcome |
| I | Interval | The timeline |
| C | Container | The unique container word |

### B.1 Container word rules

The container word is the load-bearing piece — it is what the buyer remembers and repeats. Constraints:

- Two syllables ideal, three max.
- Not a generic noun ("system," "method," "framework," "blueprint" all fail — saturated).
- Either an invented compound, a borrowed concrete object, or a verb-as-noun.
- Pronounceable on first read.

### B.2 Worked example — wealth services

| Candidate | M | A | G | I | C |
|-----------|---|---|---|---|---|
| **The 4-Year Stackworks Allocation** | Capital protection in current regime | SG founders post-exit ($2-10M liquid) | $526K every 4 years | 4 years (one cycle) | Stackworks |
| The Liquidity Ladder | Multiple withdrawal stages | SG founders post-exit | Steady income + capital growth | 4-year ladder | Ladder |
| The Singapore Capital Rail | SG-specific tax + structure | SG founders post-exit | 8-12% net annual | Annual | Rail |

Ranked: Stackworks > Ladder > Rail (Stackworks is the only invented compound — Ladder and Rail are borrowed but Ladder is widely used in fixed income).

### B.3 Worked example — agency

| Candidate | M | A | G | I | C |
|-----------|---|---|---|---|---|
| **The 30-Day Showroom Pipeline** | Booked appointments, not leads | SG agents 2-5 yrs in | 8-12 viewings/month | 30 days | Pipeline |
| The Intent Funnel | Validated buyer intent | SG agents 2-5 yrs in | Booked appointments | Rolling | Funnel |
| The Conversion Rail | Predictable closes | SG agents 2-5 yrs in | 1-2 closes/month | Monthly | Rail |

Ranked: Showroom Pipeline > Conversion Rail > Intent Funnel (Showroom = concrete-borrowed, vivid; Pipeline is overused but Showroom redeems it).

## C. Grand Slam Offer composition

**Goal:** stacked offer architecture with item-by-item value attribution, structured by Hormozi's value equation.

### C.1 The value equation

```
Value = (Dream Outcome × Perceived Likelihood) ÷ (Time Delay × Effort & Sacrifice)
```

The job: maximize numerator, shrink denominator. Every stack item must move at least one of the four levers.

### C.2 Trim + Stack

1. **List every micro-problem** the buyer faces between current state and dream outcome. Get to 8-12 items minimum. Sub-problems count.
2. **Solution per problem** — for each micro-problem, what specifically removes it?
3. **Bundle the solutions** into the deliverable stack. Each bundle item is tagged to a value-equation lever.
4. **Solve the next problem** — what does using this mechanism create as a follow-on need? Address pre-emptively as a stack item, not as an upsell.

### C.3 Stack format (template)

```
DELIVERABLE STACK

Core mechanism: [name] — addresses [Dream Outcome ↑]

Stack item 1: [Name] — addresses [micro-problem #X] — moves [Time Delay ↓ / Effort ↓]
Stack item 2: [Name] — addresses [micro-problem #Y] — moves [Perceived Likelihood ↑]
... (continue until 5-9 items)

Next-problem pre-empt: [Name] — addresses [follow-on need created by using mechanism] — moves [Effort ↓]
```

### C.4 Worked example — wealth services

```
Core mechanism: 4-Year Stackworks Allocation — Dream Outcome ↑

Stack item 1: Quarterly regime-break review call — addresses "what if markets break?" — Perceived Likelihood ↑
Stack item 2: Tax structuring layer (SG-specific) — addresses "I'll lose to tax" — Effort ↓
Stack item 3: Direct portfolio dashboard (no quarterly statement lag) — addresses "I can't see what's happening" — Effort ↓
Stack item 4: Liquidity gate at 12 months — addresses "what if I need it sooner" — Time Delay ↓
Stack item 5: Estate / trust handoff template — addresses "what about my kids" — Perceived Likelihood ↑

Next-problem pre-empt: Annual rebalance call with the operator (not an associate) — addresses the inevitable "the strategy worked, now what" — Effort ↓
```

## D. Branding via Association + Bouquet narrowing

**Goal:** transfer-influence list (3-5) plus a one-segment bouquet narrowing statement.

### D.1 Transfer influence

List 3-5 brands, people, methodologies, or outcomes the target buyer ALREADY trusts that this mechanism can be paired with positionally. Each entry is named, and each entry includes the specific trust signal it carries (credential, process, outcome, ethic).

The pairing is positional, not parasitic. Do not claim endorsement. The audit step will mark "as seen on" or fake-endorsement framing as a hard fail.

### D.2 Bouquet narrowing

State the ONE segment from `clients/<slug>/avatars/` the marketing speaks to. Not all five. Not "primarily 1 and 2 with some 3." One.

The other avatars do not get spoken to in this letter. They get their own letter or a different angle. Letters that try to address all five end up specific to none — audit Step 3 marks this as a UMP collapse.

### D.3 Worked example — wealth services

```
Bouquet narrow: SG founder, post-exit (5-7 figures liquid), 38-52, second-generation immigrant family.

Transfer influence:
- Ray Dalio's All Weather thesis (regime-aware allocation, not market timing)
- Endowment-style allocation (Yale model — 7-9% real returns through cycles)
- Singapore GIC's defensive-growth posture (a model the buyer respects)
- Naval Ravikant's framing on capital preservation post-exit
- The "Rich Dad cashflow quadrant" mental model (B → I shift)

Positioning statement: "For SG founders who've exited and now face the second hardest problem after building the company — keeping it. Allocated the way Yale's endowment is allocated, with regime-break protection a private banker can't structurally offer."
```

### D.4 Worked example — agency

```
Bouquet narrow: SG property agent, 2-5 years in, currently on 1-2 closes/month, has tried PropertyGuru lead packages and at least one cold-calling stint.

Transfer influence:
- The "Million Dollar Round Table" credential (industry-recognized close volume)
- Stuart Adam's positioning frameworks (SG real estate education space)
- The PropNex / ERA training systems (familiar pedagogy)
- Specific named SG agents who closed 12+ deals in their second year using paid-traffic systems

Positioning statement: "For SG agents 2-5 years in who've outgrown PropertyGuru leads but haven't cracked the paid-traffic playbook. The same intent-validated pipeline that took [named operator] from 2 to 12 closes in his second year."
```

## E. What-Who-When scene picker

**Goal:** for each of the 12 letter components in `references/component-matrix.md`, assign which scene type the drafter writes.

### E.1 Three scene types

- **What scenes** — contrast Nightmare (current alternative failing) vs Dream Outcome (mechanism delivering). Drives Pain Cycle, Mechanism, Offer.
- **Who scenes** — status shift among family / peers / rivals. Drives Lead, Proof, PS.
- **When scenes** — consequence of indecision across past / present / future timelines. Drives Integrity Tie-Down, Scarcity, FAQ.

### E.2 Scene-component mapping (default — adjust for offer)

| Component | Default scene type | Why |
|-----------|-------------------|-----|
| 1. Headline + Subheadline | What | Promise the contrast outright |
| 2. Lead | Who | Address the specific avatar by status |
| 3. Pain Cycle / Why Most Fail | What | Nightmare scene, named alternative |
| 4. Integrity Tie-Down | When | "If you nodded — this is past, present, or future" |
| 5. Mechanism | What | Dream Outcome delivered by Y |
| 6. Proof Stack | Who | Named operator → status shift |
| 7. Offer Breakdown + Bonus Stack | What | Stack delivers Dream Outcome |
| 8. Light Scarcity | When | Consequence-of-indecision (capacity timeline) |
| 9. Guarantee Stack | When | Future state — what we promise will be true |
| 10. CTA | Who | Future-self status |
| 11. FAQ | When (Time/Money) + Who (Authority) + What (Stall/Preference) | Mixed — split as objection demands |
| 12. PS | Who | Threshold fear → status loss |

### E.3 Companion: emotional sequence

The scene picker pairs with `frameworks/six-emotional-states.md`. What scenes do their emotional work via curiosity → understanding → conviction. Who scenes work via belonging → pride / fear-of-loss. When scenes work via urgency → resignation-prevention. Pick scenes such that the emotional arc through the 12 components is monotonic, not whiplash.

### E.4 Output format for Phase 1 drafters

```
SCENE ASSIGNMENT (per component)

C1 Headline+Sub  → WHAT (Nightmare→Dream contrast in headline)
C2 Lead          → WHO   (status address: "for SG founders post-exit")
C3 Pain Cycle    → WHAT (Endowus / T-bills / structured notes failing)
C4 Integrity     → WHEN  (past nods)
C5 Mechanism     → WHAT (Stackworks delivering)
C6 Proof Stack   → WHO   (named operator status shift)
C7 Offer + Bonus → WHAT (stack delivers)
C8 Scarcity      → WHEN  (8 spots this quarter)
C9 Guarantee     → WHEN  (future state guaranteed)
C10 CTA          → WHO   (future-self)
C11 FAQ          → mixed
C12 PS           → WHO   (threshold fear of remaining current-self)
```

## Phase 0.7 HITL gate

One gate, one screen. Operator confirms or adjusts:

1. Mechanism sentence (A.1) + named alternatives list (A.2)
2. Top-ranked MAGIC name (B) — operator may swap from candidates
3. Stack item list (C.3) — operator approves or trims
4. Bouquet-narrow segment (D.2) + top-3 transfer-influence picks (D.1)
5. Scene assignment table (E.4) — operator may flip 1-2 scene types

Single-gate not five-sub-gates: the five concerns interlock — changing the bouquet-narrow segment cascades into the scene picker and the stack. Splitting the gate makes the operator re-confirm the same decision multiple times. One screen, all five outputs visible, one approve / adjust / reject button.

If operator rejects: loop back to A. If adjusts: incorporate, re-render screen, re-confirm. If approves: hand the full Phase 0.7 document to Phase 1 drafters.
