# Mechanism

## What a mechanism is (plain English)

A mechanism is the *why* of your offer — the one specific reason your product makes its promise come true. The thing only you do, that no rival does the same way.

It lives in two layers:

- **Upstream (before drafting).** You decide what the mechanism is, its name, what it discredits, the stack around it, which buyer it speaks to, and which scene each letter component uses. This is Phase 0.7. Skip it and the drafter improvises — you get "most agents fail to follow up" instead of "Endowus runs passive index, no protection in regime breaks." One is a vibe, the other is a structural claim.
- **In-letter (during drafting).** Component 5 reveals the mechanism on the page. The reader must close that section thinking "I see why this produces a different result than the old way." Naming is not enough — they need cause-and-effect logic.

Both layers are required.

# Part 1 — Phase 0.7: Architecting the mechanism

Five sub-steps. Runs after Phase 0.5 (Claim Audit), before Phase 1 (Parallel Drafting). One HITL gate at the end.

## A. Unique Characteristic + Discredit Old Solutions

Write one sentence in this shape: `X works because of Y, which Z [named alternatives] cannot do because [structural failure mode].`

- **Name 3–5 alternatives.** Real brand or product names. Not "most coaches" — that reads as cope.
- **Each failure mode must pass three tests:** (1) a competitor would feel obligated to defend it, (2) it names a math, process, incentive, or system reason — not a vibe, (3) the buyer can verify it with one Google search.

Worked example:

> Stackworks works because each portfolio has explicit downside-protection rails set at allocation time, which Endowus, T-bills, and private banker structured notes cannot do because: Endowus runs passive index — no protection in regime breaks like Q4 2018 or 2022. DBS T-bills cap at the SG risk-free rate (~3%). Structured notes carry 2–3% fee drag plus opaque OTC pricing.

## B. MAGIC Name composition (3 candidates)

MAGIC is a checklist for naming the mechanism. Five letters, five questions the name must answer: **M**agnetic reason (why this, why now?), **A**vatar (which buyer segment?), **G**oal (dream outcome), **I**nterval (timeline), **C**ontainer (the word the buyer remembers).

Container word rules: two syllables ideal, three max. Not a saturated noun ("system," "framework," "blueprint"). Either an invented compound, a borrowed concrete object, or a verb-as-noun. Pronounceable on first read.

Worked example: **The 4-Year Stackworks Allocation** — M: capital protection in current regime, A: SG founders post-exit, G: $526K every 4 years, I: 4 years, C: Stackworks (invented compound). Beats "Liquidity Ladder" (Ladder is saturated in fixed income) and "Capital Rail" (flat).

## C. Grand Slam Offer composition (stack)

Hormozi's value equation: `Value = (Dream Outcome × Perceived Likelihood) ÷ (Time Delay × Effort & Sacrifice)`. Every stack item must move at least one of the four levers.

Steps: (1) list 8–12 micro-problems between the buyer's current state and the dream outcome, (2) write one solution per problem, (3) bundle into 5–9 stack items, each tagged to a value-equation lever, (4) add a "next-problem pre-empt" item for the follow-on need your mechanism creates.

Worked stack: Core: 4-Year Stackworks Allocation (Dream Outcome↑). Items: quarterly regime-break review (Likelihood↑), SG tax structuring (Effort↓), direct portfolio dashboard (Effort↓), 12-month liquidity gate (Time Delay↓), estate handoff template (Likelihood↑). Next-problem pre-empt: annual rebalance with the operator, not an associate (Effort↓).

## D. Branding via Association + Bouquet narrowing

**Branding via association** means listing 3–5 brands, people, or methodologies the buyer ALREADY trusts that your mechanism can be paired with. Positional pairing only — never claim endorsement. Example: pair Stackworks with Ray Dalio's All Weather thesis, Yale endowment allocation, Singapore GIC's defensive-growth posture.

**Bouquet narrowing** means picking ONE buyer segment to speak to. Not all five avatars on file. Not "primarily segment 2 with some 3." One. A letter that addresses everyone ends up specific to nobody. Example: "SG founder, post-exit (5–7 figures liquid), 38–52, second-generation immigrant family."

## E. What-Who-When scene picker

For each of the 12 letter components, assign one of three scene types: **What** (Nightmare vs Dream Outcome — drives Pain Cycle, Mechanism, Offer), **Who** (status shift among family, peers, rivals — drives Lead, Proof, PS), **When** (consequence of indecision across past, present, future — drives Integrity Tie-Down, Scarcity, FAQ).

Default mapping: C1 Headline→What, C2 Lead→Who, C3 Pain Cycle→What, C4 Integrity→When, C5 Mechanism→What, C6 Proof→Who, C7 Offer→What, C8 Scarcity→When, C9 Guarantee→When, C10 CTA→Who, C11 FAQ→mixed, C12 PS→Who. Operator may flip 1–2.

## Phase 0.7 HITL gate

One screen. Operator confirms or adjusts all five outputs (mechanism sentence, MAGIC name, stack, bouquet + transfer influence, scene assignment) in a single approve / adjust / reject. Splitting the gate makes the operator re-confirm the same decision multiple times because the five interlock.

# Part 2 — Component 5: Revealing the mechanism in the letter

Component 5 has **5 jobs**, not 4:

1. **Name** the system (the MAGIC name from Phase 0.7, Part 1 step B)
2. **Describe** function, not tools
3. **Visualize + compress** the journey
4. **Justify** why the mechanism works (the addition — see patterns below)
5. **Anchor** with numbers + time

The reader should close the section thinking: *"I see why this produces a different result than the old way."* Naming alone is a confidence signal — not a conviction signal. Serious buyers want cause-and-effect logic.

## The 4 justification patterns (pick 1–2)

**A — Cause-and-Effect Chain.** Each step's result explained by its cause. Example: "Step 1 — rebuild landing page using a 6-second comprehension test. This works because 76% of cold traffic decides to click or leave within 6 seconds."

**B — Contrast Against Conventional.** Show what everyone else does and why it underperforms. Example: "Most mortgage brokers chase pre-approvals by volume — 50 leads in, 2 closings out. 96% are tire-kickers. Instead, this method pre-qualifies with 4 income-doc checks before the first call."

**C — First-Principles Reasoning.** Derive the method from a foundational truth. Example: "Cold traffic on Meta sees your ad for 1.7 seconds. Which means the first frame does 80% of the work. Which is why this method tests visual hooks before headlines."

**D — Constraint Acknowledgment.** Name the tradeoff and why it favors the reader. Example: "This method gives up 'scale fast, worry about close rate later.' For a solo agent, 30 appointments that don't close is worse than 8 that do."

**Pattern picker.** Broad skeptical audience → B. Technical buyer (SaaS, finance, consulting) → A+C. Trade or niche pro (roofing, real estate) → B+D. Coaching or info product → C. Health, wellness, regulated → A.

**Length calibration.** 800-word letter: 50–80 justification words. 1,200 words: 80–150. 1,800+ words: 150–250.

## Verbatim classical examples

**Karbo, "Lazy Man's Way to Riches"** — names the mechanism "Dyna/Psyc" outright (invented term). Justification is identity-positioned: Karbo's own life is the proof — millionaire author, nine kids, on the beach. Damaging admissions ("I'll be honest about ad cost") double as proof. *(`research/classical-exemplars/annotated/karbo-lazy-mans-way.md`)*

**Schwartz, "Burn Disease Out of Your Body"** — mechanism "modern Chinese Medicine" + palm-of-hand self-massage. Stacks ancient authority (Chinese medicine) with modern safety (the "modern" prefix). Hits novelty and tradition at once. *(`research/classical-exemplars/annotated/schwartz-burn-disease.md`)*

**Ogilvy, Rolls-Royce** — mechanism is "patient attention to detail." Every numbered point demonstrates it (98 separate ordeals, 7 hours at full throttle, 5 coats of primer hand-rubbed). Pattern A in the wild. *(`research/classical-exemplars/annotated/ogilvy-rolls-royce.md`)*

**Halbert, Coat of Arms** — mechanism NOT named. The product itself is the deliverable. Authority is implied ("ancient heraldic archives," "seven centuries"). Shows that naming is not mandatory when the product carries the conviction. *(`research/classical-exemplars/annotated/halbert-coat-of-arms.md`)*

**Damien Tan, "Entry Price Pattern"** — mechanism framed as a thesis, not a process: "Transformation doesn't predict profit. Entry price does." Turns the mechanism into an idea the buyer can subscribe to. *(`references/copy-gems.md`, gem #8)*

## Gap evidence — modern letters skip named mechanisms

Across the 8 Stream A letters in `research/strong-exemplars/annotated/`, only 2 had a fully named mechanism: **03 RoofGrow** ("4-Step Google Ads Secrets") and **04 Green Industry** ("7 Sales Laws"). The other 6 (Brendon Luu, three Syncom variants, George Ten, Syncom Polk County) carry implicit mechanisms only — "vast network of lenders," "$50 / 48 hours / one correctly structured cold test," "local Polk County residents." Trust is earned through volume claims, credentials, and process — not through a branded mechanism.

Named mechanism is not mandatory. But when missing, the letter must work harder elsewhere (proof, identity, transparency). Most modern letters skip naming AND skip the extra work — and feel weaker for it.

## Reviewer checks

### Phase 0.7 architecture checks

1. **Named alternatives present?** Mechanism sentence names 3–5 real brand or product alternatives, not categories. *(yes / no)*
2. **Failure modes structural?** Each alternative's failure mode names a math, process, incentive, or system reason — a competitor would feel obligated to defend it. *(yes / no)*
3. **Container word survives the rules?** MAGIC name's container is not a saturated noun, is two-to-three syllables, and pronounceable cold. *(yes / no)*
4. **One bouquet segment?** Letter addresses one avatar from `clients/<slug>/avatars/`, not "primarily 1 with some 2." *(yes / no)*

### Component 5 reveal checks

5. **Justification present?** Mechanism section uses at least one of patterns A, B, C, or D. *(yes / no)*
6. **Cause-and-effect, not re-assertion?** Justification gives a verifiable reason — not "it works because it works." *(yes / no)*
7. **Length calibrated?** Justification word count matches the letter length table. *(yes / no)*
8. **Reader can articulate the why?** After reading, the reader can say in one sentence why this beats the old way. *(yes / no, score 1–5)*
