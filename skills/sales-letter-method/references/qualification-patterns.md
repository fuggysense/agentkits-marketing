# Qualification Patterns

The teardown flagged "limited explicit filtering" as a conversion cost. Broad-market persuasion generates volume but drops close rates. Premium letters earn trust by being selective.

**Rule:** Qualification is now a **required** component. Placement is flexible (Lead, mid-body, or pre-CTA) but it must appear.

---

## Why Qualification Converts

Serious buyers mistrust offers that claim to work for everyone. Selective offers read as:
- Expert — the operator understands who they can deliver for
- Honest — not chasing revenue blindly
- High-ticket — mass-market offers don't qualify, premium ones do

Selectivity lowers tire-kicker volume and raises show-up rate, close rate, and lifetime value per lead.

---

## The 3 Qualification Blocks

Pick one, two, or all three depending on letter length and offer complexity.

### Block 1: "Who This Is For"

**Template:**
```
This works best for [role/stage/situation] who:

- [Condition 1 — concrete, testable]
- [Condition 2 — concrete, testable]
- [Condition 3 — concrete, testable]
- [Condition 4 — optional, softer fit signal]
```

**Good example:**
```
This works best for SG property agents who:
- Have closed at least 3 transactions in the last 12 months
- Are currently relying on cold calling or referral-only pipelines
- Can commit 30 minutes daily to lead response in the first 60 days
- Are comfortable being coached, not just consulted
```

**Bad example (too vague):**
```
This is for ambitious agents who want to grow their business.
```

Every condition must be **testable** — the reader should be able to tick yes or no with no ambiguity.

### Block 2: "Who This Isn't For"

This is the highest-trust signal in the letter. Stronger than any guarantee.

**Template:**
```
This won't work for you if:

- [Disqualifier 1 — stage or resource based]
- [Disqualifier 2 — mindset or behavior based]
- [Disqualifier 3 — fit-based, acknowledges real limitation]
```

**Good example:**
```
This won't work for you if:
- You're looking for overnight results (our clients typically see traction in 60-90 days)
- You're not willing to rework your current sales scripts
- You've never handled a $500K+ client before (we work one stage up from that)
```

**Anti-patterns:**
- Fake disqualifiers designed to make everyone feel qualified ("This isn't for you if you don't want to succeed")
- Disqualifiers that are just veiled insults ("not for lazy people")
- Disqualifiers that conflict with the Headline's promise

### Block 3: Readiness Criteria

Shifts the question from "can this help me?" to "am I ready?"

**Template:**
```
You're ready for this if:

- You've [demonstrated baseline competence]
- You have [minimum resource threshold]
- You're committed to [specific action pattern]
- You understand [realistic expectation]
```

Best placed immediately before CTA — frames the next step as a decision of readiness, not interest.

---

## Placement Strategy

| Letter length | Recommended placement |
|---------------|----------------------|
| 800-1,200 words | One qualification block in the Lead or mid-body |
| 1,200-1,800 words | "Who this is for" in Lead + Readiness Criteria pre-CTA |
| 1,800+ words | All three blocks, spread: "For" in Lead, "Not for" after Offer, Readiness before final CTA |

---

## Tone Calibration

Qualification must feel like **honest filtering**, not exclusion theater.

**Calibration scale:**
- **Too soft:** reads like a brochure disclaimer nobody reads
- **Right:** reads like a founder protecting their delivery quality
- **Too hard:** reads like gatekeeping for the sake of it, shrinks pipeline unnecessarily

The register to hit: *professional restraint.* Think private-practice specialist, not nightclub doorman.

---

## Integration With Existing Components

- **Lead (Component 2):** First qualification block can replace or complement the Dream Outcome paragraph — sharpens relevance immediately
- **Offer Breakdown (Component 7):** Qualification blocks can sit after offer specification ("This is what it is. Here's who it's for.")
- **FAQ.Preference (Component 12):** "Will this work for my specific situation?" — point back to qualification block ("See who this is for above")
- **CTA (Component 11):** Readiness Criteria reframes the button as a fit check

---

## Failure Modes (auto-reject)

- No qualification block anywhere in the letter
- Qualification that is vague (no testable conditions)
- "Not for you" block that is actually flattery ("not for the uncommitted")
- Qualification that contradicts the Headline (promise of "anyone can" + "this isn't for beginners")
- Over-qualifying — if the letter feels like a rejection letter, pipeline collapses

---

## Diagnostic Test

Before Conversion Gate approves:

- [ ] At least one explicit qualification block present
- [ ] All conditions in "Who this is for" are testable (reader can tick yes/no)
- [ ] At least one real disqualifier appears somewhere (if a "Not for you" block is used)
- [ ] Qualification tone matches letter tone (not out-of-voice gatekeeping)
- [ ] Qualification is consistent with Headline promise (no contradiction)
- [ ] If offer is consultation-level, Readiness Criteria appears before final CTA
