# Trust Density

The teardown's core finding: most sales letters rely on **confidence** (bold claims, proof, authority) but under-invest in **credibility** (transparency, constraint disclosure, realistic framing). Sophisticated buyers convert when the page feels credible, not just confident.

**Definition:** Trust density = number of credibility signals per 200 words. Low-trust letters run hot with claims, no grounding. High-trust letters mix claims with disclosure, specificity, and realistic constraint.

---

## The 10 Trust Signals

Use at least **5 of 10** across any long letter. Distribute — don't cluster.

### 1. Constraint disclosure
State what the offer **can't** do or **won't** cover. Signals the operator isn't overclaiming.
- *"This doesn't replace your listing agent. It replaces the cold-lead chase."*
- *"We don't handle accounting — you'll still need your CPA."*

### 2. Realistic timeline
Specific, bounded, with downside acknowledged.
- *"Most clients see first results in 30-60 days. Some take 90. Nobody sees traction in week one."*
- Avoid: *"Fast results!" / "See results immediately"*

### 3. Named conditions for success
What has to be true on the client's side for the method to work.
- *"This works if you can respond to inbound leads within 2 hours. If your response time is 24+ hours, none of this matters — fix that first."*

### 4. Transparent process
Describe what happens operationally, step by step.
- *"Step 1 — we audit your last 30 days of ads. Step 2 — we rebuild your landing page using [method]. Step 3 — we test 3 creative angles over 2 weeks."*

### 5. Proof with context
Every result names the circumstances.
- *"$526K GCI in 4 years — starting from 8 transactions in year 1, in a HDB-dominant district."*
- Avoid: *"$526K generated!" (context-free)*

### 6. Who it isn't for (see qualification-patterns.md)
Direct disqualifiers lower skepticism more than any testimonial.

### 7. Founder / operator realism
Acknowledge limitations, mistakes, or learning curve.
- *"We've run this method for 3 years. The first version took 9 months to ship and failed. This is version 4."*

### 8. Specific numbers over round numbers
Specificity is credibility.
- *"3,247 appointments" > "3,000+ appointments"*
- *"$69/day ad spend" > "minimal spend"*

### 9. Operational visibility
Show the machinery behind the offer.
- Screenshots of actual dashboards
- Process diagrams
- "Here's what the onboarding email looks like"

### 10. Appropriate hedging
Avoid absolute claims where outcomes vary.
- *"Most clients see X. Some see more. A few see less."*
- Avoid: *"You will get X."* (unless literally guaranteed by contract)

## Density Calibration

Count trust signals per 200 words in the draft. Target:

| Letter length | Minimum signals | Ideal density |
|---------------|-----------------|---------------|
| 800 words | 5 total | ~1 per 160 words |
| 1,200 words | 7 total | ~1 per 170 words |
| 2,000 words | 10 total | ~1 per 200 words |

Below minimum → letter reads as hype.
Above 2× ideal → letter reads as defensive or uncertain.

## The Confidence-Credibility Ratio

For every confident claim (bold outcome, specific number, authority signal), pair it with a credibility signal within the next 3 sentences.

**Pattern:**
```
[Confident claim with specific number]. [Credibility signal: constraint, context, condition, or realism].
```

**Good example:**
```
(b)This system booked 3,247 consultations across 367 clients in the last 18 months.(b)
That's an average of 8.8 per client, per month — (h)though new clients typically hit that rate by month 2, not month 1.(h)
```

**Bad example (confidence without credibility):**
```
This system books thousands of appointments every month. Results are incredible.
```

---

## Markup Convention For Trust Signals

Trust signals rarely need `(h)highlight(h)` — highlight is reserved for outcome desire words. Trust signals should feel like quiet parentheticals, not shouted claims.

- Use plain copy for constraint disclosure
- Use `(b)bold(b)` sparingly on specific numbers (not on hedges)
- Italics (handled naturally in markdown `*word*`) for operator realism and concession

---

## Anti-Patterns

- **Trust theater:** performative humility that's really a flex (*"We only work with the top 1%"*)
- **Over-hedging:** every claim qualified to meaninglessness (*"Results may or may not happen, depending on factors"*)
- **Generic credibility:** *"We care about our clients"* — every brand says this
- **Confidence + no credibility anywhere:** the letter reads as a pitch, not a case

---

## Diagnostic Test

Before Conversion Gate approves:

- [ ] At least 5 distinct trust signal types used (across the 10 above)
- [ ] Trust signals distributed across 3+ different components (not all in one section)
- [ ] Every major confident claim has a credibility pairing within 3 sentences
- [ ] At least one constraint disclosure present ("this doesn't / can't / won't")
- [ ] At least one "conditions for success" statement present
- [ ] Specific numbers used in at least 3 places (not rounded)
- [ ] No absolute outcome promises without guarantee backing
