# Gary Halbert — Coat of Arms Letter

**Source:** swiped.co/file/coat-of-arms-letter-by-gary-halbert/ • internationalbubble.com/blog/the-best-sales-letter-ever/ • Halbert's own newsletter explaining the strategy
**Year ran:** 1971 onward, mailed for 30+ years
**Volume:** 600,000,000+ pieces mailed (most-mailed direct mail piece in history)
**Format:** 1-page personal-looking direct mail letter in plain hand-addressed envelope, first-class postage

## Strategic context (from Halbert's own newsletter)
- He was broke, no running water, no electricity, wife about to leave. "Do or die letter."
- A-pile / B-pile theory: people sort mail standing over a wastebasket. Anything that looks promotional → B-pile (trash). Anything that looks like personal mail → A-pile (opened).
- Inspired by a newspaper article about an old lady making personalized family-crest paintings sold via direct mail.
- He targeted specific common last names (Macdonald, Smith, Johnson, etc.) using phone books to build the list. The letter referenced their exact family name.
- 11 "hidden persuaders" embedded — feminine handwriting (Nancy, his then-wife, was the signatory), plain envelope, no business name, no order card, hand-addressed, first-class, name dropped multiple times, etc.

## Full letter text (variant addressed to Macdonald)

> **Dear Mr. Macdonald,**
>
> Did you know that your family name was recorded with a coat-of-arms in ancient heraldic archives more than seven centuries ago?
>
> My husband and I discovered this while doing some research for some friends of ours who have the same last name as you do.
>
> We've had an artist recreate the coat-of-arms exactly as described in the ancient records. This drawing, along with other information about the name, has been printed up into an attractive one-page report.
>
> The bottom half of the report tells the story of the very old and distinguished family name of Macdonald. It tells what the name means, its origin, the original family motto, its place in history and about famous people who share it.
>
> The top half has a large, beautiful reproduction of an artist's drawing of the earliest known coat-of-arms for the name of Macdonald.
>
> Framed, these reports make distinctive wall decorations and they are great gifts for relatives.
>
> It should be remembered that we have not traced anyone's individual family tree but have researched back through several centuries to find out about the earliest people named Macdonald.
>
> All we are asking for them is enough to cover the added expenses of having the extra copies printed and mailed.
>
> If you are interested, please let us know right away as our supply is pretty slim. Just verify that we have your correct name and address and send the correct amount in cash or check for the number of reports you want. We'll send them promptly by return mail.
>
> Sincerely,
>
> **P.S.** If you are ordering only one report, send two dollars ($2.00). Additional reports ordered at the same time and sent to the same address are one dollar each. Please make checks payable to me, Nancy L. Halbert.

## Why it printed money
- Cheap product (~$2), high-volume mail, near-zero refusal: the letter mentions "your name" 6+ times.
- Pre-internet, family-history curiosity was unscratched. The "we did this for some friends" framing is brilliant social proof + scarcity.
- Order mechanic ("send cash or check") feels low-friction and personal vs. corporate.
- P.S. carries the entire pricing/CTA — main body never mentions price.
