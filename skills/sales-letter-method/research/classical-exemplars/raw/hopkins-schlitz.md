# Claude Hopkins — Schlitz Beer "Washed With Live Steam"

**Source:** oncopy.substack.com/p/how-claude-hopkins-saved-schlitz • foolonahill.com/schiltz.html • Hopkins' own *My Life in Advertising* (1927)
**Year:** ~1919 (early scientific-advertising era)
**Status:** Schlitz went from 5th place to tied 1st in U.S. beer market in a few months. The "demonstration of the obvious" technique is one of the most studied moves in advertising.
**Vertical:** Consumer packaged goods (beer)

## Strategic context
All breweries were screaming "PURE" in their ads in giant type — taking out double-page spreads just to enlarge that one word. None explained what "pure" meant. Hopkins toured the Schlitz factory and discovered:
- Plate-glass rooms with filtered air for cooling beer
- White-wood pulp filters
- Every pump and pipe cleaned twice daily
- Each bottle sterilized four times before being filled
- 4,000-foot artesian wells for water (even though Lake Michigan was right there)
- Mother yeast cell — product of 1,200 experiments — propagated for every batch

Hopkins asked the factory exec, *"Why don't you tell people these things?"*
Answer: *"Because every beer manufacturer does it the same way."*
Hopkins: *"But others have never told this story."*

## The principle this letter established
> **Demonstration of the obvious wins.** If your competitors all do the same thing but never explain it, the first one to explain wins the "expert" perception forever.

## Approximate ad copy (reconstructed from multiple period accounts; verbatim originals are in archives)

> **HOW WE BREW SCHLITZ**
>
> Could you but see how Schlitz beer is brewed, you would never buy any other.
>
> You would see plate-glass rooms, filled with filtered air, where we cool our beer in purity.
>
> You would see immense filters filled with white-wood pulp, through which the beer is filtered. You would see how every pump and pipe is cleaned, twice daily, to keep them sweet.
>
> You would see every bottle washed four times by machinery. You would see them sterilized by *live steam* after they are sealed.
>
> You would see our 4,000-foot artesian wells, drilled into the rock to bring us pure water — though our brewery stands by the side of Lake Michigan.
>
> You would see the original mother yeast cell — born of a thousand experiments to bring out the finest flavor — and now used in every drop of Schlitz beer.
>
> You would realize then why Schlitz beer costs us so much to brew. You would understand that *pure* beer is impossible at less than this.
>
> But you cannot see all this. So we must ask you to take our word, backed by our 50-year reputation, and to ask for the Brewery Bottling.
>
> See that the cork or crown is branded **Schlitz** — *The Beer That Made Milwaukee Famous*.

## Standout pattern
- No headline benefit. The headline is *process*: how we brew.
- Body = scannable list of factual, sensory details (plate glass, white-wood pulp, live steam, 4,000-foot wells, mother yeast cell, 1,200 experiments).
- Each detail is *specific* (numbers, materials, processes) — not vague.
- The competitive frame is buried: "every brewer does this, but no one has told the story." The reader infers premium positioning without Hopkins ever claiming it.
- CTA = look for the brand mark. No urgency, no offer. Pure positioning play.
