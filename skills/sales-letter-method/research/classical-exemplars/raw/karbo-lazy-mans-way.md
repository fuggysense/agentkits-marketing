# Joe Karbo — "The Lazy Man's Way to Riches"

**Source:** harrisonbarnes.com/the-lazy-mans-way-to-riches/ • copywriting1.com/2007/06/lazy-mans-way-to-riches.html • warriorforum threads • Karbo's own 1975 Capitalist Reporter interview
**Year:** First ran 1973 (Karbo wrote it in ~2 hours)
**Format:** Full-page newspaper/magazine ad with mail-order coupon
**Status:** 2.7M+ copies of the book sold at $10 each ($50M+ gross). Karbo made $300K/yr at peak. He tried for years to write a beating control — never succeeded. The ad ran in hundreds of magazines and major newspapers for over a decade.
**Vertical:** Self-help / make-money-from-home

## Headline + sub
> ## The Lazy Man's Way to Riches
>
> *"Most People Are Too Busy Earning a Living to Make Any Money"*

## Opening (verified verbatim, multiple sources)

> I used to work hard. The 18-hour days. The 7-day weeks.
>
> But I didn't start making big money until I did less — a lot less.
>
> For example, this ad took about two hours to write. With a little luck, it should earn me fifty, maybe a hundred thousand dollars.
>
> What's more, I'm going to ask you to send me ten dollars for something that'll cost me no more than 50 cents.
>
> And I'll try to make it so irresistible that you'd be a darned fool not to do it.

## The damaging-admission move (paragraph 3-4)
Karbo opens by *admitting* that:
1. The ad cost him ~$0 (2 hours) and will earn him five-figures
2. He's about to ask for $10 for a 50-cent product
3. He's going to *try to manipulate you* into doing it

This is a textbook damaging admission used to disarm skepticism.

## "It's Not's" section
Karbo lists what his method is **NOT**:
- It's not chain letters
- It's not stocks, bonds or any other get-rich-quick scheme
- It's not real estate, mail order, franchises
- It's not gambling
- It's not "positive thinking"
- It's not selling

(This bracket-removal technique appears repeatedly through the ad.)

## The guarantee (legendary)

> What if I'm so sure that you'll make money my Lazy Man's Way that I'll make you the world's most unusual guarantee? And here it is:
>
> **I won't even cash your check or money order for 31 days after I've sent you my material.**
>
> That'll give you plenty of time to get it, look it over, try it out. If you don't agree that it's worth at least a hundred times what you invested, send it back. Your uncashed check or money order will be promptly returned.

**No refund — escrow.** Karbo doesn't *return* your money; he *never deposits it.* This is structurally stronger than a money-back guarantee because the friction of "asking for a refund" is eliminated.

## CTA (in Karbo's voice as the reader's reply)

> "Joe, you may be full of beans, but what have I got to lose? Send me the Lazy Man's Way to Riches. But don't deposit my check or money order for 31 days after it's in the mail."

## Structural notes
- Headline + subhead = paradox (lazy + riches) + permission ("most people too busy")
- Opens with story-as-proof, not product
- Damaging admission early (transparency)
- "It's not's" removes wrong mental brackets
- Defers product description until trust is established
- Single most-quoted move: the no-cash-check escrow guarantee
- One-step direct response: ad → check in mail → book delivered
