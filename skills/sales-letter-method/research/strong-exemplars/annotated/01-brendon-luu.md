- Exact opening: > "I Will Help You Get A Mortgage APPROVED & CLOSED For Your New Home in 17 Days Or Less Using My 'Vast Network' Of Lenders All Over America MAKING SURE You Get The Best Rates Ever"

## Lead type (per 6 classical leads)
- Type: promise (with mechanism named in the headline itself)
- How it executes: The headline is the offer. Body letter then re-litigates the promise across three layers — speed (17 days), price (best rates), and ease (effortless qualification).

## Standout pattern
The "enemy framing" against retail banks is the structural backbone. Brendon doesn't just sell himself — he positions every retail bank Loan Originator as the adversary, then makes shopping wholesale his unique mechanism. This converts an undifferentiated service (mortgage brokerage) into a values-driven choice. The move worth stealing: pick an obvious-but-unspoken industry enemy and let your reader feel like working with you is an act of resistance.

## Failures / weaknesses (be honest)
- No P.S. block — leaves a long letter without a final urgency or recap layer.
- No scarcity/urgency mechanism. A "I only take 10 clients per month" line would lift conversion.
- Mechanism is unnamed. "Vast Network" is a description, not a brand. Compare to George Ten's "$50 / 48 hours / one clean test" — that's a proprietary mechanism. Brendon's letter would compound if he named his 2-step system (e.g. "The 17-Day Wholesale Mortgage Method").
- Proof is aggregate ("500+") not granular. No specific client closing stories with names + dollar savings.
- Headline is double-stacked (two H1s with "MAKING SURE / You Get The Best Rates Ever") — visually clumsy, suggests template copy-paste error.
