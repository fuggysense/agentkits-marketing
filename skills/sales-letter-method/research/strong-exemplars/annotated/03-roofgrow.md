## Hook
- Type: declarative + visual metaphor ("FLOODGATES" / "phones ringing off the hook")
- Exact opening: > "OPEN THE FLOODGATES OF 'QUALIFIED LOCAL ROOFING JOBS' USING GOOGLE ADS™ SECRETS AND GET CONSISTENT LEAD FLOW FOR YOUR ROOFING BUSINESS"
- Why it works: Concrete visual ("floodgates" + "ringing off the hook") that a tradesperson would actually picture. "Qualified" addresses the #1 objection (most agencies send junk leads). "Google Ads™" with the TM signals niche specificity over generic "marketing."

## Lead type
- Type: promise + problem-solution (3% of agencies can do this; we are that 3%)
- How it executes: Frames Google Ads as a "machine less than 3% of marketing agencies on this planet know how to build." Then proves with stacked case studies before getting to the 4-step mechanism.

## Standout pattern
"Named case studies with city + business name + specific number" is the entire proof spine. DTH Roofing (NY) → 25 calls/day. Mike's Roofing (NM) → $1M/6mo. Cool Roofs (OH) → 20 closes/mo. Keystone → $300k/30 days. Each one is short, named, geographic, and quantified. The move worth stealing: when selling B2B services, build a "stat-locked case-study bullet stack" — every case is one line: [Business name, city] → [single most-impressive metric]. Letter doesn't drown the reader; it stacks proof like dominos.

## Failures / weaknesses (be honest)
- No guarantee. For a roofer being asked to spend $5-10K/mo on ad management, a risk reversal would lift this letter significantly.
- No P.S.
- No scarcity. "We only work with one roofer per metro" would write itself.
- Mechanism is named at the section level ("4-Step Google Ads Secrets") but the proper noun isn't reinforced through the letter. By the end the reader can't remember the system name.
- No testimonial video or quote with face attribution — the case studies are written FOR the prospect, not FROM the client.
- "Less than 3% of agencies" is unverifiable puffery — would benefit from a more credible authority signal (Google Partner badge, certifications, etc.).
- Copyright 2021 — letter has been live for 4+ years without refresh, suggesting it's a control they haven't beaten (or they've stopped iterating).
