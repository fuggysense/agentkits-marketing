## Standout pattern
The pricing structure is the entire move. "$97 today. You send another $97 after - only if you think it was worth it. Not charged automatically." This is a structural guarantee superior to money-back: it removes the "request refund" friction entirely and signals confidence asymmetrically. Combined with a credible scarcity reason ("I'm leaving the country"), it produces a buying environment where saying yes feels low-risk AND time-bound. The move worth stealing: when offering a workshop or one-time product, structure the price as a "tip-after" or "split-on-merit" arrangement — it disarms refund anxiety and creates psychological reciprocity.

## Failures / weaknesses (be honest)
- No P.S. block (though the modern stripped format makes this less load-bearing).
- Only 1 named student case (Zora). For a $97-$497 workshop being sold via essay-style copy, a single named case is light. More student wins would compound.
- The mechanism isn't NAMED. There's no "The Ten Method" or "The 48-Hour Validation Test™." George could brand the procedure and reuse it across products — currently it's a generic-sounding "validation workshop."
- The "leaving the country / rebuild everything" scarcity is credible-sounding but unverifiable — sophisticated readers may suspect false scarcity.
- Voice is Hemingway-clipped throughout — works for the modern direct-response audience, may underperform with classical-letter readers expecting more rhythm and warmth.
- Truncation in scrape — likely more proof + FAQs further down that weren't fully captured.
- The "$497 strikethrough → $97" price anchoring is a tactic, but the strikethrough is small and easy to miss in the layout.
