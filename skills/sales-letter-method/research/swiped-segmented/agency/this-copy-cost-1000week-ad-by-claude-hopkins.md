## Mechanism / Proof / CTA / Guarantee / P.S.
**Full Schauer commentary (verbatim):**

> Below is one of the house ads from Lord & Thomas, the famous ad agency run by advertising icon, Albert Lasker. Lasker hired the legendary Claude Hopkins as a copywriter for the agency. In the ad, the writer they're talking about is Claude Hopkins. He made $1000/week, which at the time (1909), was an incredible salary and why it made for such a good headline. More than just a piece of history, this is a great example of selling your services by demonstrating the impressive effort & process that goes into making sure it's of the up-most quality. Claude describes in detail how the talent they hire is the best of the best & what better way to advertise that than by mentioning how their copywriters make $1,000 per week. The ad is offering a free book titled "The New Way in Advertising". They used the book as a lead generation tool, to sell people on the agency. By the way, "The New Way in Advertising" was a very effective title for its time. I say "for its time" because the title sounds generic now. To work today, it would have to be much more specific. However, people are drawn to anything "New" & demonstrating that you know what's "new" in your field, shows that you're an expert. This Copy Costs $1000/Week Ad by Claude Hopkins

**Schauer's "Key Takeaways" (verbatim):**

> Have anyone on your payroll with a huge salary? Oddly enough, it might be a good idea to mention it. It grabs attention & if people are impressed with the salary, they'll assume the service is incredible & more importantly, worth $$$. There is a certain yearning in all of us to go with the best of the best. This type of ad is an example of appealing to this high-end market that is not willing to settle. In other words, if you want to charge high fees, this ad is worth studying!

## Standout pattern
**Narrative-first framing.** Schauer highlights how the letter opens with a personal story before introducing the offer. The mechanic: earn trust before pitching. Works because the buyer reads it as content, not advertising.

## Weaknesses
- **Era-locked language.** Pre-internet direct-mail or print-ad piece. The voice ("Dear Friend", "Cordially yours") and the buyer's reading patterns are 1970s-90s. Modern 2026 buyers skim faster, distrust hype keywords ("amazing", "miracle", "secret"), and won't mail in a coupon. The structural moves transfer; the surface language does not.

## Cross-niche transferability
- **Agency → agency:** direct transfer. Consulting sales letters from Kern/Deiss/Abraham are the closest documented template for Jerel's agency vertical.
- **Agency → info-products:** transfers because most info products are sold by ex-agency owners who teach what they used to do.
- **Agency → B2B SaaS:** partial. Qualification + risk reversal patterns transfer; but "I'll do it for you" framing has to become "the platform does it for you" — different proof requirements.
