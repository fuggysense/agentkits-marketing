**Schauer's "Key Takeaways" (verbatim):**

> If you're advertising mistakes to reel people in, turn those mistakes into benefits & use those benefits to sell your offer. Also, make sure you mention how your offering corrects all of the mistakes you mentioned to avoid. Don't be vague with your social proof. In the bottom landing page, Yodle simply included logos of big companies towards the bottom. In the top version, they got specific & mentioned how they've helped 35,000+ businesses. The more specific you get, the more powerful the proof is. Anyone can paste logos which is why many overlook them.

## Standout pattern
**Takeaway selling.** Removing something from the reader (deadline, availability, eligibility) instead of adding incentive. Per Schauer, this leverages loss aversion — the reader fears missing out more than they want the gain.

## Weaknesses
- **Email sequence is not a single-letter mechanic.** A 5-email sequence spreads belief-building across days; a single sales letter does it in one read. Stealing the sequence requires also stealing the sender's permission asset (list relationship).

## Cross-niche transferability
- **Agency → agency:** direct transfer. Consulting sales letters from Kern/Deiss/Abraham are the closest documented template for Jerel's agency vertical.
- **Agency → info-products:** transfers because most info products are sold by ex-agency owners who teach what they used to do.
- **Agency → B2B SaaS:** partial. Qualification + risk reversal patterns transfer; but "I'll do it for you" framing has to become "the platform does it for you" — different proof requirements.
