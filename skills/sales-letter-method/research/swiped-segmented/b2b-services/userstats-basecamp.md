## Mechanism / Proof / CTA / Guarantee / P.S.
**Full Schauer commentary (verbatim):**

> If you haven't heard of Basecamp, they are a very successful SAAS company with some great marketing minds behind it. Put simply, they know what they're doing & act with purpose. For several years, Basecamp has used different versions of this landing page below with the copy "last week, [#] companies signed up for Basecamp. I love this use of statistics because it is a current number (last week). They pull it off really well, but there are upsides & downsides. The upside is that not only are people impressed by a big number of signups, they also feel like lots of people are signing up now as they're making their buying decision, which is very assuring. The downside is that in order to pull this off effectively, you have to have a significant number of signups coming in & most importantly, you have to figure out a way to update the number. First of all, you should never lie in your marketing. Second, if people see that the number isn't changing, they likely won't believe the number is real & will lose trust in you. It's a tricky use of stats, but can be extremely powerful if your company is built for it. Using Current Stats to Get Signups from Basecamp

**Schauer's "Key Takeaways" (verbatim):**

> Keep in mind that one of the reasons these companies have great numbers is because they offer free trials. Free trials count as users so it makes the number bigger than just paid members. If you want to increase the power of your stats, make them recent. People reading your copy are making a buying decision now so knowing others are buying now is reassuring.

## Weaknesses
- **Landing pages are not sales letters.** Most landing-page swipes on swiped.co rely on social proof + free trial + form, not on building belief through narrative. Pattern transfers only to top-of-funnel free-offer pages, not to high-ticket persuasion.

## Cross-niche transferability
- **B2B/SaaS → B2B/SaaS:** direct transfer for landing-page and free-trial mechanics.
- **B2B/SaaS → agency:** partial — social proof + logo bars + free trial mechanics steal cleanly; but agencies can't offer self-serve trials, so the conversion mechanic differs.
- **B2B/SaaS → info-products:** weak transfer. Info-product buyers respond to aspirational + transformation copy; SaaS buyers respond to feature/proof copy. Different psychology.
