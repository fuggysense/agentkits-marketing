## Mechanism / Proof / CTA / Guarantee / P.S.
**Full Schauer commentary (verbatim):**

> swiped by Mike Schauer There's a reason why top marketers use this testing tactic to reel people in. The idea of taking a test that reveals something you might be doing wrong arouses a great deal of curiosity. It ignites a feeling that if you don't take the test, you might continue to make certain mistakes that the test would help you fix. It also makes you wonder just how good you think you are. For the 1-2 decades we spent in school, our intelligence was largely measured & validated by our test scores. It's ingrained in us to become proud & feel good about acing a test, especially when you know others did not do well. If you study these emails, you'll notice how there are certain similarities that specifically play to the underlying psychology - mentioning many get it wrong, posing the question if they will pass, pointing out what it will mean if you do pass etc. "Take This Test" Emails from Eben Pagan, John Reese & Ryan Deiss

## Weaknesses
- **Email sequence is not a single-letter mechanic.** A 5-email sequence spreads belief-building across days; a single sales letter does it in one read. Stealing the sequence requires also stealing the sender's permission asset (list relationship).
