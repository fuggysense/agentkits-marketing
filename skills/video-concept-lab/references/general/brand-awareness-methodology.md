# Brand / Awareness Video Methodology

**Purpose:** non-DR path. Use this when the brief is brand-building, awareness, trust, recall, or category authority — NOT direct conversion.

**When to load this file (NOT the `direct-response/` stack):**

The brief is brand/awareness if it says:
- "Brand video" / "awareness" / "category education"
- Goal is recall, trust, premium feel, perception
- No specific CTA other than brand association
- Performance metric is brand lift, share-of-voice, follower growth, not conversion
- Audience needs to FEEL something about the brand, not BUY now

The brief is DR (load `direct-response/dr-foundation.md` instead) if it says:
- Cold paid traffic
- Lead-gen or direct purchase
- ROAS / CPA / CAC targets
- Performance ad
- "Convert"

**If the brief is ambiguous, ask the operator: "Is the goal conversion (load DR), or brand lift (load this file)?"**

---

## The non-negotiable brand spine

Every brand concept must reduce to:

> **positioning → emotional anchor → credibility proof → recall**

Different from DR's pain → gap → mechanism → belief shift. Brand work is not about resolving tension — it's about **planting an association** that persists past the view.

---

## Layer 1 — Positioning (FIRST decision)

What does this brand stand for that competitors can't claim? Articulate in one sentence the AUDIENCE could repeat.

Examples:
- "The agent who actually returns your call."
- "The supplement that respects your body's chemistry."
- "The accountant who explains it like you're a friend, not a client."

**If positioning isn't crisp, the brand video has no chance. Reject the brief or ask the operator to sharpen positioning first.**

---

## Layer 2 — Emotional anchor

Brand work installs a feeling, not a transaction. What is the ONE emotional residue the viewer should carry after watching?

Common anchors:
- **Trust** ("she's reliable")
- **Aspiration** ("I want to be like her")
- **Belonging** ("these are my people")
- **Quiet confidence** ("they have their act together")
- **Warmth** ("they care about me as a person")
- **Authority** ("they know what they're doing")

Pick ONE. Brand videos that try to anchor 3+ feelings fail — viewer remembers none.

---

## Layer 3 — Credibility proof

What makes the positioning believable WITHOUT directly selling?

Brand proof differs from DR proof — it's contextual, not transactional:
- **Track record:** "12 years in the neighborhood"
- **Recognition:** "Featured in Tatler"
- **Volume:** "Over 200 families" (not "over 200 buyers")
- **Specificity:** showing actual workspace, actual team, actual workflow
- **Restraint:** what they DON'T do, not what they do

**Avoid testimonials with conversion-flavored language** ("she helped me close in 30 days"). Brand testimonials should sound like recommendations between friends, not endorsements.

---

## Layer 4 — Recall

What's the ONE visual or phrase the viewer should remember 24 hours later?

- A signature visual cue (her specific workspace, her car, her color palette)
- A signature phrase (a tagline she actually uses with clients)
- A signature ritual (how she opens every viewing)

Recall is the brand video's CTR equivalent. If you can't name the one element the viewer takes away, you don't have a brand video — you have content.

---

## Body structure by duration (brand)

Different from DR collapse rules. Brand videos earn their length differently.

| Duration | Structure |
|---|---|
| **15s** | Single emotional moment + brand signature. No story arc. |
| **30-60s** | Emotional anchor scene + credibility beat + recall close |
| **60-90s** | Mini-narrative: setup the WHO + show the HOW + signature close |
| **2-3 min (brand film)** | Full character / story arc — earns the runtime through emotional depth, not info delivery |

---

## What to avoid in brand videos (anti-patterns)

| Anti-pattern | Why it kills brand work |
|---|---|
| **Pain → gap → mechanism → belief shift** structure | That's DR. Reads as transactional. Erodes brand feeling. |
| Specific "before / after" testimonials | Conversion-flavored. Belongs in DR. |
| Open loops + cliffhangers | Brand work is about emotional resolution, not unanswered tension |
| CTAs with urgency ("DM me NOW for a free consultation") | Kills the brand register. Brand videos either have no CTA or a soft one. |
| Stats and numbers as proof | Cold. Brand is felt, not calculated. |
| Generic "uplifting music + montage of smiling people" | Looks like every other corporate brand video. Specificity is what makes brand work memorable. |

---

## Format selection for brand (looser than DR)

Brand work tolerates more format variety because the goal is emotional, not mechanical:

- **Documentary-style** — for trust, authority, real-world credibility
- **Cinematic** — for aspiration, premium positioning
- **Direct-to-camera (founder/figure)** — for personality-driven brands
- **Day-in-the-life vlog** — for relatability, accessibility
- **Mood film (no dialogue)** — for premium / aspirational / luxury

**One discipline to keep:** even in brand mode, the format must serve the emotional anchor. Don't pick "cinematic" just because it looks expensive — pick it because the anchor is aspiration.

---

## Concept-vs-content test (brand)

1. ✅ Is positioning crisp enough to repeat in one sentence?
2. ✅ Is there ONE emotional anchor (not 3)?
3. ✅ Is credibility proof contextual (not transactional)?
4. ✅ Is there ONE recall element the viewer takes away?
5. ✅ Does the format serve the emotional anchor?
6. ✅ Is the runtime EARNED by emotional depth (not info delivery)?

Any NO = not a brand concept. Reject. Re-brief.

---

## When brand and DR blur

Some campaigns straddle — a brand-built piece that ALSO has a conversion goal. Two approaches:

1. **Sequence them:** brand video runs first (builds awareness + recall), DR video runs second (converts the warmed audience). Don't combine in one asset.
2. **Pick the dominant goal:** if you must run one asset, pick whether brand or DR is the primary objective and let it lead. Hybrid assets usually do both jobs badly.
