# Stakes Hook Examples

High risk. What's on the line.

## Operator-Supplied Examples

- "My mom's business will fail if I don't do something about it."
- "We lose this $600k and we're done before we turn 23."
- "I bet my friend dinner — whoever scores higher on the test after I only study one hour."
- "If this doesn't work, my mom loses everything she built for 30 years."

## Why These Work

| Example | Stakes mechanism |
|---|---|
| Mom's business will fail | Family + failure + time pressure. Three stakes layers in one line. |
| Lose $600k before 23 | Financial loss + youth + hard deadline = maximum urgency |
| Bet for dinner | Low-dollar but high-ego stakes. Personal pride on the line. |
| Mom loses 30 years | Legacy + irreversibility. Can't undo 30 years. |

## Stakes Types

| Stake type | Examples |
|---|---|
| Financial | Losing money, going broke, burning through savings |
| Identity | Failure redefines who you are ("done before we turn 23") |
| Relational | Letting down family, parents, partner |
| Legacy | Losing what was built over years/decades |
| Time | Hard deadlines, running out of time, irreversible decisions |

## Stakes vs. Sensationalism

Stakes emphasize what's at risk if nothing changes. Sensationalism emphasizes what's possible. Both create urgency — via different emotional channels.

Stakes = loss aversion (the brain weights losses ~2× more than equivalent gains). Use stakes when the concept's spine is a warning, a rescue arc, or a cost-of-inaction frame.
