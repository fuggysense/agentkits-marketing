# Three Elements

Every hook must hit ≥1 element. Hitting all three = nuclear hook.

## Relatability

**What it does:** Mass-audience mirror. The viewer thinks "that's me / that could be me."

**How to trigger it:** Articulate a universal experience the avatar lives — sensation, scenario, identity. Use "you/your" language. The experience doesn't have to match the viewer's exact situation — tagging the underlying emotion (loneliness, fear of wasted time, cost of the wrong circle) is the universal bridge.

**Guardrails:** Keep it specific enough to be real. Generic relatability ("hard times are tough") is not relatability — it's noise.

**See examples:** `references/examples/by-element/relatability.md`

---

## Sensationalism (Wow Factor)

**What it does:** Pattern interrupt, extreme contrast, status reversal. Makes the viewer stop mid-scroll.

**How to trigger it:** Huge numbers, extreme outcomes, impossible contrasts. "I sold my company for $10 million in three days." "$600k from parents." "400 slices of bread."

**CAUTION — claim_risk gate:** Sensationalism that distorts facts is the #1 source of forbidden_expression violations.
- Validate every sensational claim against `allowed_expressions` in concept-brief.json BEFORE using it.
- When claim_risk = high, prefer Relatability + Stakes instead. Treat Sensationalism as guarded.
- A weaker hook is better than a flagged one. Claim safety wins.

**See examples:** `references/examples/by-element/sensationalism.md`

---

## Stakes

**What it does:** Communicates high risk — what's on the line if the viewer doesn't act or watch.

**How to trigger it:** Identity threat, loss frame, urgency, failure consequence. "My mom's business will fail if I don't do something." "We lose this $600k and we're done before we turn 23."

**Why it works:** Stakes convert passive interest into active engagement. The viewer can't look away from risk.

**See examples:** `references/examples/by-element/stakes.md`

## Scoring

| Elements hit | Rating |
|---|---|
| 1 element + all 6-Q questions 1–4 pass | Strong |
| 2 elements | Very strong |
| All 3 elements | Elite / nuclear |

Pro move: when a hook hits all three, it usually explodes. See `references/examples/combined/nuclear-hooks.md`.

## Differentiation Rule (A/B pairs)

Hook A and Hook B must differ on the hook ANGLE per this checklist — not just word choice.

- Acceptable: A = stakes-led (identity threat); B = sensationalism-led (mechanism reveal)
- Unacceptable: A = "your hair is thinning" / B = "is your hair thinning" (same lever, word swap only)
